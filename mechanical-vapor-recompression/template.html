Duty and temperature. The evaporation rate is the water the plant must boil off; every flow, duty, power and area is proportional to it, while the COP and the specific energy do not move with it. The evaporator temperature sets the suction pressure: at a fixed lift, a hotter evaporator needs a smaller pressure ratio and less work per kilogram. Look at the feed, concentrate and duty rows of the derived-quantities table, and at the suction pressure when the temperature changes.

Temperature lift. The lift is the temperature rise the compressor must give the vapour. Of the lift, 2 K is boiling-point elevation and 1 K is losses; the rest drives heat transfer in the calandria. A larger lift costs power and saves area; a smaller lift saves power and costs area. Look at the specific-energy chart, the pressure ratio and COP cards, and the heat-transfer area row.

Compressor efficiency. The isentropic efficiency divides the ideal compression work to give the actual work. It changes the power, the COP and the specific energy, and nothing else: pressures, duty and area are set before it enters. Look at the isentropic and actual work rows of the derived-quantities table.

Prices and hours. The electricity price, steam price and operating hours convert the computed power and the alternative's steam flow into annual costs and a simple payback. They change no pressure, flow, duty or area on this panel. Look at the annual cost chart and the payback card.
