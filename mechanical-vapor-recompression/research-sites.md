# Mechanical Vapor Recompression (MVR) — Largest Documented Commercial Installations: Research Notes

## Method and ranking status

No definitive global ranking of MVR installations is published.

were read on an opened source page in the v4 revision run

## Verified sites (opened and read, 2026-11-22)

### 1. AmulFed Dairy, Gandhinagar, India — GEA MVR evaporation plant
- Source A: Refrigerated & Frozen Foods, "GEA to build Asia's
  largest milk production facility in India", 2017-07-19.
  URL: https://www.refrigeratedfrozenfood.com/articles/93243-gea-to-build-asias-largest-milk-production-facility-in-india
  Facts read: AmulFed Dairy (formerly Mother Dairy), Gandhinagar,
  a unit of GCMMF, awarded GEA a turnkey plant for 150 tons/day
  skim milk powder and 120 tons/day dairy whitener/baby food;
  production scheduled to begin 2018; described as the largest
  skim milk powder and dairy whitener plant in Asia; processes
  around 90,000 litres of milk per hour; features "a mechanical
  vapor re-compression (MVR) system from GEA within the
  evaporation plant, which continuously recycles energy by
  recompressing the vapor within the evaporator"; also an RO
  polisher on the milk condensate approaching zero liquid
  discharge.
- Source B: Packaging South Asia, same announcement, 2017-07-26.
  URL: https://packagingsouthasia.com/packaging-production/gea-to-build-asia-s-largest-milk-production-facility-in-india/
  Facts read: order value "a lower double-digit, million-Euro
  amount"; together with two existing GEA powder plants on the
  site, AmulFed is the largest plant in Asia at a single location;
  MVR "reduces energy costs and the CO2 footprint".

### 2. Madeta a.s., Czech Republic — GEA MVR whey evaporator
- Source: Process Engineering, "GEA evaporator for major Czech
  dairy", 28 Jan 2011.
  URL: https://processengineering.co.uk/article/2007110/gea-evaporator-for-m
  Facts read: Madeta, a major private Czech dairy, awarded GEA a
  complete turn-key evaporator to process sweet whey; evaporative
  capacity 12.5 tonnes/hour; MVR technology; nearly 500 kg/h less
  live steam than a traditional design; nearly EUR 80,000/year
  economy on running costs; two effects in one body ("combi"
  design) to minimise footprint; selection criteria were lowest
  environmental impact and lowest running costs.

### 3. Synlait Milk, New Zealand — GEA milk powder plant, MVR + TVR
- Source: Food & Beverage Industry News (foodmag.com.au),
  "Timing is everything for powder plant", published 2008-11-10.
  URL: https://www.foodmag.com.au/timing-is-everything-for-powder-plant/
  Facts read: GEA awarded contract end of May 2007 for a
  greenfield milk powder plant for Synlait Milk; first run on
  milk 2 August 2008; milk concentrated to more than 50% total
  solids in a multiple-effect falling-film evaporator; "Both
  mechanical vapour recompression (MVR) and thermal vapour
  recompression (TVR) are used to boost the efficiency of the
  evaporator"; evaporator water recovered and reused for
  cleaning. No evaporation capacity is stated in the article.

## Verified vendor-class facts (opened and read, 2026-11-22)

### GEA, "Heating options for thermal separation plants"
URL: https://www.gea.com/en/campaigns/thermal-separation-heating-options
Facts read: MVR recycles all of the vapour (TVR only part);
single-stage centrifugal fans are used in evaporation plants,
suited to large flow rates at vapour compression ratios of
1:1.2 to 1:2, rotational speeds typically 3,000 to 12,000 rpm;
specific utility consumption per ton of evaporation — MVR:
minor steam, about 30–50 kWh electricity, minor cooling water
(live steam single effect: 1 t steam, 60 m³ cooling water);
a small quantity of additional steam may be required at
start-up; condensate energy is frequently used to preheat feed.

### GEA, "Evaporation plants"
URL: https://www.gea.com/en/products/evaporators-crystallizers/evaporator-plants/
Facts read: experience of several thousand installed plants;
"Today, almost all new evaporation plants are equipped with
mechanical vapor recompression (MVR)."

### GEA, "Dairy Evaporators" and "Dairy Evaporator eZero"
URLs: https://www.gea.com/en/products/evaporators-crystallizers/dairy-evaporation-plants/dairy-evaporators/
and https://www.gea.com/en/products/evaporators-crystallizers/dairy-evaporation-plants/evaporator-ezero/
Facts read: MVR and TVR are the two technologies used most
commonly in the dairy industry; MVR is usually applied to
single-effect evaporators and is more energy efficient than TVR;
product vapour is recompressed and recycled to the heating side
of the same effect using electricity.
