Chat or Agentic AI: Which Should Chemical Engineers Use?

A frontier model (Muse Spark 1.3), tested on 24 October 2026 in chat mode, without running any program, gave a materially wrong number in 4 of 26 undergraduate chemical-engineering problems. People expect numerical results from a computer to be correct. Wrong numbers in 4 of 26 problems is extremely bad, and the replies did not say which numbers were wrong. This page recomputes those four by program execution (agent mode), places each chat answer beside the computed value, and shows why the errors arose. Every agent-mode value that differs from a chat answer was also recomputed independently by agentic Claude Opus 5.5, and all agree. The conclusion: do not use chat mode for numerical work; use agent mode. Every calculation on this page runs in Python (Pyodide); if the runtime cannot load, an error is shown and nothing is computed.

The screening in brief

Twenty-six problems spanning fluid mechanics, phase equilibrium, applied thermodynamics, reaction engineering, separations, heat transfer, material balances, process dynamics and control, design economics, and data analysis were each solved twice by the same model: once as a single chat message with an instruction to use no external program (chat mode), and once by execution of a Python program verified by an independent route (agent mode). A requested quantity was classed as materially in error when its relative error exceeded 2 percent, when an integer result was wrong, when a stated uncertainty quantity was in error by more than 50 percent, or when a finite answer was returned for an infeasible specification; the criterion was fixed before the test. Twenty-two problems were solved correctly in full in chat mode. The four failures are the subject of this page.

Example 1: standard errors that were never computed (kinetic fit, 41 points)

A batch reactor experiment for the reaction A → products records the concentration of A, CA in mol/L, at time t in min at 41 times from 0 to 200 min. The rate law is −dCA/dt = k CAn. Nonlinear least-squares fitting of all 41 points gives the parameters k and n, their standard errors, the root-mean-square error of the fit, and the predicted concentration at t = 240 min. The chat-mode reply reported acceptable point estimates (n = 1.80, k = 0.038) and standard errors of 0.03 for n and 0.002 for k, stating that the scatter of the residuals was propagated by judgement; no covariance computation was performed. A standard error is a function of the residual sum of squares and of the inverse of the normal-equation matrix accumulated over all 41 points; the executed fit below produces it as a by-product.

Example 2: a root-mean-square error estimated instead of accumulated (kinetic fit, 25 points)

The same experiment at pilot scale gives 25 points over 120 min, fitted by the same rate law. The chat-mode reply reported n = 1.8 and k = 0.039 (errors of 1.3 and 1.9 percent) and a root-mean-square error of 0.013 mol/L. The root-mean-square error is the square root of the sum of squared residuals over all 25 points, divided by 25. The reply stated a value without accumulating the squares; the executed accumulation gives 0.00934 mol/L, so the stated root-mean-square error was 39 percent too large. The difference is not a matter of definition: with the divisor N − 2 = 23 in place of 25, the accumulated value is 0.00974 mol/L, and the reply is still 33 percent above it.

Example 3: a saving smaller than the rounding of the totals (pipe diameter selection)

Water at 20 °C (density 998 kg/m3, dynamic viscosity 1.002×10−3 Pa·s) is pumped at 0.110 m3/s through a horizontal line of length 9000 m with absolute roughness 0.045 mm. Two inside diameters, D = 0.28 m and D = 0.30 m, are compared on total annualised cost (pressure drop by the Colebrook equation; pumping power at combined efficiency 0.74; electricity at 0.14 dollars per kWh over 8200 h per year; installed cost 2275 D1.55 dollars per metre annualised at a capital recovery factor of 0.12 per year). The chat-mode reply computed every component to within 0.1 percent and selected the correct diameter, then stated the annual saving as approximately 140 dollars/yr. The reply's own totals, rounded to 100 dollars/yr, differ by 100 dollars/yr, so its 140 cannot be traced to them; totals carried at that resolution cannot resolve the saving at all. The saving is 0.034 percent of either total; the executed difference, at full precision, is 161.08 dollars/yr.

Example 4: a remainder flow in a looped network (pipe network)

Water enters a looped network at node A at 0.300 m3/s; demands in m3/s are B 0.050, C 0.080, D 0.070, E 0.050, F 0.050; seven pipes join the nodes, and the head loss in each follows the Colebrook friction factor. The chat-mode reply solved the network by Hardy–Cross iteration: six of seven flows within 1.5 percent of the verified values and both requested heads (at nodes D and F) within 0.2 percent. The exception is pipe EF. Node E receives only the CE flow and discharges its demand and the EF flow, so the EF flow is the difference of the CE flow and 0.050 m3/s. The reply's node balance is exact (−0.005 = 0.045 − 0.050); its error is in the CE flow, 0.045 against 0.04437 m3/s, which is 1.4 percent and within the 2 percent criterion. The same absolute error of 0.00063 m3/s is 11 percent of the small EF flow, which is what the criterion flags.

@@TABLE_EX4@@

The last two rows substitute flows into the two loop equations (the head losses around each loop must sum to zero). The executed flows satisfy them to rounding error. The chat-mode flows leave 0.081 m in the loop through CE, EF and FD, but the executed flows rounded to the same three decimals already leave −0.048 m there: the small flows of that loop carry head losses that are sensitive to the third decimal. At the precision the reply quoted, the residual check therefore cannot separate its error from rounding, and confirming the answer needs an independent solution of the network. Either way, checking a chat answer takes an executed calculation, which agent mode provides.

Independent verification of the agent-mode values

Every agent-mode value on this page that differs from the chat-mode answer was recomputed by a second implementation, written separately from the problem statements alone by three agentic Claude Opus 5.5 subagents (model claude-opus-5-5, confirmed from the transcript of every reply), without access to this page's code, and using a different method: the kinetic fits by numerical integration of the rate law with sensitivity equations for the Jacobian, in place of the closed-form solution and curve_fit; the Colebrook friction factor by its exact Lambert W solution, checked by a bracketed root solve, in place of fixed-point iteration; and the network by Newton's method on the nodal heads, checked by a Hardy–Cross iteration, in place of loop-flow corrections. The page build runs these scripts and stops if any value differs by more than 10−4 (standard errors) or 10−6 (all other values) in relative terms. The scripts are listed in the Code panel.

Conclusion for practice

Chat mode gave a materially wrong number in 4 of 26 problems. People expect numerical results from a computer to be correct, and wrong numbers in 4 of 26 problems is extremely bad. Worse, the replies did not say which numbers were wrong, so an engineer, a reviewer or a grader cannot separate the bad answers from the good ones. The errors fall where chat mode is structurally weak: quantities defined by an accumulation over all data (standard errors, a root-mean-square error) and small differences of large quantities (a saving, a remainder flow). These are routine quantities in engineering work. Agent mode computes each number by executing a program and produces its checks (a covariance matrix, loop residuals, a sensitivity run) in the same run; on this page, every agent-mode value that differs from a chat answer was also recomputed independently by agentic Claude Opus 5.5 with different methods, and all agree. Do not use chat mode for numerical work. Its place is in work without a number to rely on: prose, posing a problem, instruction and assessment, and settings where code execution is not available or not permitted, as set out in the Theory panel (Conditions under which chat mode is preferred). The screening covered one model, one attempt per problem, and 26 well-posed undergraduate problems.
