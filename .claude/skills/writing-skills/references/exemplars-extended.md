## Model paper — opening and prediction

Moves: setting, established knowledge, gap as missing knowledge, model stated as the aim, prediction with its baseline values, validity stated with the model as subject (core rules 16, 26, and 32). The numbers in this exemplar are a second constructed set and illustrate form only.

A reactor model predicts the outlet conversion of a first-order reaction from the rate constant and the residence time. Two standard forms are in use: the plug-flow form, which holds the residence time of every fluid element equal, and the mixed form, which holds the composition uniform in the reactor. No measurement set distinguishes the two forms for residence times below 3 s, where the predicted conversions differ by less than the scatter of a single measurement at longer times. The model tested here keeps the plug-flow form and adds a distribution of residence times measured by a tracer step. At a rate constant k of 0.42 1/s and a mean residence time tau of 2.0 s, the plug-flow form predicts an outlet conversion of 0.57 (1 − exp(−0.84)) and the mixed form predicts 0.46 (0.84/1.84), against a measured conversion of 0.55 with a standard deviation of 0.02 over nine runs. The plug-flow form with the measured distribution predicts 0.56. The model is valid for a first-order reaction in a single liquid phase; a second phase adds a transfer term that the model does not include.

## Closing that combines evidence strands

Moves: two named strands combined in one sentence, principal claim at the abstract's strength, limitation named, implication at the supported scope (core rule 20). A closing that combines strands names the strands; a closing that restates one strand is the closing form above.

The measurements of Fig. 1 and the design calculation together set the packing height at high loading: the film resistance doubles between loadings of 0.40 mol/mol and 0.55 mol/mol at 333 K, and a design that holds the resistance at its low-loading value underpredicts the required height by 18 % (4.1 m predicted against 5.0 m required). Neither strand alone gives the design figure — the measurements give no height, and the calculation takes the resistance as an input. The measurements cover one solvent at one carbon dioxide partial pressure, and the combination is stated for that solvent and pressure. Within that scope, absorber design at loadings above 0.40 mol/mol uses the loading-dependent resistance.
