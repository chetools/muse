# Significance and breadth [load when drafting a STEM paper, report, or abstract for readers outside its subfield, and for a submission to a general-science journal]

Activation: the drafting of a title, an abstract, or an opening for a STEM document whose intended readers include scientists outside the document's subfield. This file governs how the advance is stated for that audience. It adds no claim: every statement under this file is drawn from the document's own results, held at the strength of core rules 20 and 25, and stated to explain, not to persuade (core rule 1). The drafting order that uses this file is in `drafting.md`.

G1. Advance sentence. The document holds one sentence that states the advance: the object studied, the relation or capability that is new, and the scope at which it was tested. The sentence is written before the abstract and the opening are drafted (`drafting.md`), and the abstract and the closing each restate the advance at no greater strength. A sentence that names only the method, only the topic, or only the importance of the topic is not an advance sentence.

G2. Gap as missing knowledge. The opening states the established knowledge first and the gap as the piece of knowledge that is missing, where that is the case, in the terms a reader outside the subfield already holds. A gap stated as a defect of named prior work is used only where the document reports a measured defect. The gap sentence names what is not known, not who failed to find it.

G3. First paragraph for the outside reader. The first paragraph of the opening uses no term that is defined only inside the subfield, and no abbreviation, without the plain-language explanation that core rule 2 requires at first use. A quantity in the first paragraph is stated with its unit and its comparison, so that its size is readable without the methods. Substitution is the test: a scientist in an adjacent field, reading the first paragraph alone, can state the object, the gap, and the advance.

G4. Title. The title names the object and the advance: a relation, a mechanism, a capability, or a measured property, with its scope where the scope is part of the advance. A title that names only the method, only the system, or only the field is incomplete under this rule. No word in the title states a strength the abstract does not state. Title form beyond this rule follows the target journal's guidelines, which take precedence over this package.

G5. Abstract breadth. The abstract follows the order of core rule 17. Its setting sentence and its implication sentence are written for the outside reader under G3; its method, result, and interpretation sentences are written for the field. The implication states what the result changes, and for what class of system, at the tested scope; an implication sentence that repeats the result in general words is a restatement under core rule 15 and is cut. No limitation stands in the abstract (core rule 17); the limitation stands in the closing, in the same terms as the advance sentence's scope.

G6. One advance, ranked. Where a document reports more than one new result, the advance sentence states the principal result, and the remaining results stand as secondary results in the abstract order of core rule 17, ranked by the ranking grounds of `structure.md`: what the principal claim requires, what other results depend on, and the weight of the evidence. Two results stated as equal advances, where one supports the other, are reordered so that the supporting result follows the result it supports.

G7. Comparison named with the advance. Where the advance is an improvement, a difference, or a first measurement, the sentence that states it names the baseline or the prior value and bundles the numbers under core rule 26. An advance stated without its comparison — a reduction with no baseline, a first value with no stated prior range — is incomplete, and the comparison is supplied from the document's own results or the advance is restated at the strength the comparison supports.

G8. Closing for the outside reader. The closing restates the advance at its tested scope with its limitations named (core rule 20), and its implication sentence is the abstract's implication restated with the scope the evidence supports, which may be narrower. The closing names the figure, table, or equation that is the evidence for the principal claim, where one does. A closing that a reader outside the subfield cannot follow to the advance sentence has failed G3 at the closing, and the first closing paragraph is revised under G3.

Editor-facing summaries. A two-to-three-sentence highlight whose first sentence states the advance is one journal's measured form (Nature research highlights, 12 of 12 in the Phase 4 corpus; the longer Science summary opens with background in 8 of 12). The form is stated here as a labelled form for that use, not as a general rule. In both journals' summaries the scope or a limit stands in the summary itself (18 of 24), and the paper's numbers in the main do not (5 of 24). A comparative claim in a summary still names its baseline under core rule 26; the rarity of numbers in these summaries is a fact about the form, not a licence to state a comparison without its value. The scope element is also the cross-channel result of the Phase 5 study: it stands in every coded PNAS significance statement (24 of 24), every coded review opening (10 of 10), and every edited item coded in that study.

## Completion check

The advance sentence exists as a single sentence and is restated, at no greater strength, in the abstract and in the closing; the gap is stated as missing knowledge or as a measured defect; the first paragraph and the abstract's setting and implication sentences pass the substitution test of G3; the title names the object and the advance and states no greater strength than the abstract; each comparative advance names its baseline with its numbers bundled; the closing names the limitation and, where one exists, the figure, table, or equation that is the evidence for the principal claim; the opening states the setting and the established knowledge before the gap (core rule 16), the abstract follows the order of core rule 17, and the closing claim stands at or below the abstract claim unless the closing combines evidence strands explicitly (core rule 20).
