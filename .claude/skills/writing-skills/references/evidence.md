# Evidence and limits

This file is not loaded for writing. It holds the evidence, corpus sizes, and limits for the core and the reference files. Rule numbers refer to the current package. The house rules come from the author's standing style guide, not from a coded corpus: core rules 5–7, 33, and 35, `figures.md`, `ste100.md`, and heads C6–C7 in `ai-check.md` (version 7); the field-term clause of core rule 2 and the content-order clause of core rule 6 (version 9); the compliance clause of core rule 6 (version 10); head C8 was added after version 7; the tie-break clause of core rule 11, core rules 36 and 37, the house word list in `ai-check.md` (moved there from core rule 32), the mechanical scan (`tools/lint_prose.py`), and `examples.md` (constructed examples, not corpus quotations) were added in version 12. `structure.md` (the reorganization procedure and the opening and closing size bands) was added in version 13; its size bands (about 300 and 1500 words, and 5–15 % of the body) are house choices, not corpus measurements, and its ordering procedure states core rules 6, 10, 20, and 28 as a sequence of steps. Version 14 adds three references. `drafting.md` is a procedure, not a corpus finding: its order states core rules 17 to 20 and the pilot frequencies behind them (interpretation inside the results in 37 of 37 papers; a median of 74.1 % of results sentences quantified) as a sequence of drafting steps, and it has not been checked in a paired test. In `significance.md`, G1, G2, G5, and G6 restate measured pilot forms (abstract move frequencies: setting 36 of 37, method 33, aim or approach 30, qualitative principal result 28, interpretation 28, quantitative principal result 25, implication or scope 24; gap in 28 of 37 openings, of which absence of knowledge 15; a limitation in 2 of 37 abstracts and a limitation or a scope condition in 29 of 37 closings; implication in 35 of 37 closings); G3, G4, G7, and G8 are house choices, stated as drafting and checking forms, with no corpus frequency behind them — G4 in particular rests on no coded title corpus, and the Phase 4 study specified in 2026-11 is the assigned test of G3, G4, and G8. `exemplars.md` is constructed passages, like `examples.md`, written to the move sequences of core rules 16, 17, and 20 and of `equations.md`; the passages are not corpus quotations and their numbers illustrate form only. Paired-test cases have the prefix P (PT1–PT5, PD1–PD2, PS6, PC1–PC6), so that no case code shares a name with a rule or a check head. Version 16 adds `references/lexicon.md` (rules L1–L6), from the lexical pass described under Corpora below, and the "in conclusion" phrase flag in `tools/lint_prose.py`. (The installed copy also carries version 15 additions not yet pushed; they are outside this commit.)

## Method

Each rule was coded from a measured corpus: documents were read against a fixed rubric, constructions were counted, and a rule states a construction that the coded documents use at the stated scale. Advice literature was not a source for the main rules. Two codebook anchors for the business corpus (Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*; Gene Zelazny, *Say It with Charts*) name forms the corpus documents use; the rules state the measured forms, not the books' advice.

Provenance tags ("[Test amendment]", "[Textbook amendment]", frequency brackets) stood in the loaded files of earlier versions and were moved out of the loaded package at the tiered revision, so the loaded files hold rules only. Case codes in the tags: PT1 is the tutorial case of the combined test, PT2 the abstract case, PT3 the recommendation memo case, PT4 the executive summary case, PT5 the slide storyline case; PD1 is the equation diagnostic case and PD2 the letter diagnostic case; PS6 is an equation case from an earlier paired test of a shortened draft, with the same verdict as PD1 on symbol placement; PC1–PC6 are the cases of the tiered-package confirmation retest.

## Corpora

STEM papers: 37 papers from *Nature* and *Science*, top citation rank, 2015–2024; an 8-paper mid-cited comparison set from the same journals. Flow measures (sentence openings, connectives, sentence lengths) were computed on 34 of the 37 papers.

Instructional writing: 22 items from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and DeepMind (tutorials, explainers, and documentation).

Distinctive patterns: 26 further *Nature* and *Science* papers and 16 further instructional and research-blog items (Anthropic, DeepMind, OpenAI, Google Research, Microsoft Research, Meta AI, Distill, and independent research blogs), coded for low-frequency constructions that state a result, a scope condition, and an interpretation with no loss in one move. Core rules 25–31 and 34 rest in part on this pass.

Equations: an oversample of 10 equation-bearing papers, 19 coded equations, 103 symbol groups. The equations reference rests on this pass and on the main corpus.

Business and consulting: 22 documents. Six long-form articles from *Harvard Business Review* and *MIT Sloan Management Review*; seven documents from McKinsey & Company, the McKinsey Global Institute, Boston Consulting Group, and Bain & Company; five chief executive officer letters (Berkshire Hathaway, JPMorgan Chase, Amazon); a 4-document comparison set from the mid-readership business press.

Textbooks: a contrast study of five publicly available engineering textbooks, with detailed coding of three canonical chemical-engineering textbooks (Seborg, Edgar, Mellichamp and Doyle, *Process Dynamics and Control*; Smith, Van Ness, Abbott and Swihart, *Introduction to Chemical Engineering Thermodynamics*, 8th edition; Seader, Henley and Roper, *Separation Process Principles*): stratified samples of 160 coded passages and 30 rewrite comparisons, plus full-text counts. Core rule 32 and `textbook.md` rest on this study; a textbook guideline stands only where the coded books agree.

Lexical pass (2026-11): deterministic counting over the corpora above, extended. The Nature and Science channel holds the 37 pilot papers as segmented prose (143,470 words) and 26 further papers as reference-stripped full text (287,646 words), 431,116 words pooled; the instructional channel holds the 22 items above (61,485 words); the textbook channel holds 10 full texts (2,790,680 words). The comparison baseline is model default output: 36 no-skill drafts on 16 fixed briefs (10,327 words), comprising the without-skill versions of the paired tests, the demonstration drafts, and a fresh round written for this pass; the with-skill comparison set is 21 drafts (6,219 words). The pass tested 4,021 items (2,686 unigrams, 1,335 phrases); 1,511 passed the frozen rate thresholds and 1,500 of those were classified topic, genre, or brief vocabulary at a topic-independence screen — a rate ratio between a narrow baseline and a broad corpus measures topic concentration for content words, and no rule rests on them. `lexicon.md` rests on the remainder and on the semantic-slot counts. Principal rates, per 1,000 words, baseline against pooled corpus: "in conclusion" 0.387 against 0.002 (absent from the instructional channel); "therefore" 1.259 against 0.209 (instructional 0.114); causal "so" 2.130 against 0.209; the additive-opener family 0.969 against 0.337, all 10 baseline occurrences in the demonstration subset; "however" 0.291 against 0.585. Reporting families, pooled corpus: show 1.835, find 1.737, observe 1.262, demonstrate 0.283. Interpretation families: suggest 0.719 and indicate 0.749, against a baseline of 0 and 0.097. A total strength order over the reporting and interpretation verbs was tested against sampled contexts and is not supported; the lexicon states the present/infer distinction the contexts support. Entries that failed the pass's entry test (reveal, support, demonstrate as an entry) and slots with no entry (comparison, cause, limit) are negative results of this pass. The counting is one deterministic pass by one tokeniser (the pilot word pattern); word counts reconcile exactly with the pilot's per-paper totals for all 37 pilot papers. The study record is the Phase 6 report in the pilot workspace.

## Paired tests

Specimen pairs were written at equal word counts, one version with a skill and one version without a skill, on identical briefs, and coded blind with labels randomised. Recognition strings in the rules (for example in core rules 10, 11, and 32) record forms that occurred in the no-skill versions at the stated counts while the with-skill versions were favoured on the same feature.

A later test compared the combined single file against the two predecessor skills used separately, on five decision cases (a STEM tutorial, a STEM abstract, a recommendation memo, an executive summary, a slide storyline) and two diagnostic cases (an equation passage, a chief executive officer letter). The combined version was judged more effective in three of the five decision cases; its losses produced the amendments recorded in the provenance tags. The diagnostic loss on the letter case is the measured cost of holding the letter patterns in the appendix: one case, decided on features that the main business rules partly cover.

A paired test and a confirmation retest then compared the tiered package (core plus condition-loaded references) against the single-file form; the tiered package was installed after that test. A trigger test in the same study found that inference markers in closing frame sentences accounted for all frame-side errors in eight test documents; core rule 14 states the resulting rule.

A long-form pair (a route-comparison report at 1,200 words per version, written from one frozen fact base, one version under the skill and one version with no skill) is published with the paired samples page linked from `README.md`. The pair is a demonstration specimen, not a blind test: the versions were not coded blind, and no verdict beyond the stated feature counts is recorded for it.

## Limits

- The pilots measure text: coded features and coder verdicts on those features. No pilot measures reader outcomes, comprehension, or decision quality.
- Each condition in the paired tests ran once per case. A three-to-two split across five cases does not exclude ordinary variation.
- The combined-skill test used one coder and reports no second-coder reliability statistic. The business pilot reports no second-coder reliability statistic.
- Frequencies are frequencies over the coded sets named here. A construction frequent in these corpora is not thereby frequent in a field corpus, and a rule states a form to use, not a rate to match.
- Corpus documents are published works under their publishers' copyright and are not included in this package. Rules, counts at corpus level, and the constructed specimens on the paired samples page are the package's content.
- Version 7 (compressed core, merged checklist) was checked against version 6 on three briefs (a Couette-flow tutorial, a methanol synthesis-loop report section, and a plant-scale recommendation memo): one Opus draft per version and brief, scored blind against the version 6 rules by one Opus checker per brief. Major content findings were 0 for version 7 and 2 for version 6; version 7 ranked first on 2 of the 3 briefs, and minor findings were 34 against 31. One draft per cell does not exclude ordinary variation.
- The version 9 to version 13 additions have not been checked in a paired test.
- The lexical pass's baseline is one model's default output on 16 briefs, written in one programme of sessions; a different model, or the same model on different briefs, may show a different default profile. Its corpus channels mix two text definitions (segmented prose for the pilot papers, full text including Methods for the 26 further papers), which is recorded in the pass's specification amendments; entry ranks are stated on both rankings. The lexicon has not been checked in a paired test, and its confirmation re-run over the cross-channel study's texts is specified in the Phase 6 report and has not been run.
- These are working rules. A rule is revised when a further coded corpus or a paired test counts against it.
