The aim of this example is to calculate the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film using Fick's first law. The diffusive flux J (mol m-2 s-1) depends on the diffusivity D (m2 s-1) and on the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C is the concentration (mol m-3) and x is the position (m). Fick's first law is written as J = -D dC/dx, and the negative sign is important because it shows that diffusion takes place from regions of high concentration towards regions of low concentration.

For oxygen in water, the diffusivity in this example is D = 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. The concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film that is 0.4 mm thick, which means that the concentration gradient is dC/dx = -500 mol m-4.

Putting these numbers into the equation gives the flux, and the result of the calculation is J = 8.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The whole calculation rests on just a small set of given quantities: a diffusivity of 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1 for oxygen in water, a concentration fall of 0.2 mol m-3, a film thickness of 0.4 mm, and the gradient of -500 mol m-4 that follows from them. It is worth noting that the law in this form applies to a stagnant medium. If the medium were moving, convection would also play a role, and convection is not included in this equation, so the result should be understood within that limitation.
