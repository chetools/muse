The goal of this worked example is to calculate the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant film of water, using Fick's first law. The diffusive flux J, which has units of mol m-2 s-1, depends on the diffusivity D, in m2 s-1, and on the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C is the concentration in mol m-3 and x is the position in m. Fick's first law is written as J = -D dC/dx. The negative sign in the equation is there because diffusion takes place from a region of high concentration towards a region of low concentration.

For oxygen in water, the diffusivity is 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. Across the water film, which is 0.8 mm thick, the concentration falls by 0.25 mol m-3, so the concentration gradient is dC/dx = -312 mol m-4. Substituting these values into Fick's first law gives J = 5.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1, which is the diffusive flux of oxygen through the film.

The calculation uses the diffusivity for oxygen in water given above, together with the concentration fall of 0.25 mol m-3 across the 0.8 mm film given above, and no other data.

It should be remembered that Fick's first law, as it is stated here, applies to a stagnant medium. If the medium were moving, convection would also contribute to the transport of oxygen, and this effect is not included in the equation used in this example.
