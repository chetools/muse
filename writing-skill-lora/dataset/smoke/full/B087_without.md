The goal of this worked example is to compute the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film. The diffusive flux J, expressed in mol m-2 s-1, is computed from the diffusivity D, in m2 s-1, and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C is the concentration in mol m-3 and x is the position in m. The governing relation is Fick's first law, J = -D dC/dx. The negative sign in this law indicates that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration.

For oxygen in water, the diffusivity D is 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. The concentration falls by 0.4 mol m-3 across a film that is 0.8 mm thick, so the concentration gradient dC/dx is -500 mol m-4. Substituting these values into Fick's first law gives J = -2.6 x 10-9 x (-500), which works out to a diffusive flux of 1.30 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1.

It is important to keep in mind that the law as stated covers a stagnant medium. A moving medium adds convection, which the equation does not include, so the computed flux applies to the stagnant film only. The result of 1.30 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1 therefore rests on the diffusivity of 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1 and on the gradient of -500 mol m-4 that comes from a fall of 0.4 mol m-3 across the 0.8 mm film. If either the diffusivity or the concentration fall were different, the flux obtained from Fick's first law would change in proportion, because the law multiplies the diffusivity by the gradient with no other term included for a stagnant water film of this thickness.
The steps can be summarised as follows. First, the concentration fall of 0.4 mol m-3 over a thickness of 0.8 mm is converted into the gradient of -500 mol m-4. Then that gradient is combined with the diffusivity of 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1 in Fick's first law, and the two negative signs, one in the law itself and one in the gradient, together give the positive flux value of 1.30 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1.
