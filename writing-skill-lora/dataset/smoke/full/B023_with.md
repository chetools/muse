A washcoated monolith catalyst gave more than twice the space-time yield of an extruded pellet catalyst at one fourteenth of the pressure drop, in the reaction of carbon dioxide and hydrogen over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to give formaldehyde. Space-time yield, the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1), was measured for both catalyst forms at 280 °C and 40 bar, at a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3 and one feed composition. The washcoated monolith gave a space-time yield of 1.09 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 0.1 bar, with an activity loss of 8% after 100 h. The extruded pellet gave a space-time yield of 0.52 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 1.4 bar, with an activity loss of 19% after 100 h. Yield and pressure drop stand together here for the same catalyst charge, a pairing seldom reported. The monolith leads on each of the three measures at the tested conditions: yield (1.09 against 0.52 kg L-1 h-1), pressure drop (0.1 against 1.4 bar), and activity retained after 100 h (a loss of 8% against 19%). The comparison is limited to the conditions tested. One feed composition was used, and no sulfur-bearing feed was tested; performance with sulfur in the feed, and performance at other compositions, pressures, or temperatures, was not measured. Activity loss is stated at the same point for both forms, after 100 h, and pressure drop is stated for the same catalyst charge as the yield it accompanies, at 280 °C and 40 bar.
