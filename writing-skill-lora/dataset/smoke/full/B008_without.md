This executive summary considers a heat-recovery retrofit for a food plant, where refrigeration currently uses a substantial amount of electricity, amounting to 1871 MWh per year. According to a projection provided by the equipment vendor, the proposed retrofit could save 11% of that electricity use. This corresponds to a derived saving of 206 MWh per year, based on the vendor figure. The capital cost of the retrofit is $469k, which is a significant investment for the plant. At an electricity price of $0.1 per kWh, the derived annual saving works out to $21k, and the derived simple payback period is 22.3 years, which is quite long. It is important to emphasise that the saving figure is a vendor projection and has not been measured at this plant, so there is some uncertainty around whether the projected savings will actually be achieved in practice. In addition, the refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, which is another limitation of the current analysis. In light of these considerations, the recommendation is to carry out a metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is made. This trial approach will allow the actual savings to be measured before a major commitment is undertaken. The plant engineer owns the trial and will be responsible for reporting its results. The three refrigeration lines are therefore not all committed at once: one line carries the trial, and the remaining lines continue in their current form while the trial is carried out and its savings are measured.
