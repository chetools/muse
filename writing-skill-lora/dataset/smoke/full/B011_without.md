The aim of this worked example is to calculate the half-life of a first-order reaction, and also to work out what fraction of the reactant remains after 60 min. For a first-order reaction, the half-life is related to the rate constant through a simple equation. The half-life, written as t1/2 and measured in min, is given by t1/2 = ln(2) / k, where k is the rate constant, measured in min-1. The fraction of reactant remaining after a time t, also measured in min, is given by the expression exp(-k t). In this example, the rate constant was measured as k = 0.046 min-1 at a temperature of 323 K. This value was obtained from concentration measurements that were taken every 5 min over a period of 90 min. Substituting the value of k into the half-life equation gives t1/2 = 15.1 min. Substituting k and t = 60 min into the fraction expression gives a fraction remaining of 0.06, which is a dimensionless quantity. It should be noted that these relations hold for a first-order reaction only. A reaction of another order would follow a different decay law, so the same equations should not be applied to reactions that are not first-order. Both of the calculated values, the half-life of 15.1 min and the fraction remaining of 0.06 after 60 min, rest on the single measured rate constant of 0.046 min-1 at 323 K and on the two relations given above for a first-order reaction.
