Between 300 K and 340 K, the rate constant k (s-1) of a first-order liquid-phase reaction rose from 0.019 s-1 to 0.541 s-1. The value at 300 K (k = 0.019 s-1) is a measured value. The value at 340 K (k = 0.541 s-1) is a derived value, computed from the Arrhenius relation with an activation energy of 71 kJ mol-1, and the two values do not carry the same standing: one rests on measurement, the other on the Arrhenius relation and its stated activation energy.

Each temperature was measured in triplicate, and the relative standard deviation of k was 4%. Replication in triplicate fixes the precision statement that accompanies the measured value: the spread of the three measurements at a temperature, expressed relative to their mean, is the 4% figure, and no other precision figure is reported for either value.

Above 340 K, no rate constant is reported. The solvent evaporated during sampling above 340 K, so sampling at higher temperatures did not yield a rate constant, and the reported pair of values ends at 340 K for that reason. The increase between the two stated temperatures (0.019 s-1 at 300 K, 0.541 s-1 at 340 K as a derived value) is the full extent of the temperature dependence reported here; behaviour of the rate constant above 340 K was not detected under the sampling conditions used, because the solvent loss during sampling prevented measurement in that range.
