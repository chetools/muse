Calculate the Reynolds number for water in a pipe of diameter 0.025 m at a mean velocity of 2.3 m s-1, and state the flow regime. The Reynolds number (Re) is 57400 (dimensionless), and the flow is turbulent, the value lying well above the turbulent threshold of 4000 stated below.

The Reynolds number Re (dimensionless) is computed from density rho (kg m-3), mean velocity v (m s-1), pipe diameter D (m) and dynamic viscosity mu (Pa s) by Re = rho v D / mu. Each input is a property of the water or a dimension of the pipe: density and dynamic viscosity are properties of water at the stated temperature (293 K), and diameter and mean velocity describe the pipe and the flow in it.

Water at 293 K has a density (rho) of 998 kg m-3 and a dynamic viscosity (mu) of 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. Substitution of the pipe diameter (D = 0.025 m) and the mean velocity (v = 2.3 m s-1) with those two properties gives Re = 998 x 2.3 x 0.025 / 0.001. The numerator collects the density, velocity and diameter terms (998 x 2.3 x 0.025), and division by the dynamic viscosity in the same units as stated (0.001 Pa s) gives the worked result: Re = 57400.

Flow is laminar below Re = 2300 and turbulent above Re = 4000. Between the two values the regime is transitional and is not assigned by the number alone; a computed value inside that band carries no regime statement from the Reynolds number itself. The computed value here (57400) is above 4000 by a wide margin, and comparison of the worked result with the two thresholds places the flow in the turbulent regime. The check on the result is the threshold comparison itself: 57400 exceeds the upper threshold (4000), and no part of the value falls in the transitional band between 2300 and 4000, so the regime statement (turbulent) follows from the stated thresholds at the stated diameter (0.025 m) and mean velocity (2.3 m s-1) for water at 293 K.
