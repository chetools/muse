This summary looks at a possible heat-recovery retrofit at a food plant, where refrigeration uses 1893 MWh of electricity per year. The equipment vendor projects that the retrofit would save 17% of that use, which works out to a saving of 322 MWh per year at the vendor figure. The capital cost of the retrofit is $312k. With electricity priced at $0.13 per kWh, the annual saving would be worth $42k, and the simple payback on the investment would be 7.4 years. It is important to note, however, that the saving figure is a vendor projection and has not been measured at this plant, so the 322 MWh per year and everything calculated from it rest on the vendor's estimate rather than on local data. There is also a gap in the analysis, because refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, and the peak month may behave differently from the year as a whole. Given these uncertainties, the recommendation is to carry out a metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is taken. A trial of this kind would replace the projected saving with a measured one on a single line. The plant engineer owns the trial and would be responsible for running it and reporting what it finds.
