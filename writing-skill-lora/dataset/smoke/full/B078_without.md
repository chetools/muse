Carbon dioxide and hydrogen can react over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to produce dimethyl ether, and this study compares two different forms of the catalyst under the same test conditions. The performance of each catalyst is described using space-time yield, which is the mass of product produced per unit catalyst volume per hour, expressed in kg L-1 h-1. The tests were carried out at 230 °C and 60 bar, with a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used throughout the work.

The washcoated monolith catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.99 kg L-1 h-1, which is considerably higher than the value obtained with the extruded pellet catalyst. It also produced a much lower pressure drop of 0.3 bar, and its activity loss after 100 h was 7%. By comparison, the extruded pellet catalyst gave a space-time yield of only 0.41 kg L-1 h-1, together with a pressure drop of 0.98 bar, and it lost 14% of its activity after 100 h, which is twice the loss seen for the monolith.

Overall, the monolith therefore performed better on all three measures reported here, namely yield, pressure drop and stability over the 100 h test period. It should be noted, however, that no sulfur-bearing feed was tested, so nothing can be said about how either catalyst would behave with such a feed. In addition, yield and pressure drop are seldom reported together for the same catalyst charge, which makes comparisons of this kind relatively uncommon in practice and adds value to reporting both quantities for both catalyst forms under identical conditions in a single set of experiments at one feed composition.
