In this study, carbon dioxide and hydrogen were reacted over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to produce dimethyl ether, and two different catalyst forms were compared under the same test conditions. The performance of the catalysts is described using the space-time yield, which is defined as the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour, expressed in kg L-1 h-1. All of the tests were carried out at 250 °C and 50 bar, with a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used throughout the work. The first catalyst was a washcoated monolith, which achieved a space-time yield of 0.94 kg L-1 h-1, with a pressure drop of 0.24 bar and an activity loss of 8% after 100 h. The second catalyst was an extruded pellet, which gave a lower space-time yield of 0.48 kg L-1 h-1, with a higher pressure drop of 0.95 bar and an activity loss of 13% after 100 h. Based on these results, the washcoated monolith performed better than the extruded pellet on all three of the measures reported here, delivering roughly twice the space-time yield at about a quarter of the pressure drop, together with a smaller loss of activity over the 100 h test period. It is important to note, however, that no sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this study, so the behaviour of these catalysts in the presence of sulfur remains unknown. It is also interesting to note that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge, which makes the combined reporting of both quantities in this work particularly useful for comparing the two catalyst forms. The comparison therefore rests on a single set of test conditions, namely 250 °C, 50 bar, a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3 and the one feed composition used for both catalyst forms, with each catalyst assessed over the same 100 h period for its activity loss.
