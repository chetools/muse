Oxygen diffuses through a stagnant water film 0.4 mm thick. The diffusive flux J is 8.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The diffusive flux J (mol m-2 s-1) is computed from the diffusivity D (m2 s-1) and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C denotes the concentration (mol m-3) and x denotes the position (m). Fick's first law gives the flux as J = -D dC/dx. The negative sign in Fick's first law states that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration.

The diffusivity of oxygen in water, D, is 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. Concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across the film, and the film thickness is 0.4 mm; thus the concentration gradient dC/dx is -500 mol m-4, the stated fall divided over the stated thickness with the direction of the fall carried in the sign. Substitution of the diffusivity and the gradient into Fick's first law gives J = -(1.6 x 10-9) x (-500). Thus J = 8.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1, positive in the direction from high concentration to low concentration.

Fick's first law as stated holds for a stagnant medium. A moving medium adds convection, and convection is not included in the equation. The flux of 8.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1 holds for the stated film, the stated fall of 0.2 mol m-3 across 0.4 mm, and the stated diffusivity of 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1.
