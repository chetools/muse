In this study, carbon dioxide and hydrogen were reacted over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to produce methanol, and two different catalyst forms were compared under identical test conditions. The work focused on the space-time yield, which is defined as the mass of product obtained per unit catalyst volume per hour, expressed in kg L-1 h-1. The experiments were carried out at 280 °C and 40 bar, with a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used throughout the testing programme.

The structured catalyst delivered a space-time yield of 0.78 kg L-1 h-1, which was noticeably higher than the value achieved by the pellet catalyst. In addition to its higher yield, the structured catalyst also showed a pressure drop of 0.29 bar, and its activity loss after 100 h of operation was just 5%. By comparison, the pellet catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.53 kg L-1 h-1, alongside a considerably higher pressure drop of 0.93 bar, and it lost 14% of its activity after 100 h.

Overall, the results highlight the advantages of the structured catalyst across all three measures reported here: yield, pressure drop, and stability over 100 h. It is also worth noting that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge, which makes a direct comparison of this kind particularly valuable. At the same time, an important limitation should be kept in mind: no sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this work. Therefore, the findings apply to the conditions actually studied, namely 280 °C, 40 bar, a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, and a single feed composition. Within those conditions, the structured catalyst, with a space-time yield of 0.78 kg L-1 h-1, a pressure drop of 0.29 bar, and an activity loss of 5% after 100 h, outperformed the pellet catalyst, which recorded 0.53 kg L-1 h-1, 0.93 bar, and 14% respectively, in this comparison of methanol production from carbon dioxide and hydrogen over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst.
