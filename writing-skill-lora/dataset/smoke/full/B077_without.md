This example considers the half-life of a first-order reaction and the fraction of material remaining after 60 min. For a first-order reaction, the rate constant k, in min-1, determines the half-life t1/2, in min, through the relationship t1/2 = ln(2) / k. The fraction remaining after a time t, in min, is given by exp(-k t), which is dimensionless.

In this case, the measured rate constant is k = 0.012 min-1 at 323 K. The value was obtained from concentration measurements taken every 5 min over a period of 90 min. Substituting k = 0.012 min-1 into the half-life relationship gives a half-life of t1/2 = 57.8 min. Using the fraction expression with t = 60 min then gives a fraction remaining of 0.49, which is dimensionless.

It is important to note that these relationships hold for a first-order reaction only, and that a reaction of another order follows a different decay law.
