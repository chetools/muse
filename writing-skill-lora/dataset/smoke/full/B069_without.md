An amine absorption unit was tested at pilot scale using gas from a cement kiln, and the performance of the unit was recorded over a sustained operating period. Over 45 days of operation, during which the unit treated gas at a rate corresponding to 10 t of carbon dioxide per day, the unit successfully captured 84% of the carbon dioxide present in the treated gas. This capture performance was achieved alongside a steam demand of 3.8 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, which represents the energy requirement of the process under the conditions tested.

In addition to the capture rate and the steam demand, the solvent loss was also measured, and it came to 1.5 kg per tonne of carbon dioxide captured. Together, these three figures give a useful overall picture of how the pilot unit performed during the trial: a capture rate of 84%, a steam demand of 3.8 gigajoules per tonne captured, and a solvent loss of 1.5 kg per tonne captured, all at a throughput of 10 t of carbon dioxide per day sustained over 45 days on gas from a cement kiln.

However, it is important to recognise the limits of what this trial can tell us. Beyond 45 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent is untested, so no conclusions can be drawn about longer-term operation on that point. In summary, the pilot-scale amine absorption unit, tested on cement kiln gas over 45 days at 10 t of carbon dioxide per day, captured 84% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas, with a steam demand of 3.8 gigajoules per tonne captured and a solvent loss of 1.5 kg per tonne captured, while the effect of kiln dust on the solvent beyond 45 days remains untested.
