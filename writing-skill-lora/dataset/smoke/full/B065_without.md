The purpose of this example is to calculate the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film, using Fick's first law. Fick's first law is a fundamental relationship in mass transfer, and it states that the diffusive flux J, in mol m-2 s-1, depends on the diffusivity D, in m2 s-1, and on the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C is the concentration in mol m-3 and x is the position in m. The law is written as J = -D dC/dx. The negative sign in the equation is there because diffusion proceeds from a region of high concentration towards a region of low concentration, which is the opposite direction to the direction in which the concentration increases.

For oxygen in water, the diffusivity is D = 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. In this example, the concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film that is 0.8 mm thick, so the concentration gradient comes out as dC/dx = -250 mol m-4. Substituting the numbers into the equation gives J = -(1.6 x 10-9) x (-250), and the result of the calculation is J = 4.00x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1.

Finally, it should be remembered that the law in the form stated here covers a stagnant medium. If the medium were moving instead, convection would have to be considered as well, and that contribution is not included in the equation as it stands, so the calculated flux relates only to the stagnant situation described in this example. The two values that enter the calculation are the diffusivity of oxygen in water, 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1, and the concentration gradient across the film, -250 mol m-4, and the result of 4.00x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1 follows directly from those two stated values.
