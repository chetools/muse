On a feed of 85 wt% n-butanol in water at 60 °C, a zeolite membrane gave a separation factor of 560 with flux unchanged over 500 h within measurement error, and a polymer membrane gave a separation factor of 180 with flux falling 14% over 500 h. Pervaporation is a membrane separation in which a liquid feed contacts one side of a membrane and vacuum on the other side draws the permeating component through as vapour. Flux (the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour, in kg m-2 h-1) was 1.7 kg m-2 h-1 for the polymer membrane and 1.1 kg m-2 h-1 for the zeolite membrane. Separation factor (the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed, dimensionless) was 180 for the polymer membrane against 560 for the zeolite membrane. One feed composition was tested, and no test ran beyond 500 h. Stability over the 500 h test period followed the separation factor: polymer flux fell 14%, and zeolite flux was unchanged within measurement error. Flux, selectivity and stability are seldom reported together for the same membrane, and all three are reported here for both membranes at the one feed composition tested. At this feed composition, the polymer membrane carries the higher flux (1.7 against 1.1 kg m-2 h-1), and the zeolite membrane carries the higher separation factor (560 against 180) with flux unchanged over 500 h within measurement error.
