Recommendation: run a metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision. The plant engineer owns the trial. The plant uses 2491 MWh of electricity per year in refrigeration, a retrofit is projected to save part of that use, and the question is whether to commit capital on a projection or to measure the saving first on one line.

A heat-recovery retrofit is projected by the equipment vendor to save 12% of that use, a derived saving of 299 MWh per year at the vendor figure. Capital cost is $430k. At an electricity price of $0.11 per kWh, the derived annual saving is $33k and the derived simple payback is 13 years. Each money figure in that chain is derived from the vendor projection: the 299 MWh from the 12% figure, and the $33k and the 13-year payback from the 299 MWh at $0.11 per kWh.

The saving figure is a vendor projection and was not measured at this plant; refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled. The trial is metered, on one of the three lines, and stands before any capital decision. Capital is decided on a measured saving at this plant, at the stated electricity price, not on the vendor projection of 12%.
The trial design follows from the gaps in the record. Refrigeration use stands at 2491 MWh per year across three lines. No saving has been measured at this plant on any line, and refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled. A metered trial on one line gives a measured saving for that line, at the electricity price of $0.11 per kWh, before the capital cost of $430k for the full retrofit is considered against a derived payback of 13 years.
