The goal of this worked example is to compute the Reynolds number for water flowing in a pipe, and then to state the flow regime. The Reynolds number, Re, is a dimensionless quantity that is computed from the density rho, the mean velocity v, the pipe diameter D and the dynamic viscosity mu, using the relation Re = rho v D / mu. Density is measured in kg m-3, mean velocity in m s-1, pipe diameter in m, and dynamic viscosity in Pa s.

For water at 293 K, the density rho is 998 kg m-3 and the dynamic viscosity mu is 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe diameter D is 0.025 m, and the mean velocity v is 1.8 m s-1. Substituting these values into the relation gives Re = 998 x 1.8 x 0.025 / 0.001, which works out to a Reynolds number of 44900.

Flow is laminar below Re = 2300 and turbulent above Re = 4000. Between these two values, the regime is transitional and is not assigned by the number alone. Since the computed value of 44900 is well above 4000, the flow in this pipe is turbulent. The result therefore rests on the four input values at 293 K: a density of 998 kg m-3, a viscosity of 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s, a diameter of 0.025 m and a mean velocity of 1.8 m s-1, combined in the stated relation to give 44900, far above the turbulent threshold of 4000.
To recap the calculation, the four values are combined in a single step: the density of 998 kg m-3 is multiplied by the velocity of 1.8 m s-1 and by the diameter of 0.025 m, and the result is divided by the viscosity of 0.001 Pa s, giving the Reynolds number of 44900 stated above.
