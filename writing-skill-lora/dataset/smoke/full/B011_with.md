For a first-order reaction with rate constant k = 0.046 min-1 at 323 K, the half-life is 15.1 min and the fraction remaining after 60 min is 0.06. Rate constant k (min-1) denotes the measured first-order rate constant, obtained from concentration measurements every 5 min over 90 min at 323 K, and its value in this example is 0.046 min-1. Half-life t1/2 (min) is defined by t1/2 = ln(2) / k for a first-order reaction. Substitution of k = 0.046 min-1 into the half-life relation thus gives t1/2 = 15.1 min. Fraction remaining after time t (min) is exp(-k t) for a first-order reaction. Substitution of k = 0.046 min-1 and t = 60 min thus gives exp(-0.046 x 60), a fraction remaining of 0.06 (dimensionless). A check on the two results uses the half-life itself: 60 min spans about four half-lives of 15.1 min, and four halvings leave about one sixteenth, or 0.06, of the starting amount, in agreement with the fraction computed from exp(-k t). The half-life relation and the fraction relation hold for a first-order reaction only. A reaction of another order follows a different decay law, and neither relation applies to such a reaction.
