In this work, the rate constant of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was measured at two different temperatures in order to examine how the reaction rate changes with temperature. The rate constant, denoted by k and expressed in s-1, was determined at 300 K and at 340 K. At the lower temperature of 300 K, the value of k was found to be 0.057 s-1. At the higher temperature of 340 K, the value of k increased substantially to 2.858 s-1. It should be noted that the value at 340 K was not measured directly in the same way, but was instead computed from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 83 kJ mol-1, and it is therefore a derived value. Each temperature was measured in triplicate, which helps to ensure the reliability of the results, and the relative standard deviation of k was 4%, indicating good reproducibility. It is also important to mention that no rate constant is reported above 340 K. This is because, above 340 K, the solvent evaporated during sampling, which made it impossible to obtain reliable measurements at higher temperatures. Taken together, these results clearly show that the rate constant increases significantly as the temperature rises from 300 K to 340 K, and they provide a useful basis for understanding the temperature dependence of this first-order reaction over the range that was studied.
