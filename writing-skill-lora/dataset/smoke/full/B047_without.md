An amine absorption unit was tested at pilot scale on gas coming from a cement kiln, and the results of the test are reported here. The unit was operated over a period of 90 days, during which it treated gas at a rate corresponding to 13 t of carbon dioxide per day, and over this period it captured 85% of the carbon dioxide contained in the treated gas.

The steam demand of the process was 3.3 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, which is an important operating cost for an amine absorption system of this kind. The solvent loss was 2.4 kg per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, so solvent also had to be made up as the run continued.

These figures were obtained over the full 90 days of operation at the stated capture rate of 13 t of carbon dioxide per day, and the capture figure of 85% applies to that period and to the kiln gas that was treated. The steam figure and the solvent loss are both expressed per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, which means they can be compared directly with other systems reported on the same basis.

However, there is an important limitation to bear in mind. Beyond 90 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent has not been tested, so it is not known how the solvent would behave over longer periods of operation on this gas. In summary, the pilot unit captured 85% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas over 90 days at 13 t per day, with a steam demand of 3.3 gigajoules per tonne captured and a solvent loss of 2.4 kg per tonne captured, and any operation beyond 90 days would go beyond what has actually been tested, particularly with regard to the effect of kiln dust on the solvent.
