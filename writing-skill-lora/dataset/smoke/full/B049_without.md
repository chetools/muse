Adsorption of a dye from water onto activated carbon was studied in a series of batch tests carried out at 298 K, and the results are described in this passage. The data were fitted with the Langmuir model, and the fitted Langmuir capacity was found to be 202 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent at pH 7, which gives a measure of how much dye the carbon can take up under these conditions.

In the batch tests, an adsorbent dose of 0.5 g L-1 was used, and after 120 min the removal reached 94% of the initial dye concentration, which was 50 mg L-1. In other words, under the stated conditions of dose, time and initial concentration, almost all of the dye was removed from the water within the two-hour test period.

It is interesting to note the relationship between the two figures reported here. The capacity of 202 mg per gram of adsorbent comes from the Langmuir fit at pH 7, while the removal of 94% comes from a particular batch test at a dose of 0.5 g L-1, an initial concentration of 50 mg L-1 and a contact time of 120 min, all at 298 K.

However, an important point should be kept in mind. The Langmuir fit is reported for pH 7 only, and the capacity at other pH values was not measured in this work. This means that the capacity figure of 202 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent should not be assumed to apply at other pH values. Overall, the study shows that, at pH 7 and 298 K, activated carbon gave a fitted Langmuir capacity of 202 mg g-1 in the batch tests, and that a dose of 0.5 g L-1 removed 94% of an initial dye concentration of 50 mg L-1 after 120 min, with the capacity at other pH values remaining unmeasured.
