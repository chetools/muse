In this example, we will work through the calculation of the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film using Fick's first law. Fick's first law is an important relationship that describes how a substance diffuses from a region of high concentration to a region of low concentration. The law can be written as J = -D dC/dx, where J is the diffusive flux in mol m-2 s-1, D is the diffusivity in m2 s-1, and dC/dx is the concentration gradient, with C representing the concentration in mol m-3 and x representing position in m. The negative sign in the equation is significant because it indicates that diffusion takes place in the direction from high concentration to low concentration.

For oxygen in water, the diffusivity D is given as 2.1 x 10-9 m2 s-1. In this particular case, the concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film that is 0.5 mm thick, so the concentration gradient works out to dC/dx = -400 mol m-4. Substituting these values into Fick's first law gives J = -(2.1 x 10-9) x (-400), which gives a final result of J = 8.40x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1.

It is worth noting that the law as stated here covers a stagnant medium only. If the medium were moving, convection would also be involved, and the equation as given does not include that effect, so the result should be understood within that limitation.
