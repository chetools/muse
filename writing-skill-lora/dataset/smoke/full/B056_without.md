Carbon dioxide and hydrogen can be reacted together over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst in order to produce methanol, and in this study two different forms of the catalyst were compared with each other. The performance of the catalysts was assessed using the space-time yield, which is defined as the mass of product obtained per unit catalyst volume per hour, expressed in kg L-1 h-1. The tests were carried out at a temperature of 230 °C and a pressure of 60 bar, with a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used throughout the work.

The structured catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.71 kg L-1 h-1, which was considerably higher than the value of 0.37 kg L-1 h-1 that was obtained with the pellet catalyst. The structured catalyst also performed better in terms of pressure drop, giving a value of only 0.29 bar compared with 1.52 bar for the pellets, and it lost less of its activity over time, losing 4% of its activity after 100 h compared with a loss of 16% for the pellet catalyst over the same period.

It should be noted, however, that no sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this study, so nothing can be said about how either catalyst would behave in the presence of sulfur. It is also interesting to note that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported together for the same catalyst charge in the literature, which makes a combined comparison of this kind relatively unusual and therefore quite valuable for assessing the overall performance of the two catalyst forms side by side under the conditions used in these tests.
