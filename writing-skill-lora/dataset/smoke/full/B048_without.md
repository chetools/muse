The rate constant k (s-1) of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was measured at two different temperatures, and the results are presented in this passage. At 300 K, the rate constant was found to be k = 0.07 s-1. At the higher temperature of 340 K, the value was k = 1.08 s-1, although this second value was not measured directly in the same way; instead, it was computed from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 58 kJ mol-1, and it is therefore a derived value.

Each temperature was measured in triplicate, which helps to give some confidence in the results, and the relative standard deviation of k was 4%. This level of scatter is fairly small compared with the very large difference between the two rate constants themselves, which increase from 0.07 s-1 at 300 K to 1.08 s-1 at 340 K.

Above 340 K, however, no rate constant is reported. The reason for this is that the solvent evaporated during sampling at higher temperatures, which made it impossible to obtain a reliable value. So the reported results are limited to the two temperatures of 300 K and 340 K, with the triplicate measurements and the relative standard deviation of 4% applying to the measurements as described, and with the higher of the two values, 1.08 s-1 at 340 K, resting on the Arrhenius calculation with its activation energy of 58 kJ mol-1 rather than on a direct measurement at that temperature alone.
