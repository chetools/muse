This example calculates the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film: the flux J is 1.30 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1. The diffusive flux J (mol m-2 s-1) is computed from the diffusivity D (m2 s-1) and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C denotes concentration (mol m-3) and x denotes position (m). Fick's first law gives the flux as J = -D dC/dx. Note that the negative sign states that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration.

Oxygen in water has a diffusivity D of 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. Concentration falls by 0.4 mol m-3 across a film 0.8 mm thick; the concentration gradient thus stands at dC/dx = -500 mol m-4. Substitution of the diffusivity and the gradient into Fick's first law thus gives J = -2.6 x 10-9 x (-500), and the calculation gives J = 1.30 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1.

Fick's first law as stated holds for a stagnant medium. A moving medium adds convection, which the equation does not include. The computed flux stands for the stagnant film, at the stated diffusivity and gradient. The check on the result is the sign: a gradient of -500 mol m-4 with the negative sign in Fick's first law gives a positive flux of 1.30 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1, in the direction from high concentration to low concentration.
Each quantity in the calculation stands with its stated value. The diffusivity of oxygen in water is 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. The concentration fall of 0.4 mol m-3 across the 0.8 mm film fixes the gradient at -500 mol m-4. Fick's first law multiplies the diffusivity by the gradient, with the negative sign in the law, to give the flux. No convection term stands in the equation, and no value for a moving medium is computed in this example.
