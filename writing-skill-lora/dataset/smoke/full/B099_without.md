The aim of this worked example is to calculate the half-life of a first-order reaction, and then to find the fraction of the reactant that remains after 60 min. For a first-order reaction, the half-life is related to the rate constant by the equation t1/2 = ln(2) / k, where t1/2 is the half-life in min and k is the rate constant in min-1. The fraction of the reactant remaining after a time t can be calculated from the expression exp(-k t), where t is the time in min.

In this example, the measured rate constant is k = 0.046 min-1 at 323 K. This value was obtained from concentration measurements which were taken every 5 min over a total period of 90 min. Using the half-life equation with this value of the rate constant gives t1/2 = 15.1 min. Using the second expression with a time of 60 min gives the fraction remaining after 60 min as 0.06, which is dimensionless. Both of these results were calculated from the relations stated above, using the measured rate constant. The measurements behind the rate constant were taken at 323 K, every 5 min, over a period of 90 min in total, and the two calculations in this example are both based on the single measured value obtained from those measurements, which is k = 0.046 min-1.

Finally, it is worth noting that these relations apply to a first-order reaction only. A reaction which follows a different order would not behave in the same way, because it follows a different decay law, and so the same equations could not be used to calculate its half-life or the fraction remaining after a given time in the way illustrated here.
