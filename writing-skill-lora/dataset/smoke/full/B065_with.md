Calculate the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film 0.8 mm thick, across which the concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3. The result is a flux (J) of 4.00x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1, in the direction from the higher concentration toward the lower.

Fick's first law gives the diffusive flux J (mol m-2 s-1) from the diffusivity D (m2 s-1) and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C denotes concentration (mol m-3) and x denotes position (m): J = -D dC/dx. The negative sign in Fick's first law states that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration, opposite to the direction in which concentration increases with position.

Oxygen in water has a diffusivity (D) of 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. A fall of 0.2 mol m-3 across a film 0.8 mm thick gives a concentration gradient (dC/dx) of -250 mol m-4. Substitution of the diffusivity and the gradient into Fick's first law gives J = -(1.6 x 10-9) x (-250) = 4.00x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The gradient is smaller in magnitude than in a thinner film carrying the same fall of concentration, because the same fall (0.2 mol m-3) is spread over a greater thickness (0.8 mm), and the flux follows the gradient in proportion through the diffusivity.

Fick's first law holds for a stagnant medium. A moving medium adds convection, which Fick's first law as stated does not include; a film with flow through it would need the convection term alongside the diffusive flux computed here. The worked result (4.00x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1) is the diffusive flux for the stagnant film at the stated diffusivity (1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1) and the stated gradient (-250 mol m-4), and no other transport contribution enters it.
