Pervaporation separates a liquid mixture by contact with one side of a membrane; vacuum on the other side draws the permeating component through as vapour. Two membranes were compared on one feed (85 wt% isopropanol in water, at 60 °C), and flux, selectivity and stability are reported together for the same membrane, a combination seldom reported. Flux is the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour (kg m-2 h-1). Separation factor is the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed (dimensionless).

Polymer gave the higher flux (1.9 kg m-2 h-1, against 1.2 kg m-2 h-1 for zeolite). Zeolite gave the higher separation factor (560, against 120 for polymer). Higher flux and higher selectivity therefore sit in different membranes, and a choice between the two rests on which quantity governs the separation duty. Stability separates the pair in the same direction as selectivity: polymer flux fell 12% over 500 h, while zeolite flux was unchanged over 500 h within measurement error. A membrane selected for throughput carries a measured flux loss over the test period; a membrane selected for separation factor carries none within measurement error.

One feed composition was tested, and no test ran beyond 500 h. At that feed and over that period, zeolite holds the higher separation factor (560) and unchanged flux, while polymer holds the higher flux (1.9 kg m-2 h-1) with a 12% loss over 500 h. Behaviour at other feed compositions, and beyond 500 h for either membrane, is outside the tested record.
