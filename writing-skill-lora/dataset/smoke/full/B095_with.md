Recommendation under consideration: install the scheduling system at Plant 2 first, and hold the other plants until Plant 2 results are reported. The operations director owns the scheduling programme. Evidence base: order records for 12 months, 16892 orders, at a firm running 4 plants with revenue of $112 million per year.

On-time delivery over the last two quarters was 90%, against a target of 95%. Overtime cost was $2.4 million last year, up 29% on the year before, and the order backlog stands at 4.1 weeks, against 2 weeks a year ago. Plant 2 accounts for 67% of late orders. Late orders are concentrated in one plant, while the delivery shortfall and the overtime cost are recorded across the firm. The two quarters of delivery figures and the 12 months of order records are the whole evidence base behind the recommendation under consideration. Overtime and backlog both moved against delivery over the period the records cover, at $2.4 million of overtime cost and 4.1 weeks of backlog at the end of it.

A scheduling system for all plants is quoted at $317k; Plant 2 alone is $126k. An internal estimate, not a vendor figure, puts the overtime reduction at Plant 2 at 15-20% in the first year. Plant 2-first installation commits $126k against the plant holding 67% of late orders, and holds $317k in full installation until Plant 2 results are reported.
