This summary looks at a possible heat-recovery retrofit at a food plant. The plant currently uses 2364 MWh of electricity per year for refrigeration, which is a significant part of its energy consumption. According to projections made by the equipment vendor, the retrofit would save 16% of that electricity use, which works out at a derived saving of 378 MWh per year if the vendor's figure is correct. The capital cost of the retrofit would be $409k. At an electricity price of $0.12 per kWh, the derived annual saving would be $45k, and the derived simple payback period would be 9.1 years, which is quite a long time to recover the investment.

It is important to be careful about these numbers, because the saving figure is only a vendor projection and has not been measured at this plant. In addition, the refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, so the projection may not fully reflect the plant's actual pattern of use over the year.

For these reasons, the recommendation is that a metered trial should be carried out on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is taken. The plant engineer will own the trial and will be responsible for reporting what it shows.
