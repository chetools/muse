In this worked example, the goal is to compute the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film using Fick's first law. The diffusive flux J, expressed in mol m-2 s-1, is computed from the diffusivity D, in m2 s-1, and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C is the concentration in mol m-3 and x is position in m. Fick's first law is written as J = -D dC/dx, and the negative sign in the equation indicates that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration. For oxygen in water, the diffusivity D is 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. In the film considered here, the concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film that is 0.8 mm thick, so the concentration gradient dC/dx is -250 mol m-4. Substituting these values into Fick's first law gives J = 6.50x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. This is the worked result for the diffusive flux of oxygen through the film. It should be noted that the law as stated covers a stagnant medium. A moving medium adds convection, which the equation does not include, so the calculation applies to the stagnant case only. To recap the main numbers used in the example, the diffusivity is 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1, the concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film 0.8 mm thick, the gradient is -250 mol m-4, and the resulting flux is 6.50x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The example therefore shows how the flux follows directly from the diffusivity and the concentration gradient once those two quantities are known for the film.
 The film thickness matters to the result because the gradient is the fall in concentration divided over that thickness: a fall of 0.2 mol m-3 over 0.8 mm gives the gradient of -250 mol m-4 used above, and a different thickness would give a different gradient and therefore a different flux from the same law.
