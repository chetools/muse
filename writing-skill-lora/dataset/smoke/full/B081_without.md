The rate constant of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was measured at two temperatures, in order to examine how strongly the rate depends on temperature. At 300 K, the rate constant k was 0.068 s-1. At 340 K, the rate constant was much larger, at 1.266 s-1. This second value was not measured directly in the same way, but was computed from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 62 kJ mol-1. Each temperature was measured in triplicate, and the relative standard deviation of k was 4%, which indicates good reproducibility of the measurements.

The increase from 0.068 s-1 at 300 K to 1.266 s-1 at 340 K shows that the reaction speeds up considerably as the temperature rises over this interval. The measurements cover the range from 300 K to 340 K. Above 340 K, however, the solvent evaporated during sampling, so no rate constant is reported above 340 K, and the behaviour of the reaction at higher temperatures cannot be described from these data.

In summary, the rate constant rises from 0.068 s-1 at 300 K to a derived value of 1.266 s-1 at 340 K, with measurements made in triplicate at each temperature and a relative standard deviation of 4%. Any use of these values should keep in mind that the upper value is a derived value based on the Arrhenius relation, and that no value is available above 340 K because of the evaporation of the solvent during sampling at higher temperatures in this experimental arrangement.
The two temperatures, 300 K and 340 K, are the only ones reported, and each was measured in triplicate with the same relative standard deviation of 4% applying to the measurements as a whole across the reported range of the study.
