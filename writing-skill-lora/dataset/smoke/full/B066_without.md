This example works through the half-life of a first-order reaction, together with the fraction of material remaining after 60 min. For a first-order reaction, the rate constant k is directly linked to the half-life through the well-known relationship t1/2 = ln(2) / k, where k is expressed in min-1 and the half-life t1/2 is expressed in min. In addition, the fraction remaining after a given time t can be obtained from the expression exp(-k t), which gives a dimensionless value once k and t are inserted consistently.

In this case, the measured rate constant is k = 0.023 min-1 at 323 K. This value was obtained from concentration measurements taken every 5 min over a period of 90 min, providing a solid basis for the calculation. Substituting the rate constant into the half-life expression gives t1/2 = ln(2) / 0.023, which works out to 30.1 min.

Turning to the fraction remaining after 60 min, the calculation uses exp(-k t) with k = 0.023 min-1 and t = 60 min. This gives a fraction remaining of 0.25, which is dimensionless, as expected for a fraction. The two results describe the decay behaviour: the half-life is 30.1 min, and after 60 min a fraction of 0.25 of the material remains.

It is worth noting that these relationships hold for a first-order reaction only. A reaction of another order follows a different decay law, so the same expressions cannot simply be applied in that case. Overall, the example shows how a single measured rate constant, determined at 323 K from measurements every 5 min over 90 min, can be used to obtain both the half-life of 30.1 min and the fraction remaining of 0.25 after 60 min for this first-order reaction.
