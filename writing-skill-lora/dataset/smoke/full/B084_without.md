This summary reviews delivery performance at a contract packaging firm that runs 4 plants and has revenue of $79 million per year. The evidence base is the firm's order records for 12 months, covering 14326 orders in total, so the picture presented here rests on a full year of orders rather than a small sample.

On-time delivery over the last two quarters was 84%, which is well below the target of 95%. Overtime cost was $1.8 million last year, up 21% on the year before, and the order backlog now stands at 3.4 weeks, compared with 1.9 weeks a year ago. Plant 2 accounts for 62% of late orders, so the problem is heavily concentrated in one plant rather than spread evenly across the firm.

A scheduling system for all plants has been quoted at $319k, while Plant 2 alone would cost $138k. An internal estimate, which is not a vendor figure, puts the overtime reduction at Plant 2 at 15-20% in the first year. The recommendation under consideration is therefore to install at Plant 2 first, and to hold the other plants until the Plant 2 results have been reported. The operations director owns the scheduling programme, and the staged approach limits the initial spending to the $138k for Plant 2 while the case for the remaining plants is tested against actual results there over the first year of operation.
The figures behind this are worth restating: 14326 orders over 12 months across 4 plants, on-time delivery of 84% against a 95% target over the last two quarters, and a backlog that has grown from 1.9 weeks to 3.4 weeks in a year.
