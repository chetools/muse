The purpose of this worked example is to work out the Reynolds number for water in a pipe and then to state the flow regime that goes with it. The Reynolds number Re is dimensionless, and it is calculated from the density rho (kg m-3), the mean velocity v (m s-1), the pipe diameter D (m) and the dynamic viscosity mu (Pa s), using the formula Re = rho v D / mu.

For water at 293 K, the density is rho = 998 kg m-3 and the dynamic viscosity is mu = 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe in this example has a diameter of D = 0.025 m, and the mean velocity of the water is v = 1.8 m s-1, so every quantity that the formula needs is now stated.

Substituting the numbers gives Re = 998 x 1.8 x 0.025 / 0.001, and the result of the calculation is Re = 44900. To interpret this result, the usual thresholds can be used: the flow is laminar below Re = 2300, and it is turbulent above Re = 4000. Between these two values the regime is transitional, and it is not assigned by the number alone, so a value in that middle band would need more than the number to classify it.

Since 44900 is far above the turbulent threshold of 4000, the flow here is turbulent. In summary, water at 293 K, with a density of 998 kg m-3 and a viscosity of 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s, flowing at 1.8 m s-1 in a pipe of diameter 0.025 m, has a Reynolds number of 44900, and the flow regime is turbulent rather than laminar or transitional.
