Calculate the Reynolds number for water in a pipe and state the flow regime. The result is Re = 71900 (dimensionless), and the flow is turbulent. The Reynolds number Re is computed from the density rho (kg m-3), the mean velocity v (m s-1), the pipe diameter D (m) and the dynamic viscosity mu (Pa s) by Re = rho v D / mu. Water at 293 K has rho = 998 kg m-3 and mu = 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. Take the pipe diameter as D = 0.08 m and the mean velocity as v = 0.9 m s-1. Substitution of these values in Re = rho v D / mu gives Re = 998 x 0.9 x 0.08 / 0.001. The numerator collects the density, velocity and diameter terms (998 x 0.9 x 0.08 = 71.9, in the combined units of the three quantities), and division by the viscosity (1.00 x 10-3 Pa s) removes the units. Thus Re = 71900, and the Reynolds number is dimensionless because the units in the numerator and the denominator cancel. The regime is read from the thresholds. Flow is laminar below Re = 2300 and turbulent above Re = 4000; between the two values the regime is transitional and is not assigned by the number alone. The computed value (71900) stands above the upper threshold (4000) by a wide margin, in the range the thresholds assign to turbulent flow. No part of the transitional range is involved: the value does not fall between 2300 and 4000, and the assignment does not rest on the number alone in the way the transitional case would. The check on the result is the threshold comparison itself. A value below 2300 would be laminar, a value between the thresholds would stay unassigned, and the value obtained (71900) is turbulent.
