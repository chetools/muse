This example calculates the half-life of a first-order reaction and the fraction remaining after 60 min: the half-life t1/2 is 15.1 min, and the fraction remaining after 60 min is 0.06. For a first-order reaction the rate constant k (min-1) fixes the half-life t1/2 (min) by t1/2 = ln(2) / k, and the fraction remaining after time t (min) is exp(-k t).

The measured rate constant is k = 0.046 min-1 at 323 K, from concentration measurements every 5 min over 90 min. Substitution of the rate constant into the half-life relation thus gives t1/2 = ln(2) / 0.046, and the calculation gives t1/2 = 15.1 min. Substitution of the rate constant and t = 60 min into the fraction relation thus gives exp(-0.046 x 60), and the calculation gives a fraction remaining of 0.06 (dimensionless).

The two relations hold for a first-order reaction only; a reaction of another order follows a different decay law. Both results stand on the one measured rate constant, at 323 K, from measurements every 5 min over 90 min.
The measurement record behind the rate constant stands as follows. Concentration was measured every 5 min over 90 min, at 323 K, and the rate constant obtained from those measurements is 0.046 min-1. The half-life of 15.1 min and the fraction of 0.06 remaining after 60 min are both computed from that single value. No second rate constant, and no result at another temperature, is reported in this example.
