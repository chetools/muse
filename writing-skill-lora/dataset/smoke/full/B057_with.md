For a feed of 85 wt% ethanol in water at 60 °C, a zeolite membrane gave a separation factor three times that of a polymer membrane (560 against 180) at a lower flux (1.2 against 1.9 kg m-2 h-1), and the zeolite flux was unchanged over 500 h where the polymer flux fell by 14%. Pervaporation is a membrane separation in which a liquid feed contacts one side of a membrane and vacuum on the other side draws the permeating component through as vapour. Flux is defined as the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour (kg m-2 h-1), and the separation factor is defined as the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed (dimensionless).

One feed composition was tested, at one temperature (60 °C), and no test ran beyond 500 h. Flux, selectivity and stability are reported together for the same membrane in each case, a combination seldom reported, and the trade-off between the two membranes rests on that shared basis: the polymer membrane carries the higher flux (1.9 kg m-2 h-1) with the lower separation factor (180) and a flux decrease of 14% over 500 h, while the zeolite membrane carries the lower flux (1.2 kg m-2 h-1) with the higher separation factor (560) and no change in flux over 500 h within measurement error.

Stability beyond 500 h was not measured for either membrane, and performance at other feed compositions than 85 wt% ethanol in water is likewise untested.
