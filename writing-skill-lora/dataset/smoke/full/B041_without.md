This executive summary looks at a proposed heat-recovery retrofit at a food plant and considers whether the project should go ahead at this stage. The food plant uses 1877 MWh of electricity per year in refrigeration. The equipment vendor projects that the retrofit will save 14% of that use, which gives a derived saving of 263 MWh per year at the vendor figure. The capital cost of the retrofit is $506k. At an electricity price of $0.13 per kWh, the derived annual saving is $34k and the derived simple payback is 14.9 years. It is important to note that the saving figure is a vendor projection and was not measured at this plant, so the actual saving could be different from the projected one. In addition, refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, so the analysis does not include that part of the year. Because of this, the recommendation is a metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is made. The trial would provide measured data from the plant itself instead of relying on the vendor projection alone. The plant engineer owns the trial. In summary, the proposal is to test the retrofit on one line first, measure the saving there, and only then consider the capital decision, rather than committing the $506k capital cost on the basis of the vendor projection.
 The numbers used in this summary are consistent throughout: 1877 MWh of electricity per year in refrigeration, a projected saving of 14%, a capital cost of $506k, an electricity price of $0.13 per kWh, a derived annual saving of $34k and a derived simple payback of 14.9 years.
