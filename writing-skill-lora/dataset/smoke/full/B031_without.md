In this worked example, we are going to compute the Reynolds number for water flowing in a pipe and then state what flow regime it corresponds to. The Reynolds number, Re, is dimensionless, and it is computed from the density rho, the mean velocity v, the pipe diameter D and the dynamic viscosity mu using the relation Re = rho v D / mu. Here rho is in kg m-3, v is in m s-1, D is in m and mu is in Pa s. For water at 293 K, the density is 998 kg m-3 and the dynamic viscosity is 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe diameter is 0.05 m and the mean velocity is 2.3 m s-1. Putting these values into the equation gives Re = 998 x 2.3 x 0.05 / 0.001, which works out to a value of 114800. So the Reynolds number for this case is 114800. In general, flow is laminar below Re = 2300 and turbulent above Re = 4000. Between these two values the regime is transitional and is not assigned by the number alone. Since 114800 is well above 4000, the flow in this example falls on the turbulent side of the stated thresholds. It is worth noting that the result depends on all four quantities in the relation, namely the density, the velocity, the diameter and the viscosity, and changing any of them would change the Reynolds number obtained. The calculation therefore uses the water properties at 293 K given above, together with the stated pipe diameter of 0.05 m and the stated mean velocity of 2.3 m s-1, to give the final dimensionless result of 114800 for this pipe flow case.
 To summarise the inputs once more, the density is 998 kg m-3, the viscosity is 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s, the diameter is 0.05 m and the velocity is 2.3 m s-1.
