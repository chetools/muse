The adsorption of a dye from water onto a metal-organic framework was investigated in a series of batch tests, all of which were carried out at a temperature of 298 K. In this study the amount of dye that the material could take up was characterised using the Langmuir model. When the data were fitted to the Langmuir model, the fitted capacity came out at 197 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent. This fitted value was obtained at pH 6, and it represents the maximum uptake capacity estimated by the model under those conditions.

In a separate part of the work, the removal of the dye during a batch test was measured directly. At an adsorbent dose of 0.9 g L-1, the removal after 120 min reached 87% of the initial dye concentration, which was 50 mg L-1 at the start of the test. In other words, after two hours of contact between the water and the adsorbent at this dose, most of the dye that was initially present had been taken up by the material, leaving only a relatively small fraction still dissolved in the water.

It is important to keep in mind, however, that the Langmuir fit is reported for pH 6 only. The capacity of the metal-organic framework at other pH values was not measured in this study, so it cannot be assumed that the same capacity of 197 mg per gram would apply if the water were more acidic or more alkaline than pH 6. The results as a whole show that the material takes up a substantial amount of dye at 298 K under the conditions tested, with a fitted Langmuir capacity of 197 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent at pH 6 and a removal of 87% after 120 min at a dose of 0.9 g L-1 from an initial concentration of 50 mg L-1, and these figures together give a reasonable picture of the performance of the framework for this application within the limits of the tests that were actually carried out in this work.
