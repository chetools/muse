An amine absorption unit was tested at pilot scale on gas coming from a cement kiln, in order to evaluate its performance for capturing carbon dioxide under realistic operating conditions. The test programme ran for a period of 45 days, during which the unit treated gas at a rate corresponding to 9 t of carbon dioxide per day. Over this period, the unit captured 92% of the carbon dioxide present in the treated gas, which represents a high level of capture performance. The steam demand of the process was measured as 3.3 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, and this figure represents an important part of the operating cost of the unit. The solvent loss was 2.1 kg per tonne of carbon dioxide captured. Taken together, these results indicate that the amine absorption unit operated effectively at pilot scale over the 45 days of testing, capturing the large majority of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas at the stated steam demand and solvent loss. However, it should be noted that the test programme lasted for 45 days only. Beyond 45 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent is untested, so it is not known from this work how the solvent would be affected by kiln dust over longer periods of operation, or whether the capture performance would be maintained.
