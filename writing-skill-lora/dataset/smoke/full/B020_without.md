In this example we are going to calculate the Reynolds number for water flowing in a pipe, and then use the result to say what the flow regime is. The Reynolds number, Re, is a dimensionless quantity that is calculated from the density, the mean velocity, the pipe diameter and the dynamic viscosity using the formula Re = rho v D / mu, where rho is the density, v is the mean velocity, D is the pipe diameter and mu is the dynamic viscosity. For water at 293 K, the density is 998 kg m-3 and the dynamic viscosity is 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe in this example has a diameter of 0.08 m, and the water is flowing with a mean velocity of 0.9 m s-1. Substituting all of these values into the formula gives Re = 998 x 0.9 x 0.08 / 0.001, which works out to a Reynolds number of 71900. Now we need to interpret this result. The general rules are that the flow is laminar when the Reynolds number is below 2300, and it is turbulent when the Reynolds number is above 4000. In between these two values, that is, between 2300 and 4000, the flow is in a transitional regime, and in that range the regime is not assigned by the number alone. Since the value we have calculated, 71900, is much greater than 4000, we can conclude that the flow in this pipe is turbulent. It is worth noting that the Reynolds number itself has no units, because it is a ratio of quantities whose units cancel. In this case the comparison is straightforward, because the calculated value, 71900, does not lie anywhere near the transitional band between 2300 and 4000, and the density and viscosity used are the values for water at 293 K, with a pipe diameter of 0.08 m and a mean velocity of 0.9 m s-1.
