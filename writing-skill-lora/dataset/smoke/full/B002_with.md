At 60 °C, on a feed of 85 wt% isopropanol in water, a zeolite membrane gave a separation factor of 560 with flux unchanged over 500 h within measurement error, against 120 and a 15% fall in flux over 500 h for a polymer membrane. Pervaporation is a membrane separation in which a liquid feed contacts one side of a membrane and vacuum on the other side draws the permeating component through as vapour. Flux (the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour, in kg m-2 h-1) was 1.5 kg m-2 h-1 for the polymer membrane and 0.9 kg m-2 h-1 for the zeolite membrane. Separation factor (the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed, dimensionless) was 120 for the polymer membrane against 560 for the zeolite membrane. Stability over 500 h separated the two membranes in the same direction as the separation factor: polymer flux fell 15% over 500 h, and zeolite flux was unchanged over 500 h within measurement error. One feed composition was tested, and no test ran beyond 500 h. Flux, selectivity and stability are seldom reported together for the same membrane, and the three quantities are reported together here for both membranes at the one feed composition tested. Membrane choice at this feed composition rests on the quantity weighted most heavily: the polymer membrane carries the higher flux (1.5 against 0.9 kg m-2 h-1), and the zeolite membrane carries the higher separation factor (560 against 120) with flux unchanged over 500 h within measurement error.
