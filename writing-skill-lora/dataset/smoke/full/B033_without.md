This worked example is about computing the half-life of a first-order reaction and then finding the fraction remaining after 60 min. For a first-order reaction, the rate constant k, in min-1, fixes the half-life t1/2, in min, through the relation t1/2 = ln(2) / k. The fraction remaining after a time t, in min, is given by exp(-k t). In this example, the measured rate constant is k = 0.046 min-1 at 323 K. This value comes from concentration measurements taken every 5 min over a period of 90 min. Using the half-life relation, t1/2 = ln(2) / 0.046, which gives a half-life of 15.1 min. Then, using the fraction relation at t = 60 min, the fraction remaining after 60 min is 0.06, which is a dimensionless quantity. So the two worked results are a half-life of 15.1 min and a fraction remaining of 0.06 after 60 min. It is important to keep in mind that these relations hold for a first-order reaction only. A reaction of another order follows a different decay law, so the same formulas would not apply in that case. In summary, starting from the measured rate constant of 0.046 min-1 at 323 K, the example obtains the half-life and the fraction remaining at 60 min, and both results are computed from the stated relations for a first-order reaction.
 The measurement schedule behind the rate constant is also part of the example: concentrations were measured every 5 min over 90 min at 323 K, and it is from those measurements that the value k = 0.046 min-1 used in both calculations was obtained in the first place.
