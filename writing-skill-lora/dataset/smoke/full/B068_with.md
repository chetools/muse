Pervaporation separates a liquid mixture by contact with one side of a membrane, with vacuum on the other side drawing the permeating component through as vapour. Flux, selectivity and stability are seldom reported together for the same membrane. A polymer membrane and a zeolite membrane were compared on a feed of 85 wt% ethanol in water at 60 °C. Flux is defined as the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour (kg m-2 h-1). The separation factor is defined as the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed (dimensionless).

The polymer membrane gave the higher flux (2.1 kg m-2 h-1 against 1.1 kg m-2 h-1 for the zeolite membrane) and the lower separation factor (240 against 420). Flux stability over 500 h differed between the membranes: flux fell 15% over 500 h for the polymer membrane, and flux was unchanged over 500 h within measurement error for the zeolite membrane. Flux and the separation factor were reported for the same membrane in each case, at 60 °C on the one feed composition tested (85 wt% ethanol in water): 2.1 kg m-2 h-1 and 240 for the polymer membrane, 1.1 kg m-2 h-1 and 420 for the zeolite membrane.

One feed composition was tested, and no test ran beyond 500 h. The comparison is limited to 85 wt% ethanol in water at 60 °C and to 500 h. At the tested conditions, the polymer membrane holds the higher flux (2.1 kg m-2 h-1) with a 15% fall in flux over 500 h, and the zeolite membrane holds the higher separation factor (420) with flux unchanged over 500 h within measurement error.

Flux, the separation factor, and flux stability were reported together for the same membrane: the polymer membrane at 2.1 kg m-2 h-1, a separation factor of 240, and a fall in flux of 15% over 500 h, and the zeolite membrane at 1.1 kg m-2 h-1, a separation factor of 420, and flux unchanged over 500 h within measurement error, on 85 wt% ethanol in water at 60 °C.
