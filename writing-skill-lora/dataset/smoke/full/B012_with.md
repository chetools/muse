At 250 °C and 50 bar, a washcoated monolith catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.94 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 0.24 bar for dimethyl ether production from carbon dioxide and hydrogen, against 0.48 kg L-1 h-1 at 0.95 bar for an extruded pellet catalyst. Carbon dioxide and hydrogen react over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to give dimethyl ether. Space-time yield (the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour, in kg L-1 h-1) is reported for both catalyst forms at a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, with one feed composition tested. Washcoated monolith performance in full: space-time yield 0.94 kg L-1 h-1, pressure drop 0.24 bar, activity loss 8% after 100 h. Extruded pellet performance in full: space-time yield 0.48 kg L-1 h-1, pressure drop 0.95 bar, activity loss 13% after 100 h. Pressure drop separates the two forms by a factor of about four (0.24 against 0.95 bar), in the same direction as space-time yield (0.94 against 0.48 kg L-1 h-1). Activity loss after 100 h was lower for the monolith (8%) than for the pellet form (13%). Yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge; both quantities are reported here for each charge, at 250 °C, 50 bar and a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3. No sulfur-bearing feed was tested, and catalyst behaviour under a sulfur-bearing feed stands outside the reported results. At the one feed composition tested, the washcoated monolith gave the higher space-time yield (0.94 kg L-1 h-1), the lower pressure drop (0.24 bar) and the lower activity loss after 100 h (8%), against 0.48 kg L-1 h-1, 0.95 bar and 13% for the extruded pellet. Test duration for the activity figures was 100 h for both forms. Feed conditions were common to both forms throughout: 250 °C, 50 bar, a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, and the one feed composition tested.
