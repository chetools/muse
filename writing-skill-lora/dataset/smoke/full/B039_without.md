This memo looks at whether sensors should be installed on the pumps at the plant. There are 260 pumps running at the plant. Maintenance records for the last 12 months show 1120 h of unplanned downtime, and bearing failures account for 43% of the downtime hours. A sensor set for all 260 pumps is quoted at $434k installed, plus $68k per year. In a vendor trial on a comparable unit, the sensors cut downtime by 25%. However, this figure is a vendor figure and was not measured at this plant. A pilot on 14 pumps costs $33k over three months. Spare-part lead times are unchanged by the sensors. This memo recommends the three-month pilot. The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and reports before the March capital review. The decision on full installation is held until the pilot results are reported. In other words, the plant would first run the pilot on 14 pumps for three months at a cost of $33k, and only after the pilot results are reported would the decision on installing sensors on all 260 pumps be taken.
 The full installation, if it were approved later, would cover all 260 pumps at the quoted price of $434k installed plus $68k per year, but that step is not being recommended at this stage of the proposal.
