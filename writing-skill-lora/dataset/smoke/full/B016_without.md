The adsorption of a dye from water onto activated carbon was studied in batch tests carried out at a temperature of 298 K. The results were analysed using the Langmuir model, and the fitted Langmuir capacity was determined to be 248 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent. This capacity was obtained at pH 7, and it should be emphasised that the Langmuir fit is reported for pH 7 only, because the capacity at other pH values was not measured during the course of this study. As well as determining the capacity, the study also measured how much of the dye could be removed under a particular set of conditions. At an adsorbent dose of 0.6 g L-1, the removal after 120 min was 88% of the initial dye concentration, which was 50 mg L-1 at the start of the test. These results demonstrate that activated carbon can remove a large proportion of the dye from water within 120 min under the conditions tested, and the fitted capacity of 248 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent at pH 7 provides a measure of the maximum uptake indicated by the Langmuir model at that pH. Nevertheless, because capacity was not measured at other pH values, the findings of this study do not show how the capacity would change if the pH were different, and any application of the capacity figure should take this limitation into account. The removal figure of 88% and the capacity figure of 248 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent were both obtained at 298 K, the temperature at which all of the batch tests in this study were carried out.
