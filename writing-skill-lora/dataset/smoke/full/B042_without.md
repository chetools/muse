In this worked example, the goal is to calculate the Reynolds number for water flowing through a pipe and to determine what flow regime it corresponds to. The Reynolds number is a very important dimensionless quantity in fluid mechanics, and it can be computed from the density rho (kg m-3), the mean velocity v (m s-1), the pipe diameter D (m) and the dynamic viscosity mu (Pa s), using the well-known relation Re = rho v D / mu.

For water at 293 K, the density is rho = 998 kg m-3 and the dynamic viscosity is mu = 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. In this particular case, the pipe diameter is D = 0.025 m and the mean velocity of the water is v = 0.7 m s-1, so all of the quantities needed for the calculation are now known.

Substituting these values into the equation gives Re = 998 x 0.7 x 0.025 / 0.001, which works out to Re = 17500. Overall, this is a fairly large value compared with the usual transition thresholds. It is generally accepted that the flow is laminar when the Reynolds number is below Re = 2300 and turbulent when it is above Re = 4000, while between these two values the regime is transitional and cannot be assigned by the number alone.

Therefore, since 17500 is well above 4000, the flow in the pipe can be described as turbulent. To summarise, the calculation shows that even at a relatively modest mean velocity of 0.7 m s-1, water in a pipe of diameter 0.025 m at 293 K, with density 998 kg m-3 and viscosity 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s, reaches a Reynolds number of 17500, comfortably inside the turbulent range defined by the threshold of 4000, and far from the laminar range below 2300 and from the transitional band between 2300 and 4000 where the number alone is not enough.
