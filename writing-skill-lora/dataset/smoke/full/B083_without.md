This memo concerns the case for fitting maintenance sensors to the pumps at the plant. There are 260 pumps in operation, and maintenance records for the last 12 months show 965 h of unplanned downtime. Bearing failures are the largest single cause, accounting for 41% of the downtime hours, so they are a natural target for any monitoring programme.

A sensor set for all 260 pumps has been quoted at $433k installed, plus a further $76k per year. In a trial carried out by the vendor on a comparable unit, the sensors cut downtime by 24%. It should be stressed, however, that this is a vendor figure and was not measured at this plant, so the same saving cannot be guaranteed here. It is also worth noting that spare-part lead times are unchanged by the sensors, so that part of the downtime problem would remain even with sensors fitted.

Rather than committing to the full installation at this stage, this memo recommends a three-month pilot on 24 pumps, at a cost of $35k. The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and will report before the March capital review. The decision on full installation is held until the pilot results have been reported, so that the plant has its own measured evidence, rather than the vendor trial figure of a 24% reduction in downtime on a comparable unit, before deciding whether to spend $433k installed plus $76k per year on the full set of 260 pumps.
To put the pilot in context, the plant has 260 pumps, the records cover 12 months and 965 h of unplanned downtime, and bearing failures alone account for 41% of those downtime hours. Against that background, a pilot costing $35k on 24 pumps over three months is a modest first step compared with the $433k installed cost, plus $76k per year, that would be involved in fitting sensors to all 260 pumps at once without local evidence from this plant itself.
