A washcoated monolith catalyst gave a higher space-time yield and a lower pressure drop than an extruded pellet catalyst for dimethyl ether production from carbon dioxide and hydrogen over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst, at one feed composition. Space-time yield is the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1). Test conditions were 230 °C, 60 bar and a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, with one feed composition. The washcoated monolith catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.83 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 0.11 bar, with an activity loss of 7% after 100 h. The extruded pellet catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.36 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 0.91 bar, with an activity loss of 14% after 100 h. Yield and pressure drop stand together for each charge: the monolith's yield advantage (0.83 against 0.36 kg L-1 h-1) is paired with a pressure drop about one eighth of the pellet value (0.11 against 0.91 bar), and its activity loss after 100 h is half the pellet loss (7% against 14%). Yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge, and the pairing is what the comparison rests on. No sulfur-bearing feed was tested, and behaviour with a sulfur-bearing feed is not covered by the results at the tested composition. At the tested conditions (230 °C, 60 bar, H2/CO2 = 3, one feed composition), the washcoated monolith catalyst holds the higher space-time yield, the lower pressure drop and the lower activity loss of the two forms compared.
 Each catalyst carries the same three reported quantities under the same conditions: space-time yield (kg L-1 h-1), pressure drop (bar) and activity loss after 100 h (%), at 230 °C and 60 bar with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, and the comparison between the two forms is made quantity by quantity on that shared basis.
