Recommendation: run a three-month pilot of pump sensors on 24 pumps, at a cost of $37k, and hold the decision on full installation until the pilot results are reported. The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and reports before the March capital review.

The plant runs 180 pumps. Maintenance records for 12 months show 1335 h of unplanned downtime, and bearing failures account for 40% of the downtime hours. The downtime figure and the bearing share come from the plant's own records over the stated 12 months, and the records are the evidence base for the pilot: 180 pumps, 12 months, 1335 h of unplanned downtime.

A sensor set for all 180 pumps is quoted at $388k installed, plus $74k per year. The pilot costs $37k over three months, against $388k installed and $74k per year for the full set: the pilot buys plant data before the full commitment is made. In a vendor trial on a comparable unit, the sensors cut downtime by 19%; the figure is a vendor figure and was not measured at this plant. Spare-part lead times are unchanged by the sensors, and no downtime saving from shorter lead times stands in the case for the pilot.

The pilot resolves the open point in the evidence. The vendor figure of 19% comes from a comparable unit, not from this plant, and the 40% bearing share and the 1335 h of downtime come from this plant's records.
