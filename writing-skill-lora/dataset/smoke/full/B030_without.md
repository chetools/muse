This executive summary looks at a possible heat-recovery retrofit at a food plant and at whether the project should move forward at this stage. The plant currently uses 2362 MWh of electricity per year in refrigeration, which is a substantial part of the overall picture being considered here. According to the equipment vendor, the retrofit is projected to save 12% of that electricity use, which works out to a derived saving of 283 MWh per year at the vendor figure. The capital cost of the retrofit is $367k. Using an electricity price of $0.12 per kWh, the derived annual saving is $34k, and the derived simple payback is 10.8 years. It is important to note that these savings are based on a vendor projection and were not measured at this plant, so there is some uncertainty around how the system would actually perform in practice. In addition, refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, which means the analysis does not cover every operating condition the plant experiences during the year. Because of these points, the recommendation is to carry out a metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is made. The trial would allow the plant to gather its own measured data rather than relying only on the vendor's projection. The plant engineer owns the trial. Overall, the proposal is therefore being treated cautiously, with the metered trial on one line coming first and the capital decision held until the trial results are available and can be assessed.
