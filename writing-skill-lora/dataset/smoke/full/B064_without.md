In this worked example, the aim is to calculate the Reynolds number for water flowing in a pipe and then to use the result to state what the flow regime is. The Reynolds number, which is written as Re and is dimensionless, is a very widely used quantity in fluid mechanics because it gives an indication of whether a flow is likely to be smooth and orderly or rough and turbulent. It is calculated from four quantities: the density rho in kg m-3, the mean velocity v in m s-1, the pipe diameter D in m, and the dynamic viscosity mu in Pa s. The relationship that connects them is Re = rho v D / mu.

For water at a temperature of 293 K, the values to be used are rho = 998 kg m-3 and mu = 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe in this example has a diameter of D = 0.025 m, and the mean velocity of the water in the pipe is v = 2.3 m s-1. Putting all of these numbers into the equation gives Re = 998 x 2.3 x 0.025 / 0.001, and carrying out the calculation gives a Reynolds number of 57400.

To interpret this result, it is necessary to compare it with the standard threshold values. Flow is laminar when the Reynolds number is below 2300, and it is turbulent when the Reynolds number is above 4000. Between those two values, the flow regime is transitional, and the regime cannot simply be assigned from the number alone. Since the value calculated here, 57400, is far above the upper threshold of 4000, the flow in this pipe can be stated to be turbulent. The margin is a large one, so there is no ambiguity about the regime in this particular case, unlike a case where the computed value fell in the transitional band between 2300 and 4000 and more care would be needed before making any statement about how the water is actually flowing in the pipe.
