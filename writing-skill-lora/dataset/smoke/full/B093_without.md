The adsorption of a dye from water onto a metal-organic framework was investigated in a series of batch tests carried out at 298 K. The experimental data were fitted using the Langmuir model, and the fitted Langmuir capacity of the adsorbent was found to be 185 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent at pH 7, which represents the maximum capacity obtained from the fit under the conditions studied. Tests were also carried out at an adsorbent dose of 1.1 g L-1, starting from an initial dye concentration of 50 mg L-1. After 120 min of contact, the removal achieved under these conditions was 85% of the initial dye concentration.

It is important to note that the Langmuir fit is reported for pH 7 only, as this was the pH value used in the tests described here. The capacity of the metal-organic framework at other pH values was not measured in this study, and so no conclusions can be drawn about how the adsorption performance might change if the pH of the water were higher or lower than 7. Overall, the results show that the material can remove a large proportion of the dye from water within 120 min at the dose tested, and that it has a fitted capacity of 185 mg per gram at pH 7 and 298 K in these batch tests.
