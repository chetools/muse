Calculate the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film. The result is J = 5.25x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The diffusive flux J (mol m-2 s-1) is computed from the diffusivity D (m2 s-1) and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C denotes concentration (mol m-3) and x denotes position (m). Fick's first law states the relation as J = -D dC/dx. Note that the negative sign in Fick's first law states that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration: the flux takes the sign opposite to the gradient. For oxygen in water, D = 2.1 x 10-9 m2 s-1. Concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film 0.8 mm thick; thus the concentration gradient is dC/dx = -250 mol m-4, the fall divided by the film thickness with the direction of the fall carried in the sign. Substitution of the diffusivity and the gradient in Fick's first law gives J = -(2.1 x 10-9) x (-250). The two negative signs cancel, and the product of the diffusivity and the gradient magnitude is 5.25x 10-7, in mol m-2 s-1. Thus J = 5.25x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1, directed from the high-concentration side of the film to the low-concentration side. Fick's first law as stated holds for a stagnant medium. A moving medium adds convection, and convection is not included in the equation; the flux computed here is the diffusive part alone, at the stated gradient across the 0.8 mm film. The gradient is fixed by the stated fall and the stated film thickness, and the diffusivity is the value for oxygen in water; the flux follows from those two quantities alone.
