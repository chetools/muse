Over 45 days on cement kiln gas at 9 t of carbon dioxide per day, a pilot amine absorption unit captured 88% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas. Steam demand was 3.2 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, and solvent loss was 2 kg per tonne captured. Capture, steam demand and solvent loss are reported for the same 45-day campaign, so the three figures describe one operating period at one throughput.

Kiln gas is the feed throughout the campaign, and the unit is an amine absorption unit at pilot scale. Capture, steam demand and solvent loss each refer to the carbon dioxide in that treated gas, at the campaign rate, over the full 45 days of the test.

Steam demand sets the energy cost of the capture at the measured rate: each tonne captured carries 3.2 gigajoules of steam over the campaign. Solvent loss sets the make-up cost on the same basis, at 2 kg for each tonne captured across the 45 days. Both figures rest on the treated gas rate of 9 t of carbon dioxide per day, and both are averages over the campaign rather than values from a single day. Capture at 88% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas was held over the same period in which those two consumption figures were recorded.

Beyond 45 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent is untested. The tested record is a 45-day campaign at 9 t of carbon dioxide per day, with capture at 88%, steam demand at 3.2 gigajoules per tonne captured and solvent loss at 2 kg per tonne captured. Solvent behaviour under kiln dust exposure past 45 days, and capture performance past that point, are outside the tested record.
