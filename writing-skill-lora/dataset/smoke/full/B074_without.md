This executive summary looks at a proposed heat-recovery retrofit at a food plant, where refrigeration currently uses a large amount of electricity each year. The plant uses 2456 MWh of electricity per year in refrigeration. The equipment vendor projects that a heat-recovery retrofit would save 15% of that electricity use, which corresponds to a derived saving of 368 MWh per year when calculated at the vendor figure.

The capital cost of the retrofit is $402k. Using an electricity price of $0.12 per kWh, the derived annual saving works out to $44k, and the derived simple payback is 9.1 years.

However, several important caveats need to be borne in mind. The saving figure is a vendor projection, and it was not measured at this plant, so the projected saving of 15%, and the derived saving of 368 MWh per year that rests on it, carry the uncertainty of an unmeasured projection. In addition, the refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, which is another gap in the assessment as it stands.

For these reasons, the recommendation is a metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is taken on the retrofit. The plant engineer owns the trial. In summary, the food plant uses 2456 MWh of electricity per year in refrigeration; the vendor projects a saving of 15% of that use, a derived saving of 368 MWh per year at the vendor figure, against a capital cost of $402k; at an electricity price of $0.12 per kWh the derived annual saving is $44k and the derived simple payback is 9.1 years; and the next step is the metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines, owned by the plant engineer, before any capital decision, with the saving unmeasured at this plant and the summer peak month load not modelled.
