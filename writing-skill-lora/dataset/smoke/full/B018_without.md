This summary reviews the delivery performance of a contract packaging firm that operates 4 plants and generates revenue of $95 million per year. The analysis is based on order records covering 12 months and a total of 15415 orders, so the picture it gives is a broad one. Over the last two quarters, on-time delivery stood at 88%, which falls short of the target of 95% that the firm has set itself. At the same time, the costs of keeping up with demand have been rising: overtime cost $2.3 million last year, an increase of 28% on the year before. The order backlog has also grown considerably, and it now stands at 4.1 weeks, compared with 1.8 weeks a year ago. Looking at where the problem is concentrated, Plant 2 stands out, because it accounts for 59% of all late orders, well over half of the total. One option under consideration is a new scheduling system. A system covering all plants is quoted at $277k, while installing at Plant 2 alone would cost $142k. An internal estimate, which is not a vendor figure, suggests that overtime at Plant 2 could be reduced by 15-20% in the first year after installation. The recommendation under consideration is therefore to install the system at Plant 2 first and to hold the other plants until the results at Plant 2 have been reported and can be assessed. The operations director owns the scheduling programme and would oversee this work. In short, delivery performance is below target, costs are rising, the backlog is growing, and the lateness is concentrated mainly at a single plant, which is why a phased installation beginning at Plant 2 is the option being considered.
