Pervaporation is a membrane separation in which a liquid feed contacts one side of a membrane and vacuum on the other side draws the permeating component through as vapour. A polymer membrane and a zeolite membrane were compared on a feed of 85 wt% isopropanol in water at 60 °C. Flux denotes the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour (kg m-2 h-1). The separation factor denotes the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed (dimensionless).

The polymer membrane gave a flux of 2.1 kg m-2 h-1 and a separation factor of 240, and its flux fell 13% over 500 h. The zeolite membrane gave a flux of 1.3 kg m-2 h-1 and a separation factor of 560, and its flux was unchanged over 500 h within measurement error. Flux and separation factor trade against each other across the pair: the polymer membrane carries the higher flux (2.1 kg m-2 h-1) and the lower separation factor (240), and the zeolite membrane carries the lower flux (1.3 kg m-2 h-1) and the higher separation factor (560). Stability over 500 h separates the pair in the same direction as the separation factor, with a fall of 13% for the polymer membrane against no change within measurement error for the zeolite membrane.

Flux, selectivity and stability are seldom reported together for the same membrane. The comparison is limited to the one feed composition tested, at 85 wt% isopropanol in water and 60 °C, and no test ran beyond 500 h.
