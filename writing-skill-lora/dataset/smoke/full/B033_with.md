Calculate the half-life of a first-order reaction and the fraction remaining after 60 min: the half-life is 15.1 min and the fraction remaining after 60 min is 0.06. For a first-order reaction, the rate constant k (min-1) fixes the half-life t1/2 (min) by t1/2 = ln(2) / k, and the fraction remaining after time t (min) is exp(-k t). The measured rate constant is k = 0.046 min-1 at 323 K, from concentration measurements every 5 min over 90 min. Substitution of k in the half-life relation gives t1/2 = ln(2) / 0.046; thus t1/2 = 15.1 min. Substitution of k and t = 60 min in the fraction relation gives exp(-0.046 x 60); thus the fraction remaining after 60 min is 0.06 (dimensionless). A check on the two results against each other: 60 min is about four half-lives at 15.1 min per half-life, and four halvings leave about one sixteenth of the starting amount, consistent with the computed fraction of 0.06. The relations hold for a first-order reaction only; a reaction of another order follows a different decay law. Both worked results (t1/2 = 15.1 min, fraction 0.06 at 60 min) rest on the single measured quantity, k = 0.046 min-1 at 323 K, and on the measurement record behind it, concentrations every 5 min over 90 min.
 The measurement record fixes the standing of k in the example: concentrations measured every 5 min over 90 min at 323 K give the one measured input, and every later number in the example (15.1 min, 0.06) is computed from that input by the two stated relations, not measured separately.
