The adsorption of a dye from water onto a metal-organic framework was studied in a series of batch tests, all of which were carried out at a temperature of 298 K. The results of the tests were fitted using the Langmuir model, and the fitted Langmuir capacity came out at 148 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent, at a pH of 6. In a separate way of looking at the performance, when an adsorbent dose of 0.8 g L-1 was used, the removal achieved after 120 min was 83% of the initial dye concentration, which was 50 mg L-1, meaning that most of the dye had been taken out of the water within two hours under those conditions. It is important to be clear about the limits of these results. The Langmuir fit is reported for pH 6 only, because that is the condition under which the fitting was performed, and the capacity of the material at other pH values was not measured at all. This means that the figure of 148 mg per gram should not be assumed to apply at a different pH, since the experiments simply do not cover that ground. All of the results described here, the fitted capacity of 148 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent at pH 6 and the removal of 83% after 120 min at a dose of 0.8 g L-1 from an initial concentration of 50 mg L-1, were obtained in batch tests at 298 K, and they describe the behaviour of this metal-organic framework for dye adsorption from water under those particular conditions. In other words, the study reports how much dye this adsorbent can hold at pH 6, namely 148 mg per gram, and how much dye was removed in 120 min at a dose of 0.8 g L-1, namely 83% of the starting 50 mg L-1, at 298 K.
