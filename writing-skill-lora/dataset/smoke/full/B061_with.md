Recommendation: run a three-month pilot of maintenance sensors on 21 pumps at a cost of $38k, and hold the decision on full installation until the pilot results are reported. The plant runs 214 pumps, and maintenance records for 12 months show 1004 h of unplanned downtime, of which bearing failures account for 48% of the downtime hours. The evidence base is those 12 months of maintenance records for the full pump population (214 pumps).

A sensor set for all 214 pumps is quoted at $461k installed, plus $63k per year. Against that commitment, the pilot costs $38k over three months on 21 pumps. In a vendor trial on a comparable unit, the sensors cut downtime by 26%; the 26% figure is a vendor figure and was not measured at this plant, so the benefit at this plant is unmeasured, and no downtime reduction at this plant is claimed in this memo. Spare-part lead times are unchanged by the sensors, and no part of the case for the pilot rests on faster supply of parts.

The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and reports before the March capital review. The report from the pilot, not the vendor trial, is the evidence on which the full-installation decision rests, and that decision is held until the pilot results are reported. The pilot covers 21 of the 214 pumps for three months at $38k, against a full installation quoted at $461k installed plus $63k per year.
