This example calculates the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film using Fick's first law. The diffusive flux J, expressed in mol m-2 s-1, is computed from the diffusivity D, in m2 s-1, together with the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C is the concentration in mol m-3 and x is the position in m. Fick's first law is written as J = -D dC/dx, and the negative sign in the equation indicates that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration, which is the physical meaning behind the sign convention.

For oxygen in water, the diffusivity is D = 2.1 x 10-9 m2 s-1. In the case considered here, the concentration falls by 0.4 mol m-3 across a film that is 0.4 mm thick, which gives a concentration gradient of dC/dx = -1000 mol m-4. Substituting these values into Fick's first law gives J = -2.1 x 10-9 x (-1000), which evaluates to J = 2.10 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1.

It is important to note the scope of the law as it is used here. The law as stated covers a stagnant medium, and a moving medium would add convection, which the equation does not include. In summary, the diffusive flux of oxygen through the stagnant water film is 2.10 x 10-6 mol m-2 s-1, obtained from a diffusivity of 2.1 x 10-9 m2 s-1 and a concentration gradient of -1000 mol m-4, arising from a fall of 0.4 mol m-3 across a film 0.4 mm thick, with diffusion running from high concentration to low concentration, and with the result applying to a stagnant medium, since convection in a moving medium is not included in the equation as stated.
