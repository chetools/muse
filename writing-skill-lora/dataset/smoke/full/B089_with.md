Carbon dioxide and hydrogen react over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to give formaldehyde. Two catalyst forms were tested at one feed composition (280 °C, 40 bar, feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3). Space-time yield is the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1). Yield and pressure drop are reported for the same catalyst charge for both forms; the two quantities are seldom reported together.

Structured catalyst gave the higher space-time yield (0.71 kg L-1 h-1) at the lower pressure drop (0.1 bar), with activity loss of 6% after 100 h. Pellet catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.35 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 1.07 bar, with activity loss of 20% after 100 h. Each value stands at the stated conditions, over the 100 h of the activity measurement.

No sulfur-bearing feed was tested. Behaviour with a sulfur-bearing feed is not reported for either form. At the tested composition, the structured catalyst holds the higher yield, the lower pressure drop and the smaller activity loss over 100 h.
The record stands on one basis for both forms. Temperature was 280 °C, pressure was 40 bar, and the feed ratio H2/CO2 was 3 in each test, at the one feed composition used. The structured value of 0.71 kg L-1 h-1 and the pellet value of 0.35 kg L-1 h-1 are space-time yields at those conditions, and the pressure drops and activity losses stand in the same record.
