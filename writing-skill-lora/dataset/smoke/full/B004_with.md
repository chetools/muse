Between 300 K and 340 K, the rate constant k (s-1) of a first-order liquid-phase reaction rose from 0.057 s-1 to a derived 2.858 s-1. Rate constant k at 300 K was 0.057 s-1, measured directly. Rate constant k at 340 K was 2.858 s-1, computed from the Arrhenius relation with activation energy 83 kJ mol-1, and the 340 K value is a derived value rather than a direct measurement. Each temperature was measured in triplicate. Relative standard deviation of k was 4% across the triplicate measurements at each temperature. Sampling sets the upper limit of the reported range: above 340 K the solvent evaporated during sampling, and no rate constant is reported above 340 K. Temperature dependence in the measured range is steep, with k at 340 K (2.858 s-1, derived) standing at about fifty times k at 300 K (0.057 s-1, measured). Precision of the underlying measurements is set by the triplicate design and the relative standard deviation of 4%. Reported values cover 300 K to 340 K only: a direct measurement at 300 K (0.057 s-1) and a derived value at 340 K (2.858 s-1, from the Arrhenius relation at activation energy 83 kJ mol-1), each measured in triplicate with a relative standard deviation of 4%, and no rate constant is reported above 340 K because the solvent evaporated during sampling above that temperature.
