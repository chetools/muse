This memo considers the case for installing a sensor set on the pumps at the plant, where a total of 214 pumps are currently in operation. Maintenance records covering a period of 12 months show a total of 968 h of unplanned downtime, which is a significant burden on the plant. Bearing failures are the largest single contributor to this problem, accounting for 50% of the downtime hours recorded over the 12-month period, and it is this category of failures that the proposed sensors are mainly intended to address.

A sensor set covering all 214 pumps has been quoted at $439k installed, with an additional ongoing cost of $76k per year. In a vendor trial carried out on a comparable unit, the sensors were reported to cut downtime by 26%, although it should be noted that this figure is a vendor figure and was not measured at this plant, so it may not be reproduced here. It should also be noted that spare-part lead times are unchanged by the sensors.

Rather than committing to the full installation at this stage, this memo recommends a three-month pilot on 22 pumps, at a cost of $40k. The reliability engineering group will own the pilot and will report its results before the March capital review. The decision on full installation will be held until the pilot results have been reported, so that the decision can be based on evidence gathered at this plant.
