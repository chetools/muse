Pervaporation is a membrane separation in which a liquid feed contacts one side of a membrane and vacuum on the other side draws the permeating component through as vapour. Two membranes were tested on one feed (85 wt% ethanol in water, at 60 °C). Flux is the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour (kg m-2 h-1). The separation factor is the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed (dimensionless). Flux, selectivity and stability are reported for the same membrane; the three are seldom reported together.

Polymer membrane gave the higher flux (2.1 kg m-2 h-1) at a separation factor of 240, and flux fell 9% over 500 h. Zeolite membrane gave a flux of 0.9 kg m-2 h-1 at a separation factor of 560, and flux was unchanged over 500 h within measurement error. Stability stands in the same record as flux and separation factor for both membranes, over the same 500 h period and at the same feed composition.

One feed composition was tested, and no test ran beyond 500 h. Behaviour at other compositions, and behaviour past 500 h, is not reported for either membrane. At the tested composition, the polymer membrane holds the higher flux and the zeolite membrane holds the higher separation factor and the unchanged flux over 500 h.
Each membrane stands in the record at the same feed and the same temperature. The feed was 85 wt% ethanol in water, the temperature was 60 °C, and the test period was 500 h for both membranes. The polymer flux of 2.1 kg m-2 h-1 and the zeolite flux of 0.9 kg m-2 h-1 are stated on that basis, with separation factors of 240 and 560 in the same record.
