Carbon dioxide and hydrogen can react over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to produce formaldehyde, and this study compares two forms of the catalyst. Performance is described by the space-time yield, which is the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour, in kg L-1 h-1. The tests were run at 280 °C and 40 bar, with a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used.

The structured catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.71 kg L-1 h-1, with a low pressure drop of 0.1 bar, and it lost 6% of its activity after 100 h. The pellet catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.35 kg L-1 h-1, with a pressure drop of 1.07 bar, and it lost 20% of its activity after 100 h, a much larger loss than that of the structured form.

Overall, the structured catalyst performed better on yield, pressure drop and activity loss alike. However, no sulfur-bearing feed was tested, so these results do not cover such a feed. Yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge, so reporting both for both forms at one feed composition allows the comparison to be made on paired values from the same charge rather than on separate measurements.
All of these figures were obtained at the same conditions of 280 °C and 40 bar, with a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3 and one feed composition, so the two catalyst forms are being compared on a like-for-like basis throughout this study.
