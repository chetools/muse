The rate constant k of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was measured at two different temperatures in order to examine how the reaction rate changes with temperature. The rate constant is expressed in s-1 throughout this work. At the lower temperature of 300 K, the rate constant was found to be k = 0.024 s-1. At the higher temperature of 340 K, the rate constant rose to k = 0.337 s-1, showing a substantial increase over the 40 K interval between the two measurements.

It should be noted that the value at 340 K was computed from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 56 kJ mol-1, and it is therefore a derived value rather than a directly measured one. Each temperature was measured in triplicate, which strengthens confidence in the reported figures, and the relative standard deviation of k was 4% across the measurements.

There is also an important practical limitation to be aware of. Above 340 K, the solvent evaporated during sampling, which meant that reliable measurements could not be made at higher temperatures. As a result, no rate constant is reported above 340 K in this work.

In summary, the first-order liquid-phase reaction studied here had a rate constant of 0.024 s-1 at 300 K and a derived rate constant of 0.337 s-1 at 340 K, the latter computed from the Arrhenius relation with an activation energy of 56 kJ mol-1. Both temperatures were measured in triplicate, with a relative standard deviation of k of 4%, and the reported range ends at 340 K because the solvent evaporated during sampling above that temperature, so no rate constant is reported above 340 K for this reaction.
