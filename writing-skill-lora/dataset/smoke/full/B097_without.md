In this worked example, the goal is to calculate the Reynolds number for water flowing in a pipe, and then to use the result to state the flow regime. The Reynolds number is an important dimensionless quantity in fluid mechanics, and it is calculated from the density of the fluid, the mean velocity, the pipe diameter and the dynamic viscosity using the formula Re = rho v D / mu, where rho is the density in kg m-3, v is the mean velocity in m s-1, D is the pipe diameter in m, and mu is the dynamic viscosity in Pa s.

For water at 293 K, the density is 998 kg m-3 and the dynamic viscosity is 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe diameter in this example is 0.08 m, and the mean velocity of the water is 1 m s-1. Substituting these values into the formula gives Re = 998 x 1 x 0.08 / 0.001, which works out to give a Reynolds number of 79800.

To interpret this result, the Reynolds number is compared with the standard threshold values. The flow is laminar when the Reynolds number is below 2300, and it is turbulent when the Reynolds number is above 4000. Between these two values, the flow is in a transitional regime, which is not assigned by the number alone. Since the calculated value of 79800 is well above 4000, the flow of water in this pipe is turbulent. The values used in the calculation are the values given for this example, namely water at 293 K, flowing in the stated pipe of 0.08 m diameter, at the stated mean velocity of 1 m s-1.
