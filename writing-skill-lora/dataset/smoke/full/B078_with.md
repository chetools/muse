Carbon dioxide and hydrogen react over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to give dimethyl ether. Two catalyst forms were tested at one feed composition (230 °C, 60 bar, feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3). Space-time yield is the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1). Yield and pressure drop are reported for the same catalyst charge for both forms; the two quantities are seldom reported together.

Washcoated monolith catalyst gave the higher space-time yield (0.99 kg L-1 h-1) at the lower pressure drop (0.3 bar). Extruded pellet catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.41 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 0.98 bar. Activity loss after 100 h was 7% for the washcoated monolith and 14% for the extruded pellet. Each result stands at the stated conditions: one feed composition, 230 °C and 60 bar, with the activity loss measured over 100 h. Pressure drop and yield were measured on the same charge in both cases, so the comparison between the two forms rests on paired values rather than on values drawn from separate charges.

No sulfur-bearing feed was tested. Behaviour with a sulfur-bearing feed is not reported for either form, and the comparison covers the single feed composition tested. At the tested composition, the washcoated monolith holds the higher yield, the lower pressure drop and the smaller activity loss over 100 h.
The record stands on the same basis for both forms throughout. Temperature was 230 °C, pressure was 60 bar and the feed ratio H2/CO2 was 3 in each test. The washcoated monolith value of 0.99 kg L-1 h-1 and the extruded pellet value of 0.41 kg L-1 h-1 are space-time yields at those conditions, and the pressure drops of 0.3 bar and 0.98 bar stand beside them in the same record for the same charge.
