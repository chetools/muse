The rate constant of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was measured at two different temperatures in this study, in order to characterise how the reaction rate depends on temperature. At the lower temperature of 300 K, the rate constant k was measured as 0.024 s-1. At the higher temperature of 340 K, the value of k was considerably larger, at 0.622 s-1. This second value was not measured directly, but was computed from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 69 kJ mol-1, and it is therefore a derived value rather than a direct measurement, a distinction that should be kept in mind when the two values are compared.

Each temperature was measured in triplicate, which means that three separate measurements were made under the same conditions, and the relative standard deviation of the rate constant was found to be 4%, indicating that the measurements were reasonably consistent with one another. The increase in the rate constant between 300 K and 340 K is substantial, rising by more than an order of magnitude across a temperature interval of only 40 K, which is consistent with the relatively high activation energy used in the Arrhenius calculation, and shows the strong sensitivity of this reaction to temperature changes.

The triplicate measurements were made at each of the two temperatures studied, 300 K and 340 K, and the same relative standard deviation of 4% applies to the rate constant values reported for this first-order liquid-phase reaction at both of those temperatures.

It is also important to note the upper limit of the measurements. Above 340 K, the solvent evaporated during sampling, which made it impossible to obtain reliable samples, and so no rate constant is reported above 340 K. As a result, the temperature dependence of the reaction is only characterised up to 340 K in this work, and any behaviour at higher temperatures remains unreported and would require a different sampling approach to be studied properly.
