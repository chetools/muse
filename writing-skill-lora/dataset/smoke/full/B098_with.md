Calculate the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film. The result is J = 5.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1, directed from high concentration to low concentration.

The diffusive flux J (mol m-2 s-1) is computed from the diffusivity D (m2 s-1) and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C denotes concentration (mol m-3) and x denotes position (m). Fick's first law is J = -D dC/dx; the negative sign states that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration. Oxygen in water has D = 1.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. Concentration falls by 0.25 mol m-3 across a film 0.8 mm thick, so dC/dx = -312 mol m-4.

Substitution gives J = -(1.6 x 10-9) x (-312). Thus J = 5.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The two negative signs, one in the law and one in the gradient, give a positive flux in the direction from high concentration to low concentration, as the sign convention in Fick's first law requires.

Fick's first law as stated holds for a stagnant medium; a moving medium adds convection, which the equation does not include. The flux of 5.00 x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1 is the diffusive flux through the stagnant film alone, at the stated diffusivity and the stated concentration fall across 0.8 mm, and no convection term enters the stated result.
