This memo concerns the problem of unplanned downtime on the pumps at the plant, and whether sensors should be installed to help reduce it. There are 180 pumps running at the plant, and the maintenance records for the last 12 months show a total of 1335 h of unplanned downtime, which is a significant amount of lost running time. Bearing failures are a major part of the problem, accounting for 40% of the downtime hours, so any measure that can detect bearing problems earlier could potentially be valuable.

A sensor set for all 180 pumps has been quoted at $388k installed, with an additional cost of $74k per year. In a vendor trial carried out on a comparable unit, the sensors were reported to cut downtime by 19%, although it should be stressed that this figure comes from the vendor and was not measured at this plant, so it may not translate directly. It is also worth noting that spare-part lead times would be unchanged by the sensors.

Rather than committing to the full installation straight away, this memo recommends a three-month pilot. A pilot on 24 pumps would cost $37k over three months, which is much less than the full cost. The reliability engineering group will own the pilot and will report before the March capital review, and the decision on full installation will be held until the pilot results have been reported and can be considered properly.
