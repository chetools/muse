In this study, the rate constant of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was measured at two temperatures, in order to investigate the effect of temperature on the reaction rate. The rate constant is denoted by k and is expressed in units of s-1. At a temperature of 300 K, the value of the rate constant was measured as k = 0.042 s-1. At a higher temperature of 340 K, the value of the rate constant was 1.444 s-1. It should be explained that this second value was computed from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 75 kJ mol-1, and it is therefore a derived value rather than a directly measured one. Each of the temperatures was measured in triplicate, and the relative standard deviation of k was found to be 4%, which indicates that the measurements were reasonably consistent with one another. There is also an upper limit to the temperature range covered by this work. No rate constant is reported above 340 K, because above 340 K the solvent evaporated during sampling, which prevented reliable measurements from being made at higher temperatures. In summary, the rate constant increased from 0.042 s-1 at 300 K to a derived value of 1.444 s-1 at 340 K, showing a strong dependence of the reaction rate on temperature over the range studied.
