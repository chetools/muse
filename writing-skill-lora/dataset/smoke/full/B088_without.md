The goal of this worked example is to compute the half-life of a first-order reaction, and also the fraction remaining after 60 min. For a first-order reaction, the rate constant k, in min-1, fixes the half-life t1/2, in min, through the relation t1/2 = ln(2) / k. The fraction remaining after a time t, in min, is given by exp(-k t).

The measured rate constant is k = 0.046 min-1 at 323 K. This value came from concentration measurements taken every 5 min over a period of 90 min. Substituting k into the half-life relation gives t1/2 = ln(2) / 0.046, which works out to 15.1 min. For the fraction remaining after 60 min, exp(-0.046 x 60) gives 0.06, a dimensionless value, meaning that only a small part of the original material is still present after 60 min.

It should be remembered that these relations hold for a first-order reaction only. A reaction of another order follows a different decay law, so neither the half-life of 15.1 min nor the fraction of 0.06 remaining after 60 min should be applied to a reaction whose order is not first. Both results are computed from the stated relations using the single measured rate constant of 0.046 min-1 at 323 K.
The measurements behind the rate constant were taken every 5 min over 90 min at 323 K, and the two computed results, the half-life and the fraction remaining after 60 min, both come from that one measured value of k.
