This executive summary looks at a proposed heat-recovery retrofit at a food plant, where refrigeration currently uses 2452 MWh of electricity per year. According to projections provided by the equipment vendor, the retrofit would save 13% of that electricity use, which works out at a derived saving of 319 MWh per year based on the vendor figure. The capital cost of the retrofit is $496k. Using an electricity price of $0.13 per kWh, the derived annual saving is $41k, and the derived simple payback on the investment is 12.1 years, which is a long payback period for a project of this kind.

It is important to emphasise that the saving figure behind these calculations is a vendor projection, and it was not measured at this plant, so the actual saving achieved in practice could be different from the figure used here. It should also be noted that the refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, and the peak month could have an important effect on the results. For these reasons, the recommendation is that a metered trial should be carried out on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is taken on the retrofit. The plant engineer will own the trial, and the trial will provide measured data from the plant itself to replace the vendor projection on which the current figures are based. The metered trial is therefore the recommended next step before any commitment of the $496k capital cost is made by the plant.
