The rate constant, k, of a first-order reaction taking place in the liquid phase was measured at two different temperatures, and the results of those measurements are reported here. The rate constant has units of s-1. At the lower temperature of 300 K, the measured value of the rate constant was k = 0.077 s-1. At the higher temperature of 340 K, the value was much larger, at k = 2.526 s-1. This second value was not measured in the same direct way, but was computed from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 74 kJ mol-1, and it is therefore a derived value rather than a directly measured one. The measurements at each temperature were carried out in triplicate, meaning that each one was repeated three times, and the relative standard deviation of k across those repeats was 4%, which gives an indication of how reproducible the measurements were. It would naturally be interesting to extend the measurements to still higher temperatures, but this was not possible in practice. Above 340 K, the solvent began to evaporate while the samples were being taken, and this evaporation during sampling meant that reliable samples could not be obtained. For that reason, no rate constant is reported for any temperature above 340 K, and the results of the study cover the two temperatures of 300 K and 340 K only, with a measured value at 300 K and a derived value at 340 K, each supported by measurements made in triplicate with a relative standard deviation of 4%. Taken together, the study gives a measured rate constant of 0.077 s-1 at 300 K and a derived rate constant of 2.526 s-1 at 340 K, the derivation using an activation energy of 74 kJ mol-1, and nothing beyond 340 K.
