Pervaporation is a membrane separation technique in which a liquid feed contacts one side of a membrane and a vacuum on the other side draws the permeating component through as a vapour. The flux is the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour, in kg m-2 h-1. The separation factor is the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed, and it is dimensionless. In this study, the feed was 85 wt% ethanol in water, at 60 °C. Only one feed composition was tested, and no test ran beyond 500 h. For the polymer membrane, the flux was 1.5 kg m-2 h-1 and the separation factor was 120, and the flux fell by 7% over 500 h. For the zeolite membrane, the flux was 1.3 kg m-2 h-1 and the separation factor was 320, and the flux was unchanged over 500 h within measurement error. Looking at the two membranes, the polymer membrane had the slightly higher flux, while the zeolite membrane had the higher separation factor and showed no change in flux over the test period. Flux, selectivity and stability are seldom reported together for the same membrane, so reporting all three here is useful. In summary, the comparison covers one feed composition of 85 wt% ethanol in water at 60 °C, with tests running up to 500 h, and it reports flux, separation factor and flux change over 500 h for both the polymer and the zeolite membranes.
