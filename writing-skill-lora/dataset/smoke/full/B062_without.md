This executive summary reviews the delivery performance of a contract packaging firm that runs 2 plants and has revenue of $100 million per year. The evidence base for the review consists of the order records for a period of 12 months, covering a total of 14469 orders, so the picture presented here is based on a substantial body of data about how the firm has actually performed.

Over the last two quarters, on-time delivery stood at 88%, which is below the target of 95% that the firm has set for itself. At the same time, costs have been rising: overtime cost was $1.5 million last year, which is up 25% on the year before. The order backlog has also grown considerably, and currently stands at 3.9 weeks, compared with 1.9 weeks a year ago, which suggests that the delivery problem is getting worse rather than better.

A closer look at the data shows that Plant 2 accounts for 60% of all late orders, even though the firm only has 2 plants, so the problem is heavily concentrated in one location. A new scheduling system for all of the plants has been quoted at $280k, while installing it at Plant 2 alone would cost $157k. An internal estimate suggests that overtime at Plant 2 could be reduced by 15-20% in the first year, and this estimate is an internal one rather than a figure supplied by a vendor.

The recommendation under consideration is therefore to install the system at Plant 2 first, and to hold off on the other plants until the results from Plant 2 have been reported. The operations director owns the scheduling programme and will be responsible for taking it forward.
