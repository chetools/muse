The adsorption of a dye from water onto activated carbon was investigated in a series of batch tests carried out at 298 K. The experimental data were fitted using the Langmuir model, and the fitted Langmuir capacity was found to be 218 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent. This capacity value was obtained at pH 6, and it is important to point out that the Langmuir fit is reported for pH 6 only, since the capacity at other pH values was not measured in this study. In addition to the capacity measurements, the removal performance of the activated carbon was also evaluated under specific operating conditions. At an adsorbent dose of 0.5 g L-1, the removal after 120 min reached 86% of the initial dye concentration. The initial dye concentration used in these tests was 50 mg L-1. Overall, these findings show that activated carbon is effective for removing the dye from water under the conditions that were tested, achieving a high level of removal within 120 min at a relatively low adsorbent dose. At the same time, the results should be interpreted with the scope of the study in mind, because the fitted capacity of 218 mg of dye per gram of adsorbent applies to pH 6, and no information is available from this work about how the capacity might change at other pH values, since those conditions were not measured. Further work would be needed to establish the capacity across a wider range of pH values. The batch tests therefore cover one temperature, 298 K, one initial dye concentration, 50 mg L-1, and the single reported pH value of 6 for the fitted capacity.
