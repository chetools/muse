This worked example calculates the Reynolds number for water flowing in a pipe, and then uses the result to state the flow regime. The Reynolds number, denoted Re, is a dimensionless quantity that is computed from the density rho, the mean velocity v, the pipe diameter D, and the dynamic viscosity mu, using the formula Re = rho v D / mu. 
For water at 293 K, the density is rho = 998 kg m-3 and the dynamic viscosity is mu = 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe diameter is D = 0.05 m, and the mean velocity is v = 2.3 m s-1. Substituting these values into the formula gives Re = 998 x 2.3 x 0.05 / 0.001, which evaluates to Re = 114800.

Flow is laminar below Re = 2300 and turbulent above Re = 4000; between these two values the regime is transitional and is not assigned by the number alone. Since Re = 114800 is above 4000, the flow is turbulent.
