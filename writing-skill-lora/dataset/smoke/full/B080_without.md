An amine absorption unit was tested at pilot scale on gas from a cement kiln, in order to assess how it performed over an extended period of operation. The trial ran for 90 days, during which the unit treated gas at a rate corresponding to 11 t of carbon dioxide per day. Over this period, the unit captured 88% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas, which represents a high level of capture sustained across the whole trial rather than just at its start.

The energy demand of the process was also recorded. Steam demand came to 3.1 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, and solvent loss was 2 kg per tonne captured. Both figures are expressed per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, so they can be compared directly with other operating periods of the same unit.

These results were obtained over 90 days of operation at 11 t of carbon dioxide per day on cement kiln gas. However, beyond 90 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent is untested, so it is not known whether the solvent would continue to perform in the same way over a longer period, or whether dust carried over from the kiln would change either the capture level or the solvent loss. The reported figures therefore describe the 90 days of the trial: a capture level of 88% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas, a steam demand of 3.1 gigajoules per tonne captured, and a solvent loss of 2 kg per tonne captured, all measured at pilot scale on gas from a cement kiln.
