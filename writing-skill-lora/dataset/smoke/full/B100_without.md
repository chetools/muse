In this work, carbon dioxide and hydrogen were reacted over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to produce formaldehyde, and two different catalyst forms were compared with one another. The performance of the catalysts was expressed in terms of the space-time yield, which is defined as the mass of product produced per unit catalyst volume per hour, in kg L-1 h-1. All of the tests were carried out under the same conditions, at 250 °C and 50 bar, with a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used throughout the study.

The washcoated monolith catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.67 kg L-1 h-1, with a pressure drop of 0.24 bar, and it lost 3% of its activity after 100 h on stream. The extruded pellet catalyst, by comparison, gave a lower space-time yield of 0.36 kg L-1 h-1, together with a much higher pressure drop of 1.39 bar, and it lost 17% of its activity after 100 h. The monolith therefore performed better than the pellets on all three of the measures reported here.

Both catalysts were tested at the same temperature and the same pressure, namely 250 °C and 50 bar, and the comparison between them covers the space-time yield, the pressure drop, and the activity loss after 100 h, for each of the two catalyst forms studied in this work.

No sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this study, so the effect of sulfur on either catalyst remains unknown. It is also worth noting that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported together for the same catalyst charge, which makes comparisons of this kind less common than they might otherwise be in the literature on catalysts for this reaction.
