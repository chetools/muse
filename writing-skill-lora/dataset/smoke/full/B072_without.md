This memo considers whether sensors should be installed on the pumps at the plant, where a total of 180 pumps are in operation. Maintenance records covering a period of 12 months show a total of 1095 h of unplanned downtime, which is a significant burden on the plant. Bearing failures are a major contributor to this problem, accounting for 51% of the downtime hours recorded over those 12 months.

A sensor set covering all 180 pumps has been quoted at $384k installed, with an additional cost of $70k per year. In a vendor trial carried out on a comparable unit, the sensors were reported to cut downtime by 21%. However, it should be emphasised that this figure is a vendor figure, and it was not measured at this plant, so it cannot be taken as a guarantee of the benefit here.

As a more cautious step, a pilot on 17 pumps would cost $44k over three months. Spare-part lead times, it should be noted, are unchanged by the sensors. This memo therefore recommends the three-month pilot on 17 pumps at a cost of $44k. The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and will report before the March capital review, and the decision on full installation, at $384k installed plus $70k per year for all 180 pumps, is held until the pilot results are reported by the reliability engineering group ahead of the March capital review.
