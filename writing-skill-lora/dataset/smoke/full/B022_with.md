Calculate the half-life of a first-order reaction and the fraction remaining after 60 min. The results are a half-life t1/2 = 10 min and a fraction remaining after 60 min of 0.02 (dimensionless). For a first-order reaction, the rate constant k (min-1) fixes the half-life t1/2 (min) by t1/2 = ln(2) / k, and the fraction remaining after time t (min) is exp(-k t). The measured rate constant is k = 0.069 min-1 at 323 K, from concentration measurements every 5 min over 90 min. The half-life follows from the rate constant alone: substitution of k = 0.069 min-1 in t1/2 = ln(2) / k gives t1/2 = 0.693 / 0.069. Thus t1/2 = 10 min. Half of the material present at any time is gone 10 min later, at the stated rate constant and temperature. The fraction remaining after 60 min follows from the same rate constant and the elapsed time: substitution of k = 0.069 min-1 and t = 60 min in exp(-k t) gives exp(-0.069 x 60) = exp(-4.14). Thus the fraction remaining after 60 min is 0.02, dimensionless; 60 min is six half-lives at a half-life of 10 min, and the stated fraction is the value the relation returns for that interval. Note that the two relations hold for a first-order reaction only. A reaction of another order follows a different decay law, and neither the half-life relation nor the fraction relation applies to it; both results here rest on the first-order form and on k = 0.069 min-1 at 323 K. Both results are computed from the stated relations and the one measured rate constant; no other rate constant, temperature, or time enters the calculation, and the measurement series behind k (every 5 min over 90 min at 323 K) is the data both results rest on.
