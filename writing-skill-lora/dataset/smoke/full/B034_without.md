This study looks at the reaction of carbon dioxide and hydrogen over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to give dimethyl ether, and it compares two different catalyst forms. The space-time yield is defined as the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour, in kg L-1 h-1. All of the tests were carried out at 230 °C and 60 bar, with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used. For the washcoated monolith catalyst, the space-time yield was 0.83 kg L-1 h-1, the pressure drop was 0.11 bar, and the activity loss was 7% after 100 h. For the extruded pellet catalyst, the space-time yield was 0.36 kg L-1 h-1, the pressure drop was 0.91 bar, and the activity loss was 14% after 100 h. Looking at these numbers together, the monolith gave a higher space-time yield than the pellet form, while also showing a lower pressure drop across the catalyst. The activity losses after 100 h were 7% for the monolith and 14% for the pellet catalyst. No sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this work, so nothing can be said about how either catalyst would behave with such a feed. It is also worth noting that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge, which makes a combined comparison of this kind less common in the literature. Overall, the comparison is based on the tests at 230 °C and 60 bar with the single feed composition described above, and the results reported here are the space-time yield, the pressure drop and the activity loss after 100 h for each of the two catalyst forms tested.
 The three quantities recorded for each catalyst were the same in both cases: space-time yield, pressure drop and activity loss after 100 h, all measured under the same test conditions of 230 °C, 60 bar and a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3.
