In this work, the rate constant k of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was measured at two temperatures, and k is expressed in s-1. At 300 K, the value of k was 0.021 s-1. At 340 K, the value of k was 1.398 s-1. The value at 340 K was computed from the Arrhenius relation with an activation energy of 89 kJ mol-1, so it is a derived value rather than a direct one. Each temperature was measured in triplicate, and the relative standard deviation of k was 4%. Looking at the two temperatures together, the rate constant at 340 K is much larger than the rate constant at 300 K, which covers a temperature difference of 40 K between the two points. Above 340 K, the solvent evaporated during sampling, so no rate constant is reported above 340 K. This means that the reported values cover the range from 300 K to 340 K, and there are no values beyond the upper end of that range in this work. In summary, the rate constant was 0.021 s-1 at 300 K and 1.398 s-1 at 340 K, with the second value derived from the Arrhenius relation using an activation energy of 89 kJ mol-1, each temperature measured in triplicate with a relative standard deviation of 4%, and no rate constant reported above 340 K because the solvent evaporated during sampling at higher temperatures. The two values, at 300 K and at 340 K, are the complete set of rate constants reported for this first-order liquid-phase reaction.
 The activation energy used in the Arrhenius calculation was 89 kJ mol-1, and that figure is part of the basis for the derived rate constant at 340 K, together with the temperature of 340 K itself and the relation used to compute the value from the stated inputs.
