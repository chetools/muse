This memo concerns the possible installation of maintenance sensors on the pumps at the plant, where 180 pumps are in operation. According to the maintenance records for the last 12 months, the plant has suffered 1206 h of unplanned downtime, and bearing failures are the largest single contributor, accounting for 43% of those downtime hours. A full sensor set covering all 180 pumps has been quoted at $442k installed, with a further cost of $55k per year. In a trial carried out by the vendor on a comparable unit, the sensors reduced downtime by 19%, although it should be noted that this is a vendor figure and it was not measured at this plant. A smaller first step is available in the form of a pilot on 19 pumps, which would cost $39k spread over three months. It is also worth recording that spare-part lead times are not changed by the sensors, so that side of the maintenance picture would remain as it is. This memo therefore recommends the three-month pilot. The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and will report on it before the March capital review, and the decision about whether to proceed with full installation will be held until the pilot results have been reported.
