This memo looks at whether the plant should invest in maintenance sensors for its pumps, and recommends that a three-month pilot should be carried out before any decision is made about a full installation. The plant currently runs 214 pumps. According to the maintenance records covering the last 12 months, there were 1004 h of unplanned downtime, and bearing failures were responsible for 48% of those downtime hours, which makes bearings the single largest contributor to the problem.

A sensor set covering all 214 pumps has been quoted at $461k installed, with a further cost of $63k per year on top of that. In a trial carried out by the vendor on a comparable unit, the sensors reduced downtime by 26%, although it is important to stress that this figure comes from the vendor and was not measured at this plant, so it may not translate directly to our situation.

A pilot covering 21 pumps would cost $38k over three months, which is a much smaller commitment than the full installation. It is also worth noting that spare-part lead times would not be changed by fitting the sensors. The recommendation is therefore to run the three-month pilot, which will be owned by the reliability engineering group, who will report back before the March capital review. The decision on whether to go ahead with the full installation will be held until the pilot results have been reported and considered.
