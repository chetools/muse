Over 45 days at 14 t of carbon dioxide per day, a pilot amine absorption unit on cement kiln gas captured 92% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas, at a steam demand of 3.7 gigajoules per tonne captured and a solvent loss of 1.6 kg per tonne captured. Capture, steam demand and solvent loss are stated per tonne of carbon dioxide captured or as a share of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas, over the same 45-day run, so the three figures describe one operating period at one throughput (14 t per day).

Steam demand sets the energy cost of the capture at the stated rate (3.7 gigajoules per tonne), and solvent loss sets the make-up rate the run required (1.6 kg per tonne). Both figures are averages over the 45 days; the passage of the run is not divided into shorter periods, and no figure is reported for any single day or week within the period.

Beyond 45 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent is untested. The capture figure (92%) and the solvent loss figure (1.6 kg per tonne) are therefore statements about the first 45 days of operation on kiln gas; solvent behaviour under longer exposure to kiln dust was not measured, and neither figure is reported for a longer campaign. The unit's tested performance is a 45-day record at 14 t of carbon dioxide per day on gas from a cement kiln.
