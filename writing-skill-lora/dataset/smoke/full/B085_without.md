This summary looks at a proposed heat-recovery retrofit at a food plant. The plant uses 2491 MWh of electricity per year in refrigeration, which is the load the retrofit would act on. The equipment vendor projects that the retrofit would save 12% of that use, which works out to a derived saving of 299 MWh per year at the vendor figure.

The capital cost of the retrofit is $430k. At an electricity price of $0.11 per kWh, the derived annual saving is $33k, and the derived simple payback is 13 years, which is a long payback for a capital project of this size. It is very important to note that the saving figure is a vendor projection and was not measured at this plant, so the 299 MWh per year, the $33k per year and the 13-year payback all depend on the vendor projection being correct. In addition, refrigeration load in the summer peak month was not modelled, so the annual figures do not rest on a model of the plant's highest-load month.

Given these points, the recommendation is a metered trial on one of the three refrigeration lines before any capital decision is taken. The plant engineer owns the trial. A metered trial would replace the vendor projection with the plant's own measured saving on one line, at a price of $0.11 per kWh applied to measured rather than projected energy, before the plant is asked to commit $430k of capital to the full retrofit across all three refrigeration lines.
In short, the plant uses 2491 MWh per year in refrigeration across three lines, the vendor projects a saving of 12% of that use, and the derived figures of 299 MWh per year, $33k per year and a payback of 13 years all rest on that projection, which has not been measured at this plant and does not rest on a model of the summer peak month either.
