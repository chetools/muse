Carbon dioxide and hydrogen react over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to give methanol, and space-time yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge. A structured catalyst and a pellet catalyst were compared at 280 °C and 40 bar, at a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3 and one feed composition. Space-time yield is defined as the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1).

The structured catalyst gave a higher space-time yield than the pellet catalyst (0.78 kg L-1 h-1 against 0.53 kg L-1 h-1) at a lower pressure drop (0.29 bar against 0.93 bar). Activity loss after 100 h was lower for the structured catalyst (5% against 14%). Each value rests on the same test conditions: 280 °C, 40 bar, a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, and one feed composition, for the reaction of carbon dioxide and hydrogen to methanol over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst. Pressure drop and space-time yield were measured for the same catalyst charge for each form, the structured catalyst at 0.29 bar and 0.78 kg L-1 h-1 and the pellet catalyst at 0.93 bar and 0.53 kg L-1 h-1.

No sulfur-bearing feed was tested. The comparison is limited to one feed composition at 280 °C and 40 bar with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, and to 100 h for the activity loss values (5% for the structured catalyst, 14% for the pellet catalyst). At the tested conditions, the structured catalyst holds the higher space-time yield (0.78 kg L-1 h-1), the lower pressure drop (0.29 bar), and the lower activity loss after 100 h (5%), against 0.53 kg L-1 h-1, 0.93 bar, and 14% for the pellet catalyst.

Space-time yield was reported with pressure drop for the same catalyst charge (0.78 and 0.53 kg L-1 h-1; 0.29 and 0.93 bar), at 280 °C and 40 bar with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3 and one feed composition, and activity loss after 100 h was 5% for the structured catalyst and 14% for the pellet catalyst, with no sulfur-bearing feed tested.
