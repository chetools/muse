This summary presents the delivery performance of a contract packaging firm that operates 2 plants and has revenue of $75 million per year. The evidence behind the summary consists of order records for 12 months, covering 15647 orders in total. Over the last two quarters, on-time delivery was 85%, which is some way below the target of 95% that the firm is aiming for. The cost of keeping up has also been increasing, because overtime cost $1.5 million last year, which is up 30% on the year before. The order backlog has grown as well, and it now stands at 3.9 weeks, compared with 2.2 weeks a year ago. When the late orders are looked at more closely, Plant 2 accounts for 56% of them, so more than half of the lateness is concentrated at that one plant. A scheduling system has been quoted for the firm: the price for all plants is $276k, while Plant 2 alone would cost $152k. An internal estimate, which is not a vendor figure, suggests that overtime at Plant 2 might be reduced by 15-20% in the first year. The recommendation under consideration is to install the system at Plant 2 first, and to hold the other plants until the results at Plant 2 have been reported. The operations director owns the scheduling programme. The lateness is concentrated at Plant 2, the overtime bill and the backlog have both risen over the year, and the phased option, beginning at Plant 2 for $152k, is the one under consideration.
