An amine absorption unit was tested at pilot scale using gas taken from a cement kiln, in order to evaluate how well the unit could capture carbon dioxide under realistic conditions. The tests were carried out over a period of 45 days, during which the unit treated gas at a rate corresponding to 14 t of carbon dioxide per day. Over this period, the unit captured 92% of the carbon dioxide present in the treated gas, which represents a very high level of capture performance for a pilot-scale system of this kind.

The operating requirements of the unit were also recorded during the tests. The steam demand was found to be 3.7 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, and the solvent loss amounted to 1.6 kg per tonne of carbon dioxide captured. These figures are important because they determine much of the running cost of the process and the amount of solvent that has to be made up over time.

It should be noted, however, that the tests only lasted for 45 days. Beyond that period, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent remains untested, so it is not yet known how the solvent would behave during longer operation on cement kiln gas, or whether dust carried over from the kiln would gradually affect the performance of the solvent and of the unit as a whole over an extended campaign of operation at this site.
