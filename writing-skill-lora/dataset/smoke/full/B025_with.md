An amine absorption unit at pilot scale captured 86% of the carbon dioxide in cement kiln gas over 45 days at 13 t of carbon dioxide per day, at a steam demand of 3.9 gigajoules per tonne captured and a solvent loss of 2.1 kg per tonne captured. Capture, steam demand and solvent loss are stated together for the same campaign and the same throughput. A capture rate of 86% leaves 14% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas uncaptured, at the tested rate of 13 t of carbon dioxide per day. Steam demand is the energy figure of the campaign: 3.9 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, charged against each tonne the unit captured. Solvent loss runs at 2.1 kg per tonne captured over the same campaign, a consumption of solvent stated on the same per-tonne basis as the steam demand. The three figures describe one operating point, 45 days on cement kiln gas at 13 t of carbon dioxide per day, and the figures are not separated from that point: capture at another throughput, steam demand over a longer campaign, and solvent loss beyond 45 days were not measured. Operation beyond 45 days is outside the tested duration. Within the 45 days, the unit held the stated capture rate on kiln gas. Beyond 45 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent is untested, and solvent behaviour over a longer exposure to kiln dust is not known from this campaign. The result stands at its tested scope: 86% capture at 13 t of carbon dioxide per day over 45 days, with steam demand and solvent loss as stated, and no measurement past the 45th day. Steam demand and solvent loss are each stated per tonne captured, on the capture the unit achieved over the campaign.
