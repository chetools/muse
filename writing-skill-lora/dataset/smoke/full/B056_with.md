At 230 °C and 60 bar, a structured copper-zinc oxide catalyst gave nearly twice the space-time yield of a pellet catalyst for methanol production from carbon dioxide and hydrogen (0.71 against 0.37 kg L-1 h-1), at about one fifth of the pressure drop (0.29 against 1.52 bar). Space-time yield is defined as the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1). Both catalysts were tested at a feed ratio (H2/CO2) of 3, and one feed composition was used for every test reported here.

Activity loss after 100 h separates the two forms in the same direction as yield and pressure drop: the structured catalyst lost 4% of its activity after 100 h, the pellet catalyst 16% after 100 h. The three measures are reported for the same catalyst charge in each case, a combination seldom reported for this reaction, and the comparison between the two forms rests on that shared basis: one temperature (230 °C), one pressure (60 bar), one feed ratio, and one run length (100 h) for the activity figures.

No sulfur-bearing feed was tested. Behaviour of either catalyst form with a sulfur-bearing feed is unmeasured in this work, and the ranking of the two forms at the stated conditions (higher yield, lower pressure drop, and lower activity loss for the structured catalyst) carries no statement about performance where sulfur is present in the feed. The comparison is also limited to the single feed composition tested; space-time yield, pressure drop, and activity loss at other feed compositions were not measured for either form. Within those bounds, the structured catalyst held the higher space-time yield (0.71 kg L-1 h-1) with the lower pressure drop (0.29 bar) and the smaller activity loss after 100 h (4%).
