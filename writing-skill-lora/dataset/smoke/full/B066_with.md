Calculate the half-life of a first-order reaction with rate constant k = 0.023 min-1 at 323 K, and the fraction remaining after 60 min. The rate constant k (min-1) is the measured quantity; concentration was measured every 5 min over 90 min at 323 K to obtain k = 0.023 min-1. For a first-order reaction, the half-life t1/2 (min) is defined by the relation t1/2 = ln(2) / k, where ln(2) is the natural logarithm of 2 (dimensionless). Substitution of k = 0.023 min-1 gives t1/2 = ln(2) / 0.023 = 30.1 min, thus the half-life is 30.1 min.

For a first-order reaction, the fraction remaining after time t (min) is exp(-k t), where exp is the exponential function and the product k t is dimensionless when k is in min-1 and t is in min. Substitution of k = 0.023 min-1 and t = 60 min gives exp(-0.023 x 60) = 0.25, thus the fraction remaining after 60 min is 0.25 (dimensionless). The fraction remaining of 0.25 rests on the same measured rate constant (k = 0.023 min-1 at 323 K) as the half-life of 30.1 min.

The relations t1/2 = ln(2) / k and exp(-k t) hold for a first-order reaction only. A reaction of another order follows a different decay law, and neither relation is applied to a reaction of another order. At 60 min, a time close to twice the half-life of 30.1 min, the fraction remaining of 0.25 states the decay over the stated interval at 323 K; the half-life of 30.1 min and the fraction remaining of 0.25 (dimensionless) are the two results of the calculation, both computed from the stated relations and from k = 0.023 min-1, measured every 5 min over 90 min.
