The rate constant k (s-1) of a first-order liquid-phase reaction was 0.077 s-1 at 300 K and 2.526 s-1 at 340 K, the second value derived rather than measured. Each temperature was measured in triplicate, and the relative standard deviation of k was 4%. At 300 K, k = 0.077 s-1 is a measured value, one of the triplicate set whose spread gives the stated relative standard deviation. At 340 K, k = 2.526 s-1 was computed from the Arrhenius relation with an activation energy of 74 kJ mol-1, as a derived value; the figure carries the standing of the Arrhenius relation and the stated activation energy, not of a direct measurement at 340 K. The two values stand 40 K apart and differ by a factor of about 33, computed from the stated pair (0.077 s-1 at 300 K, 2.526 s-1 at 340 K). Reproducibility is stated on the same basis for both temperatures: triplicate measurement, with a relative standard deviation of k of 4%. No rate constant is reported above 340 K. Above 340 K the solvent evaporated during sampling, and sampling is the operation the measurement depends on; a rate constant above 340 K would rest on samples the evaporation had already changed.
