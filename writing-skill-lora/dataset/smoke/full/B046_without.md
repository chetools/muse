Pervaporation is a membrane separation process in which a liquid feed is placed in contact with one side of a membrane, while a vacuum on the other side draws the component that permeates through the membrane as a vapour. In this work, two membranes were compared for a feed of 85 wt% isopropanol in water at 60 °C. Performance was described by the flux, which is the mass permeating per unit membrane area per hour (kg m-2 h-1), and by the separation factor, which is defined as the water-to-alcohol ratio in the permeate divided by the same ratio in the feed, and is dimensionless.

The polymer membrane gave a flux of 2.1 kg m-2 h-1 and a separation factor of 240, although its flux fell by 13% over 500 h. The zeolite membrane gave a lower flux of 1.3 kg m-2 h-1 but a higher separation factor of 560, and its flux was unchanged over 500 h within measurement error.

Only one feed composition was tested, and no test was run for longer than 500 h. It should also be noted that flux, selectivity and stability are seldom reported together for the same membrane, so a comparison in which all three are available for both membranes, as they are here for the feed of 85 wt% isopropanol in water at 60 °C, is relatively unusual.
