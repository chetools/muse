An amine absorption unit has been tested at pilot scale on gas coming from a cement kiln, and this passage reports how the unit performed during the test campaign. The campaign lasted for 45 days, and during that time the unit treated gas at a rate corresponding to 13 t of carbon dioxide per day. Over the campaign, the unit succeeded in capturing 86% of the carbon dioxide that was present in the treated gas, so the large majority of the carbon dioxide entering the unit was removed from the gas stream. The energy requirement of the process was recorded as a steam demand of 3.9 gigajoules for every tonne of carbon dioxide captured, which makes steam the figure to watch when the operating cost of the unit is considered. Solvent was also consumed during operation, and the solvent loss amounted to 2.1 kg per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, so the solvent has to be made up continuously as the unit runs. These three results, the capture rate of 86%, the steam demand of 3.9 gigajoules per tonne captured and the solvent loss of 2.1 kg per tonne captured, describe the performance of the unit at the tested throughput of 13 t of carbon dioxide per day over the full 45 days of the campaign. One question remains open at the end of the work. Beyond 45 days, the effect that kiln dust has on the solvent is untested, because the campaign did not run for longer than 45 days, and so it is not yet known how the solvent would be affected by kiln dust over a longer period of operation on cement kiln gas. The campaign therefore gives a complete set of performance figures for the 45 days it covers, at 13 t of carbon dioxide per day, and no figures at all for any longer period of operation on the kiln gas.
