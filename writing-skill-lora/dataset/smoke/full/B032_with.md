Calculate the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant water film: the result is J = 6.50x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The diffusive flux J (mol m-2 s-1) is computed from the diffusivity D (m2 s-1) and the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C denotes concentration (mol m-3) and x denotes position (m). Fick's first law is J = -D dC/dx; the negative sign states that diffusion runs from high concentration to low concentration. Oxygen in water has D = 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1. Concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film 0.8 mm thick; thus dC/dx = -250 mol m-4. Substitution of D and dC/dx in Fick's first law gives J = -(2.6 x 10-9) x (-250), and the calculation gives J = 6.50x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1. The sign of the result is positive because the gradient is negative: concentration decreases in the direction of increasing x, and the flux runs in that same direction, from the high-concentration side of the film to the low-concentration side. Fick's first law as stated holds for a stagnant medium; a moving medium adds convection, which the equation does not include. The check on the magnitude is the product itself: a diffusivity of 2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1 multiplied by a gradient of 250 mol m-4 in magnitude gives 6.50x 10-7 in the flux units stated, mol m-2 s-1. Oxygen crosses the stagnant water film (0.8 mm, a fall of 0.2 mol m-3) at a diffusive flux of 6.50x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1.
 The gradient carries the film dimensions into the result: the fall (0.2 mol m-3) divided by the film thickness (0.8 mm) fixes dC/dx at -250 mol m-4, and Fick's first law then combines that gradient with the diffusivity (2.6 x 10-9 m2 s-1) in a single multiplication, with no further quantity entering the calculation of J.
