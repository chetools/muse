Carbon dioxide and hydrogen can be reacted over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst to produce methanol, and in this study two different forms of the catalyst were compared under the same conditions. The performance of each catalyst was described using the space-time yield, which is defined as the mass of product formed per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1), together with the pressure drop across the catalyst and the loss of activity over time.

The tests were carried out at 280 °C and 40 bar, with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, and only one feed composition was used throughout the work. The washcoated monolith catalyst gave a space-time yield of 1.05 kg L-1 h-1, with a pressure drop of only 0.1 bar, and it lost 9% of its activity after 100 h. The extruded pellet catalyst, by comparison, gave a considerably lower space-time yield of 0.34 kg L-1 h-1, together with a much higher pressure drop of 1.17 bar, and it lost 18% of its activity after 100 h.

Overall, the monolith performed better than the pellets on all three measures reported here. It is also worth noting that no sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this work, so nothing can be said about how either catalyst would behave with such a feed. Finally, it should be pointed out that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge in the literature, which makes direct comparisons of this kind relatively uncommon, even though the two quantities were both measured here for the same charge at 280 °C, 40 bar and a feed ratio of 3.
