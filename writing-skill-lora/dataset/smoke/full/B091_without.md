An amine absorption unit was tested at pilot scale on gas from a cement kiln in order to evaluate its performance for carbon dioxide capture under realistic operating conditions. The pilot campaign was carried out over a period of 45 days, during which the unit treated gas at a rate corresponding to 9 t of carbon dioxide per day. During the campaign, the unit captured 88% of the carbon dioxide in the treated gas, which represents a strong level of performance for a pilot-scale system of this kind, and this capture level is the main headline result of the work.

The energy demand of the process was also measured. The steam demand was found to be 3.2 gigajoules per tonne of carbon dioxide captured, and the solvent loss was 2 kg per tonne of carbon dioxide captured. These figures are important because steam demand and solvent loss are two of the main operating costs associated with amine-based capture, and they help to determine whether the process can be economically attractive at a larger scale.

It should be noted, however, that the campaign lasted only 45 days. Beyond 45 days, the effect of kiln dust on the solvent is untested, and so it is not yet known how the solvent will behave over longer periods of operation on cement kiln gas. Further work over a longer period would therefore be needed in order to confirm the durability of the solvent and the stability of the capture performance reported here, and to establish whether the steam demand and solvent loss remain at the levels measured during this 45-day campaign.
