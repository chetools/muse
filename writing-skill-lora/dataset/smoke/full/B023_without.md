Carbon dioxide and hydrogen can be reacted over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst in order to produce formaldehyde, and this study compares two different forms of the catalyst for this reaction. The performance measure used throughout is the space-time yield, which is defined as the mass of product obtained per unit catalyst volume per hour, expressed in kg L-1 h-1. All of the tests were carried out at 280 °C and 40 bar, with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3, and with a single feed composition. The first catalyst is a washcoated monolith, and it achieved a space-time yield of 1.09 kg L-1 h-1 with a pressure drop of only 0.1 bar, losing 8% of its activity after 100 h of operation. The second catalyst is an extruded pellet, and it gave a considerably lower space-time yield of 0.52 kg L-1 h-1, together with a much higher pressure drop of 1.4 bar, and it lost 19% of its activity after 100 h. It should be mentioned that no sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this work, so nothing can be said about how these catalysts would behave in the presence of sulfur. It is also interesting to note that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported together for the same catalyst charge in the literature, which makes a direct comparison of this kind relatively unusual. On the figures reported, the monolith gave the higher space-time yield, the lower pressure drop and the smaller activity loss of the two catalysts at 280 °C and 40 bar with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3.
