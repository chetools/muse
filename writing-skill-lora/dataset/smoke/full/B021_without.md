This worked example calculates the diffusive flux of oxygen through a stagnant film of water. The diffusive flux, J, which is measured in mol m-2 s-1, is obtained from the diffusivity D, measured in m2 s-1, together with the concentration gradient dC/dx, where C is the concentration in mol m-3 and x is the position in m. The relationship used is Fick's first law, J = -D dC/dx. The negative sign in the equation is there because diffusion always runs from a region of high concentration towards a region of low concentration. For oxygen in water, the diffusivity is 2.1 x 10-9 m2 s-1. In this example the concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across a film that is 0.8 mm thick, which means the concentration gradient is dC/dx = -250 mol m-4. Putting these numbers into Fick's first law gives J = 5.25x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1, which is the answer to the problem. Finally, it should be remembered that Fick's first law in the form given here applies to a stagnant medium. If the medium were moving, convection would also contribute to the transport of oxygen, and the equation as written does not include that contribution, so it would no longer tell the whole story. To recap the numbers used in the calculation, the diffusivity of oxygen in water is 2.1 x 10-9 m2 s-1, the concentration falls by 0.2 mol m-3 across the 0.8 mm film, the gradient is -250 mol m-4, and the resulting flux is 5.25x 10-7 mol m-2 s-1.
