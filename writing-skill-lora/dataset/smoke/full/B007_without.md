This executive summary presents the delivery performance of a contract packaging firm that operates 4 plants and generates revenue of $89 million per year. The analysis is based on order records covering a period of 12 months, comprising a total of 15392 orders, which provides a comprehensive evidence base for the findings presented below. Over the last two quarters, the company's on-time delivery performance stood at 85%, which is significantly below the target of 95% that the company aims to achieve. At the same time, overtime cost reached $2.6 million last year, representing an increase of 27% on the year before, and the order backlog has grown to 2.9 weeks, compared with 1.7 weeks a year ago. A closer look at the data reveals that Plant 2 is the main source of the problem, accounting for 63% of all late orders across the company. In order to address this issue, a scheduling system has been considered, with a quoted cost of $333k for installation across all plants, or $122k for Plant 2 alone. An internal estimate, which is not a vendor figure, suggests that installing the system at Plant 2 could reduce overtime at that plant by 15-20% in the first year. Based on these findings, the recommendation under consideration is to install the scheduling system at Plant 2 first, while holding the other plants until the results from Plant 2 have been reported. The operations director owns the scheduling programme and will be responsible for overseeing its implementation. In conclusion, the delivery problem is concentrated at Plant 2, and a phased installation beginning at that plant offers a targeted way to improve on-time delivery from its current level of 85% towards the target of 95%.
