In this worked example, we will calculate the Reynolds number for water flowing in a pipe and use it to determine the flow regime. The Reynolds number is a very important dimensionless quantity in fluid mechanics, and it is calculated from the density of the fluid, the mean velocity, the pipe diameter, and the dynamic viscosity, using the formula Re = rho v D / mu, where rho is the density (kg m-3), v is the mean velocity (m s-1), D is the pipe diameter (m), and mu is the dynamic viscosity (Pa s).

Let us now insert the values for water at 293 K. The density of the water is rho = 998 kg m-3, and its dynamic viscosity is mu = 1.00 x 10-3 Pa s. The pipe diameter is D = 0.025 m, and the mean velocity of the flow is v = 2.1 m s-1.

Putting these numbers into the formula gives Re = 998 x 2.1 x 0.025 / 0.001, which works out to Re = 52400.

To interpret this result, we compare it with the usual thresholds. The flow is laminar below Re = 2300 and turbulent above Re = 4000, while between these two values the regime is transitional and cannot be assigned by the number alone. Since our calculated value of 52400 is well above 4000, we can conclude that the flow in the pipe is turbulent.
