Recommendation: run a three-month pilot of maintenance sensors on 23 pumps at $38k before any decision on full installation. The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and reports before the March capital review; the decision on full installation is held until the pilot results are reported.

The plant runs 180 pumps. Maintenance records for 12 months show 947 h of unplanned downtime, of which bearing failures account for 44% of the downtime hours. A sensor set for all 180 pumps is quoted at $392k installed, plus $66k per year. A pilot on 23 pumps costs $38k over three months, against that full-installation quote.

Evidence for a benefit at this plant is indirect. In a vendor trial on a comparable unit, the sensors cut downtime by 23%; the figure is a vendor figure and was not measured at this plant. No downtime reduction has been measured on any of the 180 pumps. Spare-part lead times are unchanged by the sensors, so a pilot result would speak to downtime detection and not to spare-part supply.

Next step: the reliability engineering group runs the pilot on 23 pumps over three months and reports before the March capital review, at which the full-installation decision, on the quote of $392k installed plus $66k per year, is taken on pilot results.
