Methanol is produced from carbon dioxide and hydrogen over a copper-zinc oxide catalyst, and catalyst form changes the yield obtained from a given catalyst volume. Yield and pressure drop are seldom reported for the same catalyst charge, so a comparison that reports both, for both forms, is missing. Two forms, structured and pellet, were compared at 230 °C and 60 bar with a feed ratio H2/CO2 = 3 and one feed composition. Space-time yield is defined as the mass of product per unit catalyst volume per hour (kg L-1 h-1). Pressure drop (bar) and activity loss after 100 h (%) were recorded for the same charges.

At these conditions the structured catalyst gave the higher space-time yield (0.74 kg L-1 h-1 against 0.36 kg L-1 h-1 for the pellet catalyst). Pressure drop was lower for the structured catalyst (0.25 bar against 1.37 bar). Activity loss after 100 h was also lower for the structured catalyst (7% against 12%). The three measures agree in direction: at the stated temperature, pressure and feed ratio, the structured form produced more methanol per unit catalyst volume per hour, at a lower pressure drop, with less activity lost over 100 h. Each figure is a single-charge figure at one feed composition, and the comparison holds at those conditions.

The comparison is limited to the conditions tested. No sulfur-bearing feed was tested, and catalyst behaviour with a sulfur-bearing feed is not reported. Activity is reported at 100 h and not beyond that time, and the feed composition was not varied. Within that scope, the structured catalyst gave a space-time yield of 0.74 kg L-1 h-1 at a pressure drop of 0.25 bar with an activity loss of 7% after 100 h, against 0.36 kg L-1 h-1, 1.37 bar and 12% for the pellet catalyst.
