This memo considers whether the plant should invest in maintenance sensors for its pumps, and it recommends a cautious first step. The plant operates 180 pumps, and maintenance records for the past 12 months show a total of 947 h of unplanned downtime, which is a significant burden on operations. Bearing failures are the single largest contributor, accounting for 44% of the downtime hours, and this makes the pumps a natural target for monitoring.

A sensor set covering all 180 pumps has been quoted at $392k installed, plus a further $66k per year. This is a substantial commitment, so the supporting evidence deserves careful attention. In a vendor trial carried out on a comparable unit, the sensors were reported to cut downtime by 23%. However, it is important to bear in mind that this figure comes from the vendor and was not measured at this plant, so it may not transfer directly to our own conditions.

As a lower-cost alternative, a pilot on 23 pumps would cost $38k over three months. It should also be noted that spare-part lead times are unchanged by the sensors. On balance, the memo recommends running the three-month pilot first. The reliability engineering group owns the pilot and will report before the March capital review, and the decision on full installation will be held until the pilot results have been reported.
