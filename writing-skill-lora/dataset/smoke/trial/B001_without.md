The conversion of carbon dioxide and hydrogen into methanol is an important reaction, and the choice of catalyst plays a crucial role in determining how well it performs. In this study, two different catalyst forms are compared: a structured catalyst and a conventional pellet catalyst, both based on copper-zinc oxide. The reaction was carried out under well-defined conditions, namely a temperature of 230 °C, a pressure of 60 bar, and a feed ratio of H2/CO2 = 3, with a single feed composition being used throughout the work.

To evaluate the performance of the catalysts, the space-time yield was used as a key metric. The space-time yield is defined as the mass of product obtained per unit catalyst volume per hour, expressed in kg L-1 h-1. In addition to the yield, the pressure drop across the catalyst and the loss of activity over time were also measured, providing a more complete picture of catalyst behaviour.

The results show that the structured catalyst performed considerably better than the pellet catalyst. The structured catalyst achieved a space-time yield of 0.74 kg L-1 h-1, whereas the pellet catalyst reached only 0.36 kg L-1 h-1, meaning that the structured form produced roughly twice as much methanol under the same conditions. The pressure drop was also much lower for the structured catalyst, at 0.25 bar, compared with 1.37 bar for the pellet catalyst. Furthermore, the structured catalyst proved to be more stable, losing 7% of its activity after 100 h, while the pellet catalyst lost 12% over the same period.

It is worth noting that yield and pressure drop are seldom reported together for the same catalyst charge, which makes this combined comparison particularly valuable. It should also be noted that no sulfur-bearing feed was tested in this work, so the behaviour of the catalysts in the presence of sulfur remains an open question for future investigation.
