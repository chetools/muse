McCabe–Thiele and Ponchon–Savarit

Binary distillation design by the two classical graphical methods, computed side by side on one specification. The real systems use the ChemCAD NRTL parameters and the ChemCAD Latent Heat enthalpy model; the synthetic preset holds the relative volatility fixed. All calculation runs in Python (Pyodide) in the browser; the first display is the precomputed default run, computed when this page was built.

Fig. 2 | Stage construction on the enthalpy–composition diagram. h is the saturated-liquid molar enthalpy and H the saturated-vapour molar enthalpy, in kJ/mol; tie lines join the two phases of a stage, and the rays run to the rectifying difference point (pole) above the diagram and the stripping difference point below it.

Per-stage results (Ponchon–Savarit internal flows)

Stagex (liquid)y (vapour) T (°C)L (kmol/s)V (kmol/s)

Stage 1 is the top stage. L and V are the liquid and vapour molar flow rates leaving the stage, in kmol/s. The last row is the final stepped stage: its liquid composition overshoots xB because the reported stage count is fractional, so the last stage is counted in part; the bottoms product composition is the specified xB, and the liquid flow on that row is the bottoms flow B. The section flow relations (V − L = D in the rectifying section, L − V = B in the stripping section, with D the distillate flow rate) pair streams crossing one cut through the column — the vapour leaving a stage with the liquid leaving the stage above it — not the two flows in a single row of this table. Under constant molal overflow L and V would be constant within each section; their variation here is what the McCabe–Thiele construction neglects.
