Report (transformed)

Carbon dioxide absorption into aqueous monoethanolamine in a bench packed column: Pall rings and structured packing compared

Abstract

Post-combustion capture of carbon dioxide (CO2) by absorption into aqueous monoethanolamine (MEA) places the absorber packing at the centre of both separation performance and gas-side energy demand. A packing comparison that reports capture, mass transfer and pressure drop for the same charge under the same conditions appears to be the missing basis for bench-scale screening. Stainless-steel Pall rings and a stainless-steel gauze structured packing were compared in a bench column at one gas composition and one solvent concentration. At the reference condition the structured packing gave the higher capture and the higher overall volumetric mass-transfer coefficient at the lower pressure drop (capture fraction 0.91 against 0.78; coefficient 0.85 against 0.58 mol m-3 s−1 kPa-1; pressure drop 350 against 920 Pa m−1), and rich solvent loading was higher (0.27 against 0.25 mol of CO2 per mol of MEA). Bench screening ranks the two packings on all three quantities before pilot work, and the ranking at the reference condition favours the structured packing.

1 Introduction

Absorption into aqueous MEA is an established route for post-combustion CO2 capture from gas-fired and cement exhaust. A packing provides the gas–liquid contact inside the absorber, and packing choice sets three quantities together: the fraction of CO2 captured, the mass-transfer rate that fixes column size, and the pressure drop that fixes blower power. Ratings for structured packing rest largely on measurements made under differing conditions, and a comparison of a random packing and a structured packing in one column, at one gas composition and one solvent concentration, with all three quantities measured for each packing, appears to be missing. The aim of the measurements reported here is that comparison: a bench column was charged in turn with each packing, capture fraction, the overall volumetric mass-transfer coefficient and wet pressure drop were measured over a range of gas and liquid rates, and one extended run of 120 h was made on the structured packing.

2 Methods

The column internal diameter D (m) was 0.075 m and the packed height Z (m) was 1.20 m; the column was glass, and gas and liquid flowed countercurrently. The Pall rings were stainless steel, of nominal size 0.016 m, and the structured packing was stainless-steel gauze, of nominal specific surface area 500 m2 m−3. Fresh solvent was aqueous MEA at a mass fraction of 0.30, with a lean loading of 0.18 mol of CO2 per mol of MEA. Feed gas was CO2 in nitrogen at an inlet CO2 mole fraction yin (dimensionless) of 0.12, with no oxygen, sulfur dioxide or particulate matter present. At the reference condition the gas superficial velocity uG (m s-1) was 0.85 m s−1, the liquid superficial velocity uL (m s-1) was 0.0085 m s−1, inlet gas and lean solvent temperatures were 40 °C, and pressure was 101 kPa. Twenty-four runs per packing covered uG from 0.55 to 1.15 m s−1 and uL from 0.004 to 0.013 m s−1. Inlet and outlet CO2 mole fractions were measured by infrared analysis (range 0 to 0.20, stated accuracy 0.003), liquid loading by acid titration, temperature at five positions along the column, and pressure drop by a differential pressure transducer. Each condition was measured three times (n = 3); steady state was taken as an outlet CO2 mole fraction varying by less than 0.002 over 600 s. Capture fraction is defined as the inlet CO2 molar flow minus the outlet CO2 molar flow, divided by the inlet CO2 molar flow. Eq. (1) relates the molar absorption rate of CO2 N (mol s-1), the column cross-sectional area A (m2), computed from D, the packed height Z (m), the log-mean CO2 partial-pressure difference over the packed height Δplm (kPa), the overall gas-phase mass-transfer coefficient KG (mol m-2 s−1 kPa-1) and the effective interfacial area per unit packed volume a (m2 m-3); the product KGa (mol m-3 s−1 kPa-1) was computed from Eq. (1) for each run, and KG and a were not measured separately.

KGa = N / (AZΔplm)(1)

Analyser zero was checked daily against drift, and one run was discarded and repeated after a leak was found in a gas sampling line.

3 Results

At the reference condition, capture fraction was higher for the structured packing than for the Pall rings (0.91, standard deviation (SD) 0.02, against 0.78, SD 0.03; n = 3 for each value), and rich loading was higher (0.27 against 0.25 mol of CO2 per mol of MEA). Column temperature peaked part-way down the packing for both charges, at 58 °C at about 0.35 of the packed height from the top for the structured packing and at 52 °C for the Pall rings. Values of KGa at the reference condition were 0.85 mol m-3 s−1 kPa-1 (SD 0.05) for the structured packing and 0.58 mol m-3 s−1 kPa-1 (SD 0.04) for the Pall rings. Wet pressure drop per unit packed height at the reference condition was 350 Pa m−1 (SD 30 Pa m−1) for the structured packing and 920 Pa m−1 (SD 80 Pa m−1) for the Pall rings. Capture fraction rose with liquid rate for both packings, and the difference between the packings narrowed at the highest liquid rate tested, uL = 0.013 m s−1 (structured packing 0.93; Pall rings 0.88). As gas rate rose, capture fraction fell, and at uG = 1.15 m s−1 capture was 0.82 for the structured packing against 0.64 for the Pall rings. Pressure drop across the Pall rings rose sharply above uL = 0.011 m s−1 at uG = 1.15 m s−1, the loading behaviour that precedes flooding; no sharp rise was detected for the structured packing over the tested range, up to uG = 1.15 m s−1 and uL = 0.013 m s−1. Repeatability of the structured packing was poorer at the lowest liquid rate, uL = 0.004 m s−1, where capture fraction had an SD of 0.05 against 0.02 at the reference condition. In the extended run of 120 h at the reference condition, capture fraction for the structured packing fell from 0.91 to 0.87, the solvent darkened in colour, and solvent viscosity, measured at 40 °C, rose by 8%.

4 Discussion

Pressure drop separates the two packings by a wider margin than capture does, and pressure drop is the quantity that a higher liquid rate cannot correct: capture for the Pall rings approached capture for the structured packing at the highest liquid rate (0.88 against 0.93), while wet pressure drop at the reference condition remained higher for the Pall rings by a factor of about 2.6 (920 against 350 Pa m−1), and blower power follows pressure drop. Packing cost runs the other way: the structured packing costs more per unit packed volume than the Pall rings, and the difference was not priced in this work. Higher rich loading for the structured packing (0.27 against 0.25 mol of CO2 per mol of MEA) corresponds to a lower regeneration energy, estimated from the rich loading with a steam correlation at 3.6 GJ per tonne of CO2 for the structured packing against 4.1 GJ per tonne for the Pall rings; regeneration was not operated in this work, and the two energy values are estimates, not measurements. Incomplete wetting, wall flow, or the liquid distributor shared by both packings is a possible explanation of the poorer repeatability of the structured packing at the lowest liquid rate; liquid distribution was not measured, and the explanation is untested. Solvent change over 120 h (capture fraction 0.91 to 0.87; viscosity rise of 8%; solvent darkening) is consistent with degradation or accumulation in the solvent; MEA loss was not quantified and no degradation product was analysed, so no specific cause is assigned, and 120 h is short against the months of industrial solvent service. Wall effects in a column of diameter 0.075 m are larger than in an industrial column; the values reported here rank the packings at bench scale and do not transfer directly to industrial diameters.

Conclusions

At the tested gas composition (inlet CO2 mole fraction 0.12), the tested solvent concentration (MEA mass fraction 0.30) and bench scale, the structured packing gave a higher capture fraction (0.91 against 0.78), a higher overall volumetric mass-transfer coefficient (0.85 against 0.58 mol m-3 s−1 kPa-1), a lower wet pressure drop (350 against 920 Pa m−1) and a higher rich loading (0.27 against 0.25 mol of CO2 per mol of MEA) than the Pall rings. The comparison is limited to one gas composition, one solvent concentration and one column diameter, with a gas that contained no oxygen, sulfur dioxide or particulate matter; solvent degradation under real flue gas would probably be greater than the degradation observed with the tested gas. Regeneration energy was estimated, not measured, and packing cost was not measured, so no ranking on total cost is made.

How a transformation with this skill is done

Summary

The source text is frozen first, and nothing is added to it. The text is then split into units, one claim with its support in each, and the units are ranked and ordered by dependency, so that definitions and method stand before the results that rest on them. Duplicates are merged with every qualifier kept, and material of low rank is kept at the end of its passage rather than cut. The opening and the closing are sized to the body. The ordered material is then written under the core rules of the package; a full rewrite, where a change of document form is requested, is made under the whole package: a fixed order for the abstract and the opening of a STEM document, symbols and terms defined at first use, values stated with their conditions and comparators, limits stated inside the claims they bound, one name for each object, and a closing held at the strength of the evidence. A revision pass checks the draft against the source claim by claim, and a document of consequence is then checked by a reader who did not write it. What the source does not settle is reported, not resolved by invention. The case record for the two columns above — units, ranks, moves and merges — stands in the next section; the wording changes stand in the section after it; the points this transformation could not settle stand in the section after that.

Details

  1. Load by document type. The core rules load for all writing under the package. Each reference file loads only when its condition holds. For this transformation the loaded files were the core; the structure reference, because a reorganization was requested and the body exceeds about 300 words; the textbook and equations references, because the target document carries detail sentences and a displayed equation; and the revision and check reference, with its examples file, for the check passes. The figures reference did not load: the target document has no figure or table. The business reference did not load: the target is a STEM report, not a business document.
  2. Freeze the source. The notes were fixed as the only source of facts before any drafting. No fact, consequence or causal link may be stated that the source does not state, and a derived figure is stated as derived, with its derivation. Every later check runs against the frozen source.
  3. Inventory the units. The source is split into units, each one claim with the sentences that support it, and each unit is tagged by role — thesis, claim, evidence, definition, example, limit or aside — with its place in the source recorded. The inventory is a working record; it does not enter the document.
  4. State the thesis from the source. The principal claim is stated in one sentence taken from the source. Where the source states no principal claim, or states two that compete, that finding is reported and no thesis is invented. The record for this case, including the two competing conclusions in the notes, stands under “How the organisation was done”.
  5. Rank the units. Each unit is ranked principal, supporting or peripheral on three grounds, in order: whether the thesis requires it, how many other units depend on it, and the weight of its evidence. A rank that rests on judgement rather than on a dependency is marked as a judgement in the record.
  6. List the dependencies and order the body. A unit requires another when it uses a term, symbol, premise, method or result that the other unit introduces; every such requirement is listed as a pair. The body order is a topological order of those pairs, with rank as the tie-break: no unit stands before a unit it requires, and among units free to move the higher rank stands first. Units on one topic stand together in one passage, unless a dependency splits them. Implementation detail, where a document has any, stands last.
  7. Merge duplicates; place peripheral units. Two units stating the same claim are merged into one, and the merged unit keeps every qualifier, number and condition of both. A peripheral unit is kept, moved to the end of its passage or into the closing limits, and listed in the record; it is cut only on request.
  8. Size the opening and the closing. Opening and closing scale with the word count of the body: one sentence each below about 300 words; one paragraph each from about 300 to about 1,500 words; up to three paragraphs, or a headed section, above that size. Each stays near 5–15% of the body. Where a document type fixes the content of the opening and the closing — the abstract order and the opening order for STEM text, in this case — the type frame governs the content and the size bands govern the length. The closing restates the principal claim at its tested scope, with its limits named, and adds no new claim.
  9. Write under the core rules. Where only an order was requested, body sentences keep their source wording and edits are limited to the opening, the closing, transitions and broken joins. A change of document form, as here from notebook fragments to a report, is a full rewrite, made only on request and then under the whole package. The rules that carry most of the change in this case are: formal, impersonal register and the field's own terms; the STEM opening order (setting, established knowledge, gap, aim, method preview) and abstract order (setting, aim, method, principal result, interpretation, implication, with no limitation in the abstract); every symbol defined at or before first use, with its unit where a unit applies, and every abbreviation expanded at first use (standard notation — element symbols, formulae of common species and SI unit symbols — takes no definition); one name for each object, restated rather than replaced by a synonym; each number bundled in its sentence with its qualifier, comparator and condition; the limit inside the claim sentence, before the number it qualifies, with validity stated for the law or model as subject; a null result stated as a detection statement over a stated range; no inference marker in a frame sentence; and interpretation placed next to the result it interprets.
  10. Run the revision pass. The writer revises the draft against the package in a fixed order: loading (the document type named, and exactly the references whose conditions hold loaded); fidelity to the source; notation; sentence openings and lengths; claims and limits; the document-type completion checks; the AI-writing patterns and the house word list, judged by density rather than by single occurrences; a mechanical scan by the package's lint tool, with each flag resolved or kept with a reason; a second pass on the revision, including a claim-by-claim fidelity diff; and a remove-10% pass that cuts any element whose removal loses no information.
  11. Run the independent check. A document of consequence is checked by a checker that did not write it, holding the frozen source, the draft and the package, and never the writer's reasoning. The checker works under eight heads — traceability, recomputation, literal truth, cross-part agreement, completeness against the brief, notation, citations, and regression after corrections — and returns findings without editing the draft. Each finding is adjudicated against the source before any correction is applied, and the corrected passages are checked again. The review record for this page, including the two balance failures the checkers found in the first draft of the dataset, stands in the covering message that delivered the page.
  12. Report what the source does not settle. A transformation under the package does not invent citations, data, figures or report front matter, and does not silently settle an ambiguity in the source. Each unsettled point is reported in the open, in this case in the “Uncertainties in the transformation” section, with the item a complete report would need.

How the writing was improved

  • One name for each object. The notes cycle between the full names and short forms — “rings”, “the rings”, a bare “structured” — and the structured packing enters as “gauze type”. The report uses “structured packing” and “Pall rings” throughout, so a value can be traced to its packing at every occurrence.
  • Values bundled with their conditions. The notes state “capture 91%!!” in one entry, its standard deviation in the same entry, its repeat count in the final entry, and its conditions in the first entry. The report states each principal value once per passage with its comparator in the same sentence, and with its standard deviation in the same sentence where the notes record one (the rich loading comparison carries no standard deviation in either text); the repeat count is stated once for all conditions in Methods and again with the capture values, and each condition is stated in the sentence or in the Methods statement that governs it.
  • One unit form for each quantity. Percentages, millimetres, minutes and “points” in the notes become fractions, metres, seconds and the same fraction form in the report (91% to 0.91; 75 mm to 0.075 m; 10 min to 600 s; pressure drop in Pa m−1 throughout).
  • Symbols defined at first use. The notes use KGa for a full entry before defining it, and define the area term afterwards in words (“the effective area bit”). In the report each symbol is defined with its unit at first use in the body, and the coefficient is defined by Eq. (1) in Methods, ahead of the Results values that rest on it; the Abstract states the principal values in summary form.
  • Claims held at the evidence. “Breakthrough”, “HUGE difference” and “outperforms on every metric” are replaced by the four measured comparisons, each with its values. A grading word stands only where its numbers stand in the same sentence.
  • Absence stated as a detection result. “Structured didn't do that” becomes “no sharp rise was detected for the structured packing over the tested range”, with the range stated; the notes' own caution, that the packing was never pushed to flooding, is carried by that form.
  • Inference separated from measurement. The wetting guess stays in the report as a possible explanation, in the same passage as the statement that liquid distribution was not measured and the explanation is untested. The distributor, named in the notes as an alternative cause, is named with it.
  • Derived values labelled where they stand. The notes ask for the energy estimate to be marked “in big letters”. The report marks it in the sentence itself: the values “are estimates, not measurements”, and regeneration is stated as not operated.
  • Limits placed inside the closing claim. Limits scattered through the notes (one gas composition, no oxygen or dust, one diameter, cost unmeasured) are collected in Conclusions, and the closing claim opens with its scope. The abstract states the tested scope as method and carries no limitation statement, in the abstract order for a report; the limits stand in the closing.
  • Register and sentence form. Diary voice, dates, abbreviations of speech (“tbh”), exclamation marks and rhetorical questions are removed. Sentence openings vary across each passage, and no passage opens successive sentences with the same word.
  • Length. The report is longer than the notes (1,837 against 1,100 words). The added words are definitions at first use, conditions bundled with values, the abstract, and the closing restatement at scope. Shortening was applied to wording, not to qualifications, ranges or evidence.

Uncertainties in the transformation

  2. Source of the regeneration-energy estimate. The notes cite “that steam correlation from the lit” and record that the reference was never written down. How this skill was created: key components

The skill is a package of writing rules for scientific, engineering, instructional and business text. Its components are a set of measured corpora, a coding method that turned the corpora into rules, a tiered package that loads the rules by document type, a check system, and an evidence file that records what each rule rests on and where its limits stand. The components are described here; the succession of versions is not.

Measured corpora

The rules were coded from documents, not taken from advice literature. Six corpora were measured. STEM papers: 37 papers from Nature and Science at top citation rank, 2015–2024, with an 8-paper mid-cited comparison set from the same journals, drawn in the pilot design as the check that the coded patterns are journal style rather than an artefact of citation rank; flow measures (sentence openings, connectives, sentence lengths) were computed on 34 of the 37 papers. Instructional writing: 22 items from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and DeepMind (tutorials, explainers and documentation). A distinctive-patterns pass: 26 further Nature and Science papers and 16 further instructional and research-blog items, coded for low-frequency constructions that state a result, a scope condition and an interpretation in one move; the rules on claims, limits and captions rest in part on this pass. Equations: an oversample of 10 equation-bearing papers, with 19 coded equations and 103 symbol groups. Business and consulting documents: 22 documents (long-form articles from Harvard Business Review and MIT Sloan Management Review; documents from McKinsey & Company, the McKinsey Global Institute, Boston Consulting Group and Bain & Company; five chief executive officer letters; and a 4-document comparison set from the mid-readership business press). Textbooks: a contrast study of five publicly available engineering textbooks, with detailed coding of three canonical chemical-engineering textbooks and stratified samples of 160 coded passages and 30 rewrite comparisons; a textbook rule stands only where the coded books agree.

Coding method

Each corpus was read against a fixed rubric, constructions were counted, and a rule states a construction that the coded documents use at the stated scale. The pilot design's rubric covered abstract moves, opening moves, results text and per-figure coding including caption self-containment, per-equation coding including the fraction of symbols defined at or before first use, closing moves, paragraph connection, and computed surface measures with word lists for hedging and certainty fixed in advance. Two codebook anchors for the business corpus (Barbara Minto, The Pyramid Principle; Gene Zelazny, Say It with Charts) name forms the corpus documents use; the rules state the measured forms, not the books' advice. The pilot design set second-coder agreement thresholds for an item: retention at a kappa or intraclass correlation of at least 0.70, revision and retest at 0.60–0.69, and removal or splitting below 0.60.

Package architecture

The package is tiered so that a document loads only the rules its type needs. A core file of 37 rules loads for all writing under the package: register and words, source fidelity, notation, document structure, sentences and paragraphs, the STEM and instructional frames, claims and numbers, captions and citations, covering and commit messages, and a completion check. Reference files load by condition: textbook forms for detail sentences; equations, loaded with the textbook file, for a document with displayed equations; figures, for a document with figures, plots or tables; structure, for reorganizing existing text or outlining a document whose body exceeds about 300 words; business, for a business document; a Simplified Technical English reference, only when that style or a percentage of it is requested; a revision and check reference, with an examples file of rejected and accepted forms, at a check pass. An appendix holds two chief executive officer letter patterns outside the main rules, because they rest on one corpus. The evidence file is part of the package and is not loaded for writing.

House rules

Part of the package is not corpus-derived. A named set of rules comes from the author's standing style guide: the depth, document-structure and notation-roster rules in part, the number-and-unit typography rule, the citation rule, the figures reference, the Simplified Technical English reference, two of the independent-check heads, the field-term clause and the content-order clause, the rule against stating that a requirement has been met, the covering-message and commit-message rules, the house word list, the regression check head, and the tie-break clause of the sentence-openings rule. The size bands in the structure reference (about 300 and about 1,500 words, and 5–15% of the body) are house choices, not corpus measurements. The evidence file names which component each rule belongs to, so a reader can tell a measured rule from a house rule at every point.

Check system

Three instruments check work done under the package. A revision pass, run by the writer in a fixed order, ends with a fidelity diff of the revision against the source and a remove-10% pass. An independent check pass is run by a checker that did not write the draft, under eight heads from traceability to regression after corrections; the checker returns findings and does not edit. Two tools support the passes: a mechanical scan that flags candidate defects in a draft file for the writer to resolve or keep with a reason, and a package checker that verifies the package's own cross-references. Worked specimens — pairs of passages written with and without the rules from one shared fact base, with the differing forms highlighted — are published with the package as demonstrations of the rules in use.

Testing, and the limits recorded with the rules

The package was tested by paired comparison: specimen pairs written at equal word counts, one version with a skill and one without, on identical briefs, coded blind with labels randomised. A confirmation retest compared the tiered package against a single-file form of the same rules, and the tiered package was installed after that test. The evidence file records the limits of this base alongside the rules: the pilots measure text — coded features and coder verdicts — and no pilot measures reader outcomes, comprehension or decision quality; each paired-test condition ran once per case; the combined-skill test used one coder and reports no second-coder reliability statistic, and the business pilot reports no second-coder reliability statistic; and a construction frequent in the coded corpora is not thereby frequent in a field corpus. A copy of the package without its evidence file overstates what the rules rest on. The standing rule for revision is stated in the evidence file: a rule is revised when a further coded corpus or a paired test counts against it.
