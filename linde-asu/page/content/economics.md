## Production scale

Cryogenic air separation covers a wider capacity range than any other industrial gas process. Linde's packaged (standardised) plants reach approximately 850 tonnes per day (t/d) of oxygen or 2000 t/d of nitrogen per train in one current product description [1]; engineered (customised) plants from the same supplier span 1000 to 5500 t/d of oxygen per train [2]. The largest single trains in service are of the order of 5000 t/d: the five units at Jamnagar, India are each designed for 5250 t/d of oxygen [2]. Multi-train complexes exceed any single train by a wide margin; the eight units at Ras Laffan, Qatar produce 30,000 t/d of oxygen in total [2]. At the small end, merchant plants of approximately 250 t/d serve regional liquid markets [3], and below approximately 500 t/d the capital cost and energy penalty of cryogenic separation become uncompetitive against pressure swing adsorption for gaseous oxygen, according to a United States Department of Energy assessment [4].

World and United States production totals for elemental oxygen and nitrogen gas are not published as an authoritative statistical series. Secondary sources quote approximately 100 to 150 million tonnes per year of oxygen worldwide, but the two figures are mutually inconsistent and neither comes from a statistical agency; the page therefore reports no world total [5]. The United States Geological Survey nitrogen series is not a substitute: it measures fixed nitrogen as ammonia, not air separation nitrogen gas [6]. The industry is supplied by a small number of producers, principally Linde, Air Liquide, Air Products and Chemicals, Taiyo Nippon Sanso (Nippon Sanso Holdings), Messer and Yingde Gases; commercial estimates of revenue market share differ between firms (Linde approximately 22 to 31 percent) and are not verified against filings [7].

## Energy consumption

Electricity is the dominant operating cost of an air separation unit (ASU), and the specific power consumption is the controlling economic variable. Reported values for cryogenic production cluster between approximately 200 and 260 kilowatt-hours per tonne of oxygen (kWh/t) for gaseous product at 95 to 99.5 mol percent purity, against a thermodynamic minimum of approximately 51 to 53 kWh/t [8][9]. A National Energy Technology Laboratory (NETL) low-pressure design at 99.5 mol percent purity reports 204.5 kWh/t [10]; an oxy-combustion study reports 200 kWh/t at 95 mol percent purity rising to 240 kWh/t at 99.5 mol percent, which is the cleanest published statement of the purity penalty [9]. Vendor figures for internal-compression oxygen generators of 1000 to 15,000 normal cubic metres per hour (Nm3/h) at 90 to 98 mol percent purity are 0.40 to 0.55 kWh/Nm3 [11], which is consistent with the per-tonne range at those purities. The model of this page gives 250 kWh/t at its default (conservative) settings, within the reported range.

The electricity share of cost is reported between 60 and 70 percent of total production cost in commercial market research [12], between 65 and 75 percent of life-cycle operating expenses in a vendor technical guide [13], and up to 70 to 80 percent of operating cost in a regional market report [14]. The sources differ in scope and authority, but the conclusion is uniform: a change in electricity price moves the unit cost of oxygen almost in proportion, which the App tab demonstrates directly through its electricity price input.

## Capital cost

No peer-reviewed capital cost correlation and no air separation specific cost-scaling exponent were found in the sources verified for this page; the 0.70 exponent used in the App model is a stated modelling assumption anchored to published project points, not a sourced correlation. The verified project points are: a Linde investment of approximately 100 million euros in a unit of approximately 72,000 Nm3/h of gaseous oxygen for the Lipetsk steel site, announced in 2018 [15]; and an investment of approximately 130 million euros for two plants of 55,000 Nm3/h of oxygen each at Yantai, China, announced in 2011 [16]. A commercial market report places large cryogenic facilities in the range of 100 to 500 million United States dollars (USD) depending on capacity and configuration [12]. An itemised operating cost breakdown beyond the electricity share was not found in authoritative sources; a commercial report gives maintenance at 2 to 4 percent of capital cost per year [17], which is the basis of the 0.04 non-electricity operating fraction used, as an assumption, in the model.

## Business models

Large plants are normally built on site at the consumer's fence line under sale-of-gas contracts. Air Products' 2022 annual report describes on-site supply under contracts of typically 10 to 15 years containing fixed monthly charges and/or minimum purchase requirements with price escalation provisions, while merchant supply (liquid delivered by tanker, and packaged gas) carries no minimum purchase requirement and contracts of generally five years or less [18]. The tonnage model transfers the capital and energy risk to the gas producer and prices the gas as a utility; the merchant model monetises liquid production, for which argon is a significant credit. Argon prices retrieved for 2026 are of the order of 675 USD per tonne in China and 725 to 763 USD per tonne in India, with sharp regional spot movements, against an analyst base-case production cost of approximately 100 USD per tonne for oxygen and 20 USD per tonne for nitrogen (an analyst's cost estimate, not a verified market price) [19][20].

## Process trade-offs

Product pressure is obtained either by compressing the gaseous product or by internal compression: pumping the liquid product from the low-pressure column and vaporising it in the main heat exchanger, which removes the oxygen compressor (a machine with demanding safety requirements, Safety section) at the cost of a higher air-side pressure [11]. Purity carries the energy penalty quantified in the energy section: specifying 99.5 mol percent oxygen for a consumer that accepts 95 mol percent raises the specific power by approximately one fifth in the cited study [9]. Liquid production raises the refrigeration load and therefore the specific power per unit of gaseous product, but provides storage, supply backup and access to the merchant market, and allows production to be shifted toward periods of low electricity price; a Linde modular plant in Germany is operated explicitly in that load-shifting mode [21]. Argon recovery requires the additional side column and condenser of PFD-102 and constrains the operation of the low-pressure column, but at large scale the argon credit is a material offset to the oxygen cost; argon supply is rigidly coupled to air separation operating rates, which is the structural cause of its price volatility [19].
