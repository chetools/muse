## Process overview

The Linde air separation unit (ASU) separates air into a gaseous oxygen product (GOX), a gaseous nitrogen product (GAN), a waste nitrogen stream and a crude argon product by cryogenic rectification. Air contains, by volume, approximately 78.09 percent nitrogen (N2), 20.95 percent oxygen (O2) and 0.93 percent argon (Ar), with carbon dioxide (CO2), water vapour and hydrocarbons as trace contaminants that must be removed before cooling. The separation exploits the difference in normal boiling point among the three main components at atmospheric pressure: nitrogen 77.4 kelvin (K), argon 87.3 K and oxygen 90.2 K. No chemical reaction takes place anywhere in the process and no catalyst is used; every separation step is physical, by adsorption in the purification section and by rectification in the cold section. The one consumable separation medium is the molecular sieve adsorbent of the front-end purification unit, which is regenerated thermally and reused.

The process divides into a warm front end (filtration, compression, cooling and purification at ambient temperature) and a cold section inside an insulated enclosure, the cold box, where heat exchange, expansion and rectification take place at temperatures down to approximately 90 K. Drawing PFD-102 (Process flow diagram 102) shows the arrangement: 16 equipment items and 30 numbered streams on a sample basis of 1000 kilomoles per hour (kmol/h) of feed air. The process description follows the published Linde accounts of the double-column process [22][24].

## Air filtration (F-101)

Atmospheric air is drawn through a filter that removes dust and particulates that would foul the compressor and the downstream adsorbent. The filter is a barrier device (fabric or cartridge elements) with a small pressure drop; the filtered air of stream 2 remains at approximately ambient temperature and pressure.

## Main air compression (C-101)

The main air compressor (MAC) raises the air to the pressure column pressure, approximately 560 to 650 kilopascals (kPa) in the sample basis (stream 3 at 625 kPa). Industrial machines are multi-stage centrifugal compressors with interstage cooling, which approaches isothermal compression and is the basis of the power estimate in the Theory section. Compression is the dominant energy input of the plant: the electricity consumed by the compressor train is the largest single operating cost (Economics section). The discharge temperature of an intercooled machine is limited by the intercoolers; the sample basis shows 175 degrees Celsius at the final stage outlet before the aftercooler.

## Aftercooling (E-101)

The aftercooler cools the compressed air to near ambient temperature (22 degrees Celsius in stream 4) against cooling water and condenses a first part of the water vapour, which is separated and drained. Cooling at this point reduces the water load on the purification unit by more than half, because the saturation water content of air falls steeply with temperature at fixed pressure.

## Molecular sieve purification (V-101)

The purification unit removes water vapour, carbon dioxide and hydrocarbons by adsorption on molecular sieve (a synthetic zeolite) in two or more vessels operated in turn: one vessel adsorbs while the other is regenerated with heated waste nitrogen. Removal is a process requirement, not a product specification: water and carbon dioxide freeze at cold-section temperatures and would deposit as solids in the main heat exchanger and the columns, and hydrocarbons (acetylene in particular) concentrate in liquid oxygen and create an explosion hazard (Safety section). The purified air of stream 5 is specified at a carbon dioxide content of the order of one part per million by volume and a water dew point below the coldest plant temperature. Adsorption is a physical, reversible uptake in the zeolite pore structure; no reaction and no catalyst are involved, and regeneration restores the adsorbent by heating and purging.

## Main heat exchanger (E-102)

The main heat exchanger (MHE) is a brazed aluminium plate-fin exchanger in which the purified air is cooled to approximately its dew point (stream 6 at minus 171 degrees Celsius, 568 kPa) against the three cold product streams leaving the cold section (oxygen, nitrogen and waste nitrogen, streams 19 to 21). A single exchanger therefore performs the heat integration of the whole plant: the refrigeration of the products is recovered into the feed, and the products leave at ambient temperature (streams 22 to 24). The temperature approach at the warm end is a few kelvin; a larger approach is a direct loss of refrigeration and of compressor power. The exchanger passages for the condensing and vaporising streams operate close to the phase boundaries, which is why freeze-out of residual contaminants is a design concern (Safety section).

## Expansion turbine (X-101)

Refrigeration is produced by expansion of a part stream of the cooled air (stream 8, 200 kmol/h of the 1000 kmol/h basis) in an expansion turbine (a turboexpander) to the low-pressure column pressure (stream 9 at 138 kPa, minus 182 degrees Celsius). The expansion is approximately isentropic; the temperature fall of the expanded stream, together with the work recovered at the turbine shaft, supplies the refrigeration that balances heat leak into the cold box, the warm-end approach loss of the main heat exchanger and the withdrawal of cold products. The expanded air is not wasted: it enters the low-pressure column as a feed stream. The front-end sequence of compression, direct-contact cooling, adsorption and main heat exchange follows the standard Linde plant description [23]. The remaining air (stream 7, 800 kmol/h) enters the pressure column directly.

## Pressure column (T-101)

The pressure column (also called the high-pressure column) performs a partial rectification at approximately 560 kPa. Rising vapour becomes richer in nitrogen (the more volatile component); descending liquid becomes richer in oxygen. The column delivers two streams: an overhead vapour of approximately pure nitrogen (stream 10, 97.0 mol percent nitrogen in the sample basis, the balance being mostly oxygen at 2.0 mol percent in this simplified accounting) and a bottom liquid, the rich liquid, enriched to approximately 40 mol percent oxygen (stream 16). The pressure column has no reboiler of its own in the usual sense: the entering air vapour provides the boilup, and its condenser is the shared main condenser/reboiler.

## Main condenser/reboiler (E-103)

The main condenser/reboiler is the thermal link that defines the double-column process. It sits between the two columns: pressure-column overhead nitrogen condenses inside it (stream 10 to stream 11), and the heat released vaporises liquid oxygen in the sump of the low-pressure column. The arrangement is possible only because the pressure difference between the columns raises the condensation temperature of nitrogen at the higher pressure above the boiling temperature of oxygen at the lower pressure. The condensed liquid nitrogen (stream 11, 400 kmol/h) is divided: one part returns to the pressure column as reflux (stream 12, 200 kmol/h) and the other part is sent, after subcooling, to the top of the low-pressure column as its reflux (streams 13 to 15). No external heating or cooling medium is involved in the main separation duty.

## Subcoolers (E-104, E-106) and throttling valves (VLV-101, VLV-102)

The subcoolers cool the two liquid streams leaving the pressure-column system (liquid nitrogen, stream 13 outlet temperature minus 186 degrees Celsius; rich liquid, stream 17) against cold waste nitrogen before throttling. Subcooling suppresses flash gas formation when the streams are throttled to the low-pressure column pressure in the throttling valves (streams 15 and 18): liquid that flashes at the valve would pass through the column as vapour without contributing reflux or feed liquid at the intended entry point. Throttling is an isenthalpic expansion across a valve; the temperature fall is the Joule–Thomson effect of the liquid and its flash fraction.

## Low-pressure column (T-102)

The low-pressure column completes the separation at approximately 120 to 140 kPa. It receives four feeds: subcooled liquid nitrogen at the top (stream 15, the column reflux), throttled rich liquid at an intermediate point (stream 18), expanded air at a lower intermediate point (stream 9) and the returning liquid from the crude argon column (stream 30). Three products leave: gaseous nitrogen of 99.5 mol percent purity at the top (stream 21, 500 kmol/h), waste nitrogen drawn a few stages below the top (stream 20, 290 kmol/h, carrying most of the argon not recovered and the bulk of the remaining oxygen loss) and gaseous oxygen of 99.0 mol percent purity from the sump (stream 19, 200 kmol/h), vaporised in the main condenser/reboiler. The argon content of the column vapour rises to a maximum (the argon belly, of the order of 8 to 10 mol percent argon in the vapour at low oxygen content penalty) at an intermediate height; that maximum is where the side draw to the crude argon column is taken (stream 29, 100 kmol/h at 9.0 mol percent argon).

## Crude argon column (T-103) and condenser (E-105)

The crude argon column rectifies the argon-enriched side draw against its own bottom product. Because argon (boiling point 87.3 K) is less volatile than oxygen, the column separates oxygen downward: the bottom liquid, nearly pure oxygen, returns to the low-pressure column (stream 30, 90 kmol/h), and the overhead vapour, enriched to approximately 90 mol percent argon, is condensed in the crude argon condenser (E-105), which is cooled by a part of the rich liquid (shown as a utility stub on PFD-102, because the rich liquid continues to the low-pressure column after giving up its cold). The condensate divides into reflux (stream 27, 20 kmol/h) and the crude argon product (stream 28, 10 kmol/h). Crude argon still contains nitrogen and oxygen; purification to commercial pure argon (removal of oxygen by reaction with hydrogen over a catalyst, then drying and removal of nitrogen in a further column) is a separate downstream process outside the boundary of this unit, and is the only catalytic step associated with argon production. Within the boundary of PFD-102 there is no catalyst and no reaction.

## Products and battery limits

The gaseous products leave through the main heat exchanger at ambient temperature: gaseous nitrogen at 110 kPa (stream 24), gaseous oxygen at 115 kPa (stream 22) and waste nitrogen at 105 kPa (stream 23), of which a part is used, after heating, to regenerate the purification adsorbent before discharge. Plants that deliver products at higher pressure either compress the gaseous product or use internal compression (pumping the liquid product and vaporising it in the main heat exchanger), a variant discussed in the Economics section. Liquid products (liquid oxygen and liquid nitrogen drawn from the columns for storage and merchant sale) are outside the sample basis of PFD-102, which is an all-gaseous plant with a crude argon draw.
