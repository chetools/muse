Input ranges: recoveries are fractions between 0 and 1; purities are between 50 and 100 mol percent; the discharge pressure must exceed the suction pressure of 101 kPa. Definitions of every input are in the Theory section.

Task

Size and cost a cryogenic air separation unit for a stated oxygen duty. Set the feed flow rate, the recoveries and purities of the three products, the compressor discharge pressure and efficiency, and the electricity price; the model returns the product rates, the power, the specific power against the reported commercial range of 200 to 260 kWh per tonne of oxygen, the minimum work and second-law efficiency for the product slate, and the unit cost in 2018 United States dollars with all cost allocated to the oxygen product. Two questions organise the exploration: how far the actual specific power stands above the minimum work of separation, and how the unit cost falls with capacity while the specific power does not.

Read the chart as the balance of the Theory section: each product bar is fixed by a recovery and a purity, and the waste bar is what remains. A negative waste component, flagged above the results, means the specified recoveries and purities are jointly infeasible.

Unit cost against capacity

Read the chart as the capital scaling of the Theory section: the specific power is constant along the curve, and the decline of the unit cost is the dilution of the scaled capital cost. Below a contained oxygen capacity of approximately 500 t/d, pressure swing adsorption is the competing technology (Economics section).
