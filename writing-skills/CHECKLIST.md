# Completion checklist

Rule numbers cite `SKILL.md`. A document is complete when each item that applies to its type passes.

## Before drafting

- [ ] Document type named, and the business section activated or not activated by that choice (Business, Activation).
- [ ] Source material fixed. No fact, consequence, or causal link enters the draft that the source material does not state; a derived figure is marked as derived, with its derivation (Register constraints).

## Sentences and paragraphs

- [ ] Each sentence opens with the noun the sentence is about; participle openings are a minority (rule 1).
- [ ] Explicit connectives are sparse and spent on contrast; addition and sequence are unmarked (rule 2).
- [ ] One object keeps one name and one reference form through a passage; no synonym cycling (rules 3, What not to do 1 and 9).
- [ ] First word of each sentence listed: no repeated opening word across a passage, and no "The" opening where a better opening serves (rules 3, What not to do 7).
- [ ] In a document with several items of one type, peer items do not open with the same word or phrase; the first words of peer items are listed alongside the first words within each item (rule 3, What not to do 10).
- [ ] A sentence that must stand on its own (a validity statement, a closing, a section or paragraph opening) restates the object's name; no bare category noun carries a named object (rule 3, What not to do 12).
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary inside each paragraph; each paragraph closes on shorter sentences than it develops with. Sentence and paragraph structures vary as well, within reason; variety is not bought at the cost of a definition in place, a bundled number, a named owner, or claim strength (rule 4, What not to do 11).
- [ ] Data values sit in parentheses inside the sentence, not in a clause of their own (rule 5).
- [ ] Each descriptive word is the most specific word for the observation; no general or figurative word stands in its place (Register constraints, What not to do 8).

## Claims, numbers, definitions

- [ ] The limit, exception, or condition stands inside the claim sentence, before the number it qualifies (rule 15).
- [ ] Each number is bundled in its sentence with its denominator or cohort, its interval, and its named baseline or contrast; in a worked example, a scored result carries its baseline in the same sentence (rule 16).
- [ ] A null result is stated as a detection statement with its conditions, not as an absence (rule 17).
- [ ] Every term, symbol, and abbreviation is defined in place at first use, symbol by symbol for a composite notation, units included where applicable (rule 18, Register constraints).
- [ ] Each caption fixes what the text leaves open: method, threshold, baseline, error-bar provenance, replication count, conditions (rule 19).
- [ ] A choice or threshold is stated condition-first (rule 20).
- [ ] An analogy or simplification is followed within one sentence by the point where it stops holding (rule 22).

## STEM structure

- [ ] Opening order: setting, established knowledge, gap, aim, method preview (rule 6).
- [ ] Abstract order: setting, aim or approach, method, principal result, interpretation, implication; no limitation in the abstract (rule 7).
- [ ] Interpretation stands next to the result it interprets; results sentences carry a number by default (rule 8).
- [ ] Closing claim is at or below the abstract claim in strength; limitations and scope conditions are named in the closing (rule 10).

## Instructional text

- [ ] Goal stated in the opening sentences; outcome shown or described near the start (rule 11).
- [ ] Each worked example has inputs, result, and what the result shows or how it is checked (rule 12).
- [ ] Checkpoints, expected outputs, and warnings stand at the point of risk; each code block has a purpose sentence before it and an output statement after it (rule 14).

## Business documents, where the business section governs

- [ ] Bottom line stands before the evidence (rule B1).
- [ ] Opening frame performed, in order, and not labelled: situation, complication, question, or a named alternative frame (rule B2).
- [ ] Evidence base named where the finding is stated, with its count (rule B3).
- [ ] Each recommendation is an action with a named owner class; timing and quantified benefit stand outside the recommendation sentence (rule B4).
- [ ] Each trade-off carries both numbers in one sentence; each period result carries its signed components at the same scale (rules B7, B8).
- [ ] Persuasion screen passed: no closing claim stronger than the opening claim without added evidence, no superlative without its comparison set, no certainty opener without an evidence pointer in the same sentence (rule B9).
- [ ] Completion check in rule B2 passed: bottom line present, frame performed, owner class named, evidence base named.

## Equations

- [ ] Symbol placement follows the preference order in rule E1: set-up sentence definitions in symbol order where the set-up is a complete sentence; a where-clause roster after the display where the set-up is a lead-in clause (rule E1).
- [ ] The sentence after a display is the definition sentence; interpretation follows the roster (rule E3).
- [ ] A boundary that is a case on the equation's own variable stands in the sentence immediately after the display (rule E4).
- [ ] The validity of a law or an analysis is stated with the law named as subject and the conditions in the same sentence ("holds for", "applies when", "is valid for"); the case outside is stated as what changes (rule E4).

## Final scan

- [ ] No inferential connectives ("therefore", "thus", a causal "so") in STEM or instructional text; no meta-signposts announcing the text's own moves; no restatement that adds no scope, condition, or limit (What not to do 3, 4, 5).
- [ ] AI-writing patterns scanned as clusters, not single items: inflated importance claims, trailing participle clauses, promotional wording, vague attribution, the overused word set, stock negative parallelisms, filler phrases, mechanical boldface, and placeholder text (AI-writing patterns 1–5).
- [ ] Headings are nominal and impersonal; titles are short; no hype phrases (Register constraints).
