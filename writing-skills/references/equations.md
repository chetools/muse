## Equations [documents with displayed equations; STEM patterns 28–31]

E1. Symbol definitions follow a preference order. Symbols in this rule are the display's mathematical symbols; chemical species notation in a display (an element symbol, a species formula) is not defined in the set-up sentence or in the roster, for a chemical-engineering audience. Where the set-up is a complete sentence naming the quantities, all mathematical symbols of the display are defined in the set-up sentence, in symbol order, with units in parentheses. Where the set-up is a lead-in clause, the central object is defined in prose before the display and the remaining symbols in the sentence immediately after the display, as a where-clause roster in equation order. A table carries the roster only where the text states the deferral. One sentence per symbol, ahead of the display, is not the corpus form and fragments the passage.
E2. The set-up is a lead-in clause that the display completes, or a complete sentence that names the quantities the equation relates. A set-up states an assumption or a derivation from a named prior relation where either is the equation's basis.
E3. The sentence immediately after the display is the definition sentence. An interpretation follows the roster, not the display directly.
E4. A boundary or case (a piecewise threshold, an endpoint case, an interpretation boundary) stands adjacent to the equation: in the set-up, the display, or the following text. Where the boundary is a case on the equation's own variable (a threshold value of that variable), the boundary stands in the sentence immediately after the display. A numbered display is referred back to by its number; an unnumbered definitional display is referred back to by name. The validity of a law or an analysis is stated with the law named as subject and the conditions in the same sentence, in the textbook form: "holds for" a stated case, "applies when" a condition is met, "is valid for" a stated range; the case outside is stated as what changes (a moving medium adds a convection term to the flux). "Scope ends at" is not the form for a law's validity.
