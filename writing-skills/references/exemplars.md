Each exemplar is a complete passage in an accepted form, preceded by the move sequence it follows. The exemplars are constructed; they are not corpus quotations, and their numbers illustrate form only. ## Abstract

Moves: setting, gap, aim and method, principal result (qualitative, then quantitative), interpretation, implication (core rule 17; `significance.md` for the setting and implication sentences).

Packed absorbers remove carbon dioxide from gas streams with aqueous amine solvents, and the solvent rate sets both the removal and the energy demand of the regeneration step. The rate at which the liquid film resistance changes with solvent loading is not known for loadings above 0.4 mol of carbon dioxide per mole of amine. Film resistance was measured in a wetted-wall column at loadings from 0.10 to 0.55 mol/mol and temperatures from 313 K to 353 K. The film resistance rose with loading over the whole range and doubled between loadings of 0.40 mol/mol and 0.55 mol/mol (from 41 s/m to 83 s/m at 333 K). A resistance that rises at high loading is consistent with a falling free-amine concentration in the film, and a constant-resistance design underpredicts the required packing height by 18 % at a loading of 0.55 mol/mol (4.1 m predicted against 5.0 m required). Within the measured range, absorber designs for high-loading operation require the loading-dependent resistance, not a value fitted at low loading.

## Opening paragraph

Moves: setting, established knowledge, gap as missing knowledge, aim, method preview (core rule 16).

A packed absorber transfers carbon dioxide from a gas stream into an aqueous amine solvent, and the height of packing is set by the overall mass-transfer coefficient. Measurements of the coefficient exist for solvent loadings up to 0.4 mol of carbon dioxide per mole of amine, at temperatures from 298 K to 333 K. Industrial absorbers operate at loadings up to 0.55 mol/mol, and the liquid film resistance, the term through which the loading enters the coefficient, has not been measured over that range. The measurements reported here extend the film resistance to loadings of 0.55 mol/mol and temperatures of 353 K in a wetted-wall column, so that the loading dependence enters the design coefficient directly.

## Results paragraph, figure-anchored

Moves: claim, values in figure order, comparison against a named baseline, interpretation next to the result (core rules 18 and 26).

The liquid film resistance rose with solvent loading at every temperature measured (Fig. 1). At 333 K the resistance rose from 24 s/m at a loading of 0.10 mol/mol to 83 s/m at 0.55 mol/mol, and the rise steepened above 0.40 mol/mol, where the resistance gained 42 s/m (from 41 s/m to 83 s/m) over an interval of 0.15 mol/mol that had gained 17 s/m over the 0.30 mol/mol below it. A constant resistance of 34 s/m, fitted over the loadings below 0.40 mol/mol, underpredicts the resistance at 0.55 mol/mol by 59 % (34 s/m against 83 s/m). The steepening is consistent with amine depletion in the film at high loading.

## Equation passage

Moves: set-up clause naming the central object and stating the assumption, display, definition roster in equation order, interpretation after the definitions (`equations.md`).

For a first-order reaction in a plug-flow reactor with a constant volumetric flow rate and a uniform temperature, the outlet concentration is c_out = c_in exp(-k tau), where c_out is the outlet concentration (mol/m3), c_in is the inlet concentration (mol/m3), k is the rate constant (1/s) at the reactor temperature, and tau is the residence time (s). A temperature rise of 10 K raises the rate constant k by the factor that the Arrhenius relation sets for the reaction's activation energy, and the outlet concentration c_out then falls below the value the display gives at the uniform temperature.

## Closing paragraph

Moves: principal claim at its tested scope, limitation named inside the claim's scope, specific next measurement, implication restated at the supported scope (core rule 20).

The liquid film resistance in aqueous monoethanolamine rises with solvent loading up to 0.55 mol/mol and doubles between loadings of 0.40 mol/mol and 0.55 mol/mol at 333 K (Fig. 1). Measurements cover one solvent at one carbon dioxide partial pressure, and the resistance at other partial pressures is not measured; a measurement at 5 kPa and 20 kPa, at the same loadings, settles whether the doubling is a loading effect alone. Within the measured range, absorber design at high loading requires the loading-dependent resistance, and a design that holds the resistance constant at its low-loading value underpredicts the packing height.

## Use

An exemplar is a form to follow, not text to copy: the drafted passage takes the move sequence and the sentence forms, with its own objects, values, and scope. Where a drafted passage departs from its exemplar's move sequence, the departure is checked against the rule behind that move, and the passage is revised or the departure is recorded with its reason in the covering message.
