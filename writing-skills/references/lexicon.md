# Word choice [load with drafting.md, and at a revision or check pass with ai-check.md]

This file governs the choice of a word for a concept where the package's corpora differentiate the choices: connective openers, two closing and inference forms, the reporting and interpretation verbs, and two words whose fault is one of sense. Rates and contexts are in `evidence.md`. A choice this file ranks is a form to use, not a rate to match; field terms and the operation verbs of `textbook.md` T6 are outside its scope. L1 and L6 state single-sighting forms, as the house word list in `ai-check.md` does; for every other sense of a word on that file's check list, the density rule there stands.

L1. A closing is not announced. "In conclusion" does not open a closing sentence or paragraph, and no other announcing opener stands in its place (core rule 15); the closing restates its content under core rule 20. A summary that a document type requires (an executive summary, a chapter summary under `textbook.md` T13) is named by its heading, not by an announcing opener inside the text.

L2. Application of core rule 14, stated with its measured forms. "Therefore" and a causal "so" do not mark an inference in a frame sentence: a recommendation, a comparison result, a limit, and a restated finding stand without them. A frame consequence is stated as the restatement itself, with the ground in the sentence before it. In a detail sentence the derivation markers of `textbook.md` T3 govern, and this rule does not reach them.

L3. Connective choice follows core rule 9, whose exception stands as written there. The measured distribution behind that rule — the baseline's additive openers and "therefore" against the corpus's "however" — is recorded in `evidence.md` and adds no rule here.

L4. Reporting verbs, by the evidence relation each asserts. Detail-sentence uses under `textbook.md` (the operation verbs of T6, including an equation that "shows" and "find from") are outside this rule.
- "show": the sentence presents what material the reader can inspect presents — a figure, a data set, an analysis, a measured group, or the study in "we show". In instructional text, "show" also introduces a worked demonstration ("shows how to").
- "find": the result was reached by the study's own procedure — a test, an analysis, a survey, a sample ("we found", "the survey found"; a null result is "found no effect").
- "observe": the verb reports what the record contains — a measured value, an inspected state, or a phenomenon or pattern registered in the data ("we observe tunable zero-resistance states"). The adjective and term senses ("fully observed", an observed/expected ratio) are outside this rule.
- "demonstrate" is not the general reporting verb, and is not the verb for presenting a figure's content. It states, with a that-clause or a nominal object, a proposition the work establishes — a capability achieved, a finding, or a value shown by examples ("we demonstrate that the agent was able to surpass…", "examples demonstrate the value of…").

L5. Presenting and inferring verbs. "Show", "find", and "observe" present: the content stands in the record. "Suggest" and "indicate" infer: the sentence states an inference or a consequence, at hedged strength. The two are not ranked against each other, and their subjects overlap — a projection, a calculation, an analysis, and a set of results each take either verb in the corpus. An inference stated with a presenting verb, or an inference stated as a demonstration, is a finding under this rule, in the same way as a claim above its scope under core rule 25.
- "suggest": the subject is the data, the evidence, the observations or findings, a projection, a calculation, or a trend, and the claim is an inference stated as one.
- "indicate", in its interpretation use: the subject is the analysis, the results, the data, a projection, a calculation, or a pattern, and the sentence states the consequence the pattern carries. The denotation use in legends, axis statements, and symbol statements ("error bars indicate s.d.") is a separate sense and is outside this rule.

L6. Sense, for two words on the check list in `ai-check.md`.
- "robust", where it grades a result, a method, an estimate, or an effect, takes its ground in the form the corpus uses — the perturbation or the check named with "to", "under", or "against" ("robust to publication bias", "robust under cross-validation", "robust against small variations in twist angle") — in the same sentence or the one before, as `textbook.md` T11 states for grading words. A property of an organism or a material, and any proper name, are outside this rule.
- "landscape" names a distribution mapped over a space (a genomic landscape, a p-value landscape). As a metaphor for a field, a situation, or a state of knowledge ("the evolving landscape of"), it is not used. A proper name is not a use under this rule.

L7. "Together" does not open a sentence to bundle its items. A statement that several quantities are reported, measured, or held jointly names them as its subject in plain form ("Flux, selectivity and stability are seldom reported for the same membrane"), with no bundling opener.

## Completion check

No closing is announced (L1); no frame sentence carries "therefore" or a causal "so" (L2); each reporting verb matches the relation its sentence asserts and no inference is stated with a presenting verb (L4, L5); every grading "robust" names its perturbation or check and every "landscape" names its space (L6); no sentence opens with "Together" to bundle its items (L7).
