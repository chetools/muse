# Drafting procedure [load when drafting a new STEM paper or report from results, before the first draft]

Activation: a new STEM paper or report is to be drafted from a set of results, figures, or a fact base. This file fixes the order in which the parts are drafted. It governs no sentence form; sentence form is the core, and the order of finished parts inside a document is `structure.md`. Where the document is for readers outside its subfield, `significance.md` is loaded with this file.

D1. Fact base first. The inputs, results, figures, and limits are listed before any prose is drafted, each labelled as given, measured, fitted, estimated, or assumed. A result that is not in the fact base is not drafted. The fact base is a working record and does not enter the document (core rule 6).

D2. Advance sentence. The advance is stated in one sentence under G1 of `significance.md`, from the fact base alone. Where the fact base supports no single advance sentence, that finding is reported before drafting; the draft is not given an advance the results do not state.

D3. One claim sentence per figure. Each figure or table is assigned one sentence stating what it shows at its tested scope, with its principal number bundled under core rule 26. A figure with no claim sentence is merged with the figure whose claim it supports, split, or cut, before drafting. The claim sentences, in the order the results need the figures (core rule 19), are the outline of the results.

D4. Results in figure order. The results are drafted in the order of D3. Each results paragraph opens with its claim sentence, states the values that support it, and places the interpretation next to the result it interprets (core rule 18). A paragraph whose claim sentence cannot be written from its figure is returned to D3.

D5. Closing before opening. The closing is drafted next, from the claim sentences and the fact base: the principal claim at its tested scope, its limitations named, and the implication at the scope the evidence supports (core rules 20 and 25, G8 of `significance.md`). A claim that appears first in the closing, and in no claim sentence, is a new claim and is returned to the fact base.

D6. Opening after the closing. The opening is drafted after the closing, in the order of core rule 16: setting, established knowledge, gap, aim, and method preview. The gap is the gap the advance sentence answers, stated under G2 of `significance.md`. Material that serves neither the gap nor the aim is cut at this step, not kept in the draft.

D7. Abstract last among the text parts. The abstract is drafted from the opening, the claim sentences, and the closing, in the order of core rule 17, at the length the target journal sets. A sentence in the abstract that states a result, a method, or an implication found in none of those parts is a finding under core rule 3 and is removed or returned to the fact base.

D8. Title last. The title is written after the abstract, under G4 of `significance.md`, from the advance sentence. A title drafted before the abstract is rechecked against the finished abstract and revised where the two state different strengths.

D9. Methods and supporting parts. The methods, the nomenclature, and the supporting information are drafted or assembled after the parts in D2 to D8 stand, at the depth core rule 5 requires. A method that no claim sentence uses is reported; it is not added to the draft to complete a record.

D10. Handover to revision. The finished draft enters the revision pass in `ai-check.md`. The fact base, the advance sentence, and the claim sentences accompany the draft as the checker's source material for the independent check pass, where one is run.

## Completion check

A fact base exists and every drafted result stands in it with its label; one advance sentence exists; every figure and table has one claim sentence, and the results stand in claim-sentence order; the closing states no claim that is in no claim sentence; the abstract states nothing that is in none of the opening, the claim sentences, and the closing; the title states no greater strength than the abstract; in the finished draft, checked after the abstract stands, the opening follows the order of core rule 16, the abstract follows the order of core rule 17, interpretation stands next to the result it interprets (core rule 18), and the closing claim stands at or below the abstract claim (core rule 20).
